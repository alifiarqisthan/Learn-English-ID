## What this page covers

All 12 English tenses in one place. For each tense you'll find:
the **form**, the **core use**, the key **time markers**, and the **Indonesian equivalent**. Use this as a cheat sheet when choosing between tenses on TOEFL/IELTS.

---

## The Present Tenses

### Present Simple

**Form:** subject + **V1** (he/she/it + **V1-s**)

**Use:** habits, routines, universal facts, permanent states.

**Time markers:** every day, always, usually, never, on Mondays, in general.

**Indonesian equivalent:** plain verb with no *sedang* — *Saya makan nasi setiap hari.*

> I **work** from home. Water **boils** at 100°C. She **goes** to the gym every morning.

---

### Present Continuous

**Form:** subject + **am/is/are** + **Ving**

**Use:** action in progress right now or around now; temporary situations; arranged future; annoying habits with *always*.

**Time markers:** now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, currently, Look!/Listen!

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sedang / lagi / tengah* + verb — *Saya sedang makan.*

> I **am writing** an email. She **is staying** with her aunt this month. They **are always arguing**!

---

### Present Perfect

**Form:** subject + **have/has** + **V3**

**Use:** completed action with present relevance; experience up to now; recent event (*just, already, yet*); duration from past to now with *for/since*.

**Time markers:** just, already, yet, ever, never, recently, for, since, so far, this year.

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sudah / telah / baru saja* — *Saya sudah makan.* / *Dia baru saja pergi.*

> I **have lost** my wallet. She **has just arrived**. We **have lived** here for five years.

---

### Present Perfect Continuous

**Form:** subject + **have/has been** + **Ving**

**Use:** duration of an ongoing action up to now; visible result of prolonged activity; emphasis on process not result.

**Time markers:** for, since, all morning/day/week, lately, recently, How long…?

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sudah … selama … (masih)* — *Saya sudah belajar selama 3 jam dan masih belum selesai.*

> I **have been studying** for three hours. She **has been crying** — her eyes are red.

## The Past Tenses

### Simple Past

**Form:** subject + **V2** (irregular must be memorised; regular = V1 + -ed)

**Use:** completed action at a specific time in the past. No connection to now.

**Time markers:** yesterday, last week/month/year, ago, in 2015, when I was a child, then.

**Indonesian equivalent:** plain verb + past time marker — *Kemarin saya pergi ke pasar.*

> I **ate** breakfast at 7am. She **wrote** a letter. They **didn't go** to the party.

---

### Past Continuous

**Form:** subject + **was/were** + **Ving**

**Use:** action in progress at a past moment; background action interrupted by simple past; two simultaneous past actions.

**Time markers:** at 8pm yesterday, when (+ simple past event), while, as, all day, at that time.

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sedang* + verb + past time context — *Saya sedang tidur ketika dia datang.*

> I **was sleeping** when the alarm went off. While she **was cooking**, he **was reading**.

---

### Past Perfect

**Form:** subject + **had** + **V3** (same for all subjects)

**Use:** the earlier of two past events — "past in the past." Unambiguously marks which event happened first.

**Time markers:** by the time, before, after, already, just, never … before, when (with two past events).

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sudah / telah* + verb + *sebelum/ketika* past event — *Dia sudah pergi sebelum saya tiba.*

> When I arrived, she **had already left**. He **had never eaten** sushi before that trip.

---

### Past Perfect Continuous

**Form:** subject + **had been** + **Ving** (same for all subjects)

**Use:** duration of an ongoing action before a past point; evidence of prolonged past activity; explains why a past state existed.

**Time markers:** for, since, all day/morning, when (later event), by the time, before.

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sudah … selama … ketika/sebelum* — *Saya sudah menunggu selama 2 jam ketika bis tiba.*

> She **had been crying** for an hour before he apologised. They were tired — they **had been travelling** all night.

## The Future Tenses

### Future Simple (will / going to)

**Form (will):** subject + **will** + **V1**
**Form (going to):** subject + **am/is/are going to** + **V1**

**Use — will:** spontaneous decisions, promises, offers, general predictions.
**Use — going to:** pre-decided plans, intentions, predictions with visible evidence.

**Time markers:** tomorrow, next week/month, in two hours, soon, later, this weekend.

**Indonesian equivalent:** *akan / mau / nanti* — *Saya akan membantu kamu.* / *Saya mau pergi besok.*

> "I**'ll** get it!" *(spontaneous)* vs "I**'m going to** study medicine." *(plan)*

---

### Future Continuous

**Form:** subject + **will be** + **Ving**

**Use:** action in progress at a specific future moment; polite inquiry about plans ("Will you be using…?").

**Time markers:** at this time tomorrow, at 9am next Monday, this time next week, while.

**Indonesian equivalent:** *sedang* + verb + future time marker — *Besok jam 9, saya sedang rapat.*

> At midnight, we **will be flying** over the Pacific. **Will** you **be using** the car this evening?

---

### Future Perfect

**Form:** subject + **will have** + **V3** (same for all subjects — never *will has*)

**Use:** action completed before a future deadline.

**Time markers:** by, by the time, before + future point/event.

**Indonesian equivalent:** *akan sudah / sudah selesai sebelum/pada saat* — *Sebelum kamu tiba, saya sudah selesai memasak.*

> **By Friday**, I **will have finished** the report. **By the time** you arrive, she **will have left**.

---

### Future Perfect Continuous

**Form:** subject + **will have been** + **Ving** (same for all subjects)

**Use:** duration of an ongoing action up to a future point; emphasises how long a process will have been going.

**Time markers:** by, by the time, for + duration, How long…?

**Indonesian equivalent:** *akan sudah + selama + future point* — *Pada Desember, saya sudah belajar selama 5 tahun.*

> **By December**, I **will have been studying** English for five years. How long **will** you **have been working** here when you retire?

## Master Comparison Table

| Tense | Form | Core signal |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | V1 (/ V1-s) | habit, fact |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + Ving | right now, temporary |
| Present Perfect | have/has + V3 | result relevant now, experience |
| Present Perfect Continuous | have/has been + Ving | duration ongoing to now |
| Simple Past | V2 | completed past + specific time |
| Past Continuous | was/were + Ving | in progress when interrupted |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | earlier of two past events |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been + Ving | duration before a past point |
| Future Simple (will) | will + V1 | spontaneous, promise |
| Future Simple (going to) | am/is/are going to + V1 | plan, intent, visible prediction |
| Future Continuous | will be + Ving | in progress at a future moment |
| Future Perfect | will have + V3 | done before a future deadline |
| Future Perfect Continuous | will have been + Ving | duration to a future deadline |

## Choosing between tenses — decision tree

**Is the time future, present, or past?**

↓ **Future:**
- Snap decision / promise → **will**
- Pre-decided plan / visible evidence → **going to**
- In progress at a future moment → **will be + Ving**
- Done before a deadline → **will have + V3**
- Duration up to a deadline (emphasis on process) → **will have been + Ving**

↓ **Present:**
- Habit / fact / permanent → **present simple**
- In progress now / temporary → **present continuous**
- Result relevant now / experience → **present perfect**
- Duration from past to now (ongoing) → **present perfect continuous**

↓ **Past:**
- Completed event, specific time → **simple past**
- Background action / interrupted → **past continuous**
- Earlier of two past events → **past perfect**
- Duration before a past point → **past perfect continuous**

## Stative verbs — never use continuous

These verbs describe **states**, not actions. Use simple or perfect tenses even when continuous meaning is intended:

**know, understand, believe, remember, forget, mean, agree**
**love, like, hate, prefer, want, need**
**have** *(= own)*, **own, belong, possess**
**see, hear, smell, taste** *(involuntary)*
**be, seem, appear, exist**

> ❌ I **am knowing** the answer. ✅ I **know** the answer.
> ❌ She **has been wanting** a car. ✅ She **has wanted** a car.

## V2 vs V3 — the most common trap

After *had / have / has / will have*, always use **V3** (past participle), never V2:

| V1 | V2 (simple past) | V3 (past participle) |
|---|---|---|
| go | **went** | **gone** |
| eat | **ate** | **eaten** |
| write | **wrote** | **written** |
| see | **saw** | **seen** |
| take | **took** | **taken** |
| give | **gave** | **given** |
| do | **did** | **done** |
| come | **came** | **come** |
| run | **ran** | **run** |
| speak | **spoke** | **spoken** |

## was/were — quick reference

| Subject | Present | Simple Past | Past Continuous | Past Perfect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | am | **was** | **was** + Ving | had + V3 |
| He / She / It | is | **was** | **was** + Ving | had + V3 |
| You / We / They | are | **were** | **were** + Ving | had + V3 |

## have vs had vs will have

| Tense group | Auxiliary |
|---|---|
| Present Perfect / Present Perfect Continuous | **have** (I/you/we/they) · **has** (he/she/it) |
| Past Perfect / Past Perfect Continuous | **had** (all subjects) |
| Future Perfect / Future Perfect Continuous | **will have** (all subjects — never *will has*) |
