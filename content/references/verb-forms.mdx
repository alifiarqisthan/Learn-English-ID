## Verb Forms Guide (V1, V2, V3)

In English, every verb has up to **three principal forms**, used in different
tenses. Indonesian textbooks call these **V1, V2, V3** — a useful shorthand,
but native English materials use the long names.

### Main Explanation

| Indonesian shorthand | Native / academic name | Use in tenses |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **Base form** (infinitive) | Present simple, after *to*, after *did/didn't* |
| **V2** | **Past simple** (preterite) | Simple past tense |
| **V3** | **Past participle** | Present perfect (*have eaten*), past perfect (*had eaten*), passive (*was eaten*) |

> Native speakers say **"the past tense of *go* is *went*"**, not "the V2 of go." Both are correct, but you'll see the long names on TOEFL/IELTS materials and any Cambridge/Oxford grammar book.

#### When to use each

| Form | Tense / structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Present simple | I **work** every day. |
| V1 | After *to* | I want **to work**. |
| V1 | After *did / didn't* | Did you **work**? She didn't **work**. |
| V1 | After modal verbs | I can **work**. You should **work**. |
| **V2** | **Simple past** | I **worked** yesterday. |
| V3 | Present perfect | I **have worked** here for 5 years. |
| V3 | Past perfect | I **had worked** before they arrived. |
| V3 | Passive voice | The job **was done**. |
| V3 | As an adjective | a **broken** window |

#### Regular verbs — V2 = V3 = V1 + -ed

For regular verbs, V2 and V3 are the **same** (just V1 + -ed):

| V1 | V2 | V3 |
|---|---|---|
| work | worked | worked |
| play | played | played |
| live | lived | lived |
| study | studied | studied |
| stop | stopped | stopped |

### English vs Bahasa Indonesia

In Bahasa Indonesia, **the verb never changes form for tense**:

- *Saya **makan*** — could be I eat / I ate / I have eaten / I will eat depending on context
- The time information is carried by **separate words** (*kemarin*, *sudah*, *besok*)

In English, **the verb itself changes**:

- I **eat** (V1) — present simple
- I **ate** (V2) — simple past
- I **have eaten** (V3) — present perfect

This concept of "the verb has multiple forms" is genuinely **foreign** to
Indonesian grammar. You have to memorize the V2 and V3 forms — there's no
shortcut.

### Common Mistakes by Indonesian Learners

- ❌ I **goed** to school. → ✅ I **went** to school. *(go is irregular: V2 = went)*
- ❌ She has **wrote** a letter. → ✅ She has **written** a letter. *(after "have", use V3)*
- ❌ The window was **break**. → ✅ The window was **broken**. *(passive uses V3)*
- ❌ Did you **went** there? → ✅ Did you **go** there? *(after "did", return to V1)*
- ❌ I want **going** home. → ✅ I want **to go** home. *(after "want", use to + V1)*
- ❌ He has **eat** lunch. → ✅ He has **eaten** lunch. *(present perfect needs V3)*

### Quick Summary

- Every verb has **3 principal forms**: V1 (base), V2 (past simple), V3 (past participle)
- Regular verbs: V2 = V3 = V1 + **-ed**
- Irregular verbs: must memorize all three
- **V1** after: *to, did, didn't, will, can, must*
- **V2**: simple past tense
- **V3** after: *have, has, had, was, were, been, being*
