## TOEFL / IELTS Grammar Question Types

Almost every grammar question on TOEFL and IELTS is one of **four types**.
Once you know what each type looks for, you can answer faster and more
accurately.

### Main Explanation

#### 1. Error Identification

##### What it looks like
You see a sentence with **four underlined parts**. One of them has a grammar error. Pick it.

> She **(A) have lived** in Jakarta **(B) for** five years **(C) since** she **(D) was** young.

##### What it tests
- Subject-verb agreement (he **has**, not he **have**)
- Tense (past vs. present perfect)
- Word forms (adjective vs. adverb)
- Articles (a / an / the)
- Prepositions
- Pronouns (he/him, who/whom)

##### Strategy

1. **Read the whole sentence first** — context tells you which tense is needed.
2. **Check the subject and the verb** — do they agree? (He has, not he have.)
3. **Look at each underlined part** in order. Mentally substitute alternatives.
4. **The error is usually in the most "ordinary"-looking part.** Test-makers know you'll skim past easy words.
5. If two parts seem wrong, the **most clearly broken** one is the answer — only one option is wrong by design.

> In the example above: (A) is wrong. With "she" + "for five years" → present perfect needs **has lived**, not *have lived*.

#### 2. Sentence Completion (Fill in the Blank)

##### What it looks like
A sentence with one or two blanks. Pick the option that fits.

> The committee ___ a final decision before the deadline.
> (A) reach (B) reaches (C) reached (D) will reach

##### What it tests
- Tense consistency
- Subject-verb agreement
- Vocabulary in context
- Word forms (verb / noun / adjective / adverb)
- Connectors (although, because, however)

##### Strategy

1. **Find time markers in the sentence** (before, after, since, yesterday, tomorrow). They tell you the tense.
2. **Check the subject** — singular or plural? That narrows the verb choice.
3. **Read the answer choices for what's different** between them — that's what's being tested. (e.g., if all 4 are forms of "reach," the test is about tense.)
4. **Plug each option in mentally** and read the whole sentence. The right one will *feel* natural.
5. **Eliminate impossible answers first.** If two options are the same tense, only one will fit subject agreement.

> Example answer: (D) **will reach** — because "before the deadline" + the future timing suggests an upcoming action.

#### 3. Sentence Improvement (Choose the Better Sentence)

##### What it looks like
A sentence is given. Part of it is **underlined**. Pick the option that improves the underlined part — or "(A) NO CHANGE" if it's fine.

> Many students struggle **with the way how the system works**.
> (A) NO CHANGE
> (B) with how the system works
> (C) with the system's way of working
> (D) by the way how the system works

##### What it tests
- Clarity and conciseness
- Redundancy
- Awkward phrasing
- Parallel structure
- Misplaced modifiers

##### Strategy

1. **Read the sentence first without looking at the options.** Notice what feels off.
2. **The shorter option is often correct.** TOEFL/IELTS rewards conciseness.
3. **Watch for redundancy.** "the way how" is wrong because *the way* and *how* both mean the same thing.
4. **Check parallel structure.** "I like reading, writing, and **to swim**" → wrong. Should be "swimming."
5. **(A) NO CHANGE is sometimes the answer** — don't avoid it just because.

> Example answer: (B) **with how the system works** — concise, no redundancy.

#### 4. Paraphrasing (Sentence Rewriting)

##### What it looks like
You're given a sentence. Pick the option with **the same meaning**, expressed differently.

> Despite the heavy rain, the match continued.
> (A) Because of the heavy rain, the match was canceled.
> (B) The match continued, although it was raining heavily.
> (C) The match stopped because of the heavy rain.
> (D) The match continued even though there was no rain.

##### What it tests
- Synonyms
- Connector equivalence (despite / although / even though)
- Active vs. passive voice
- Cause-effect logic

##### Strategy

1. **Identify the core meaning** of the original (who did what, with what result).
2. **Watch for connectors** that change the meaning:
   - *Despite / In spite of / Although / Even though* — contrast (X happened, surprise — opposite of X expected)
   - *Because / Since / As* — cause
   - *So / Therefore / Thus* — result
3. **Eliminate options that change the facts** (different actor, opposite outcome, different cause).
4. **Check synonyms carefully.** "Heavy rain" ≈ "raining heavily" ✅. "No rain" ❌ — opposite meaning.

> Example answer: (B) — same contrast meaning ("despite" → "although"), same outcome ("continued").

### Connector cheat sheet

| Concept | Connectors |
|---|---|
| Contrast | but, however, although, though, even though, despite, in spite of, while, whereas |
| Cause | because, since, as, due to, owing to |
| Result | so, therefore, thus, consequently, as a result |
| Addition | and, also, moreover, furthermore, in addition |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as |
| Time | when, while, as, before, after, since, until |

### Time-management strategy

| Question type | Time per question | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| Error Identification | 30-45 seconds | Spending too long on one tricky part |
| Sentence Completion | 30-60 seconds | Picking the first option that "sounds right" |
| Sentence Improvement | 45-75 seconds | Forgetting (A) NO CHANGE is valid |
| Paraphrasing | 60-90 seconds | Reading the original too fast |

If you're stuck for **more than 90 seconds** on one question, mark it and move on. Come back later.

### Common Mistakes by Indonesian Learners

- ❌ Picking the **most "complicated"** option — TOEFL/IELTS rewards simple, correct sentences.
- ❌ Translating from Indonesian first instead of working in English.
- ❌ Ignoring time markers (*yesterday, since 2010, by next year*) that tell you the tense.
- ❌ Forgetting that *despite* / *although* signal **contrast** — they shouldn't introduce the same outcome the cause would.
- ❌ Confusing **subject-verb agreement** when the subject has a long phrase between it and the verb. *(The book on those high shelves **is** mine — not "are".)*
- ❌ Spending too long re-reading. Trust your first instinct unless you spot a clear reason it's wrong.

### Quick Summary

- 4 question types: **Error ID**, **Sentence Completion**, **Sentence Improvement**, **Paraphrasing**
- Always read the **whole sentence** before looking at choices
- **Time markers** dictate tense; **subject** dictates agreement
- For improvement questions: **shorter and clearer wins**
- For paraphrasing: match the **logical structure** (cause / contrast / result), then check synonyms
- If stuck >90 sec, mark and move on
