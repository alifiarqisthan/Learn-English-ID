## The Verb "to be" Master Guide

"to be" is the **most important verb in English** — and the one Indonesian
learners almost always get wrong, because Bahasa Indonesia drops it most of the
time. There is no equivalent for *am / is / are / was / were* in Indonesian.

### Main Explanation

#### All forms across tenses

| Subject | Present (am/is/are) | Past (was/were) | Future (will be) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **am** | **was** | will be |
| You (singular & plural) | **are** | **were** | will be |
| He / She / It | **is** | **was** | will be |
| We | **are** | **were** | will be |
| They | **are** | **were** | will be |

> Memory rule: **"I" + singular he/she/it** use *was*. **You / we / they** use *were*.

#### Contractions

| Long | Short |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| You are | You're |
| He is / She is / It is | He's / She's / It's |
| We are | We're |
| They are | They're |
| is not | isn't |
| are not | aren't |
| was not | wasn't |
| were not | weren't |

#### Positive form

```
subject + am/is/are/was/were + (rest)
```

> I **am** a student.
> She **is** Indonesian.
> They **were** at the cinema.

#### Negative form

```
subject + am/is/are/was/were + not
```

> I **am not** ready. (or *I'm not ready*)
> He **isn't** at home.
> They **weren't** invited.

⚠️ Never use *do/does/did* with "to be":
- ❌ He **doesn't is** here.
- ✅ He **isn't** here.

#### Question form

```
am/is/are/was/were + subject + (rest) ?
```

> **Are** you tired?
> **Is** she Indonesian?
> **Were** they at school yesterday?

⚠️ Same rule — no *do/does/did*:
- ❌ **Do** you **are** ready?
- ✅ **Are** you ready?

### When to use "to be" vs a normal verb

| Situation | Use "to be" | Don't use it |
|---|---|---|
| Identity: "I **am** a teacher" | ✅ | |
| Description: "She **is** tall" | ✅ | |
| Location: "The book **is** on the table" | ✅ | |
| Continuous tense: "I **am** working" | ✅ (as auxiliary) | |
| Passive voice: "The cake **was** eaten" | ✅ (as auxiliary) | |
| Action: "I run / I work / I study" | | ❌ no "to be" needed |

### English vs Bahasa Indonesia

This is where Indonesian learners struggle most. **Indonesian drops "to be"
almost always.** English **never** drops it.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Saya **lapar**. | I **am** hungry. |
| Dia **guru**. | She **is** a teacher. |
| Mereka **di rumah**. | They **are** at home. |
| Tadi malam saya **lelah**. | Last night I **was** tired. |
| **Dingin** sekali. | It **is** very cold. |
| Buku itu **mahal**. | That book **is** expensive. |

There is **no Indonesian word** in any of those sentences that translates to
*am, is, are, was, were*. The copula simply isn't there.

In English, **you must always include it**, because every English sentence
needs a verb — and "to be" is the verb when you're describing a state.

### Common Mistakes by Indonesian Learners

- ❌ I **happy** today. → ✅ I **am** happy today.
- ❌ She **teacher** in Jakarta. → ✅ She **is a** teacher in Jakarta.
- ❌ They **at the meeting** yesterday. → ✅ They **were** at the meeting yesterday.
- ❌ He **didn't was** ready. → ✅ He **wasn't** ready. *(no "did" with "to be")*
- ❌ **Do** you **are** Indonesian? → ✅ **Are** you Indonesian?
- ❌ We **was** late. → ✅ We **were** late. *(plural subject takes "were")*
- ❌ **It's** color is red. → ✅ **Its** color is red. *(possessive, no apostrophe)*
- ❌ I **am not** know him. → ✅ I **do not** know him. *(action verb uses do/does)*

### Quick Summary

- Forms: **am / is / are** (present), **was / were** (past), **will be** (future)
- Indonesian drops "to be" — English **never** does
- **Never** use do/does/did with "to be" — it makes its own negative and question forms
- *am/is/are/was/were* + **not** for negative
- *am/is/are/was/were* + **subject** for questions
- "to be" is also the auxiliary for continuous tenses (*I am working*) and passive voice (*it was made*)
