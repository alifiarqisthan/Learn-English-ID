## Subject, Object & Possessive Pronouns

Indonesian uses **one form** for most roles (*saya bertemu dia* / *dia melihat saya* — same *saya*, same *dia*). English has **four different forms** for the same person, depending on what role the pronoun plays.

### Main Explanation

#### The full comparison table

| Person | Subject | Object | Possessive Adjective | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st singular | **I** | **me** | **my** | **mine** |
| 2nd | **you** | **you** | **your** | **yours** |
| 3rd masculine | **he** | **him** | **his** | **his** |
| 3rd feminine | **she** | **her** | **her** | **hers** |
| 3rd neuter | **it** | **it** | **its** | — |
| 1st plural | **we** | **us** | **our** | **ours** |
| 3rd plural | **they** | **them** | **their** | **theirs** |

#### When to use which

| Form | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | does the action (before the verb) | **I** love English. **They** arrived. |
| **Object** | receives the action (after the verb or preposition) | She saw **me**. Give it to **him**. |
| **Possessive adjective** | shows ownership, **comes before a noun** | **My** book. **Their** house. |
| **Possessive pronoun** | shows ownership, **stands alone** (no noun follows) | This book is **mine**. The car is **theirs**. |

#### Side-by-side examples

| Subject | Object | Possessive Adj. | Possessive Pron. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** love it. | She loves **me**. | **My** car. | The car is **mine**. |
| **He** waits. | Wait for **him**. | **His** dog. | The dog is **his**. |
| **She** wrote it. | I called **her**. | **Her** idea. | The idea is **hers**. |
| **We** went home. | Join **us**. | **Our** team. | The team is **ours**. |
| **They** know. | Tell **them**. | **Their** plan. | The plan is **theirs**. |

#### "Its" vs "It's" — the famous trap

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **its** | possessive (belongs to it) | The dog wagged **its** tail. |
| **it's** | contraction of *it is* / *it has* | **It's** raining. **It's** been a long day. |

There is **no** "it's" possessive form. Ever.

### English vs Bahasa Indonesia

In Bahasa Indonesia, pronouns rarely change form:

| Indonesian | English (4 forms!) |
|---|---|
| **saya** bertemu **dia** | **I** met **him** / **her** |
| **dia** melihat **saya** | **He/she** saw **me** |
| ini buku **saya** | This is **my** book / This book is **mine** |
| ini bukunya | This is **his / her** book |

Possession in Indonesian is shown by **placing the owner after the noun** (*buku saya, rumah dia*) — the noun doesn't change. In English, the *pronoun itself* changes (my, your, his, her...).

### Common Mistakes by Indonesian Learners

- ❌ **Me** like coffee. → ✅ **I** like coffee. *(use subject form before verb)*
- ❌ Give the book to **I**. → ✅ Give the book to **me**. *(object after preposition)*
- ❌ This is **me** book. → ✅ This is **my** book. *(possessive adjective before noun)*
- ❌ The book is **my**. → ✅ The book is **mine**. *(possessive pronoun stands alone)*
- ❌ **Him** is my brother. → ✅ **He** is my brother. *(subject form for the doer)*
- ❌ The dog wagged **it's** tail. → ✅ The dog wagged **its** tail. *(no apostrophe in possessive)*
- ❌ **Their** is happy. → ✅ **They are** happy. *("their" is possessive, not subject)*

### Quick Summary

- **4 forms** per person: subject, object, possessive adjective, possessive pronoun
- Subject = does the action; object = receives it
- **My / your / his / her / its / our / their** come **before** a noun
- **Mine / yours / his / hers / ours / theirs** stand **alone**
- **It's** = "it is"; **its** = possessive (no apostrophe)
