## Welcome to the Past Tenses

English has **four past tenses**, and you will see all of them on TOEFL and
IELTS. Each one captures a different shape of past time:

| Tense | Angle | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple Past** | Completed past action at a specific time | I **walked** to the market yesterday. |
| **Past Continuous** | Action in progress at a past moment (often interrupted) | I **was walking** when it started to rain. |
| **Past Perfect** | An "earlier past" — finished before another past action | She **had left** by the time I arrived. |
| **Past Perfect Continuous** | Duration of an action before a past point | She **had been studying** for 2 hours when I called. |

### Why this matters for Indonesian learners

Bahasa Indonesia has **one verb form** for all of these. The same word *makan*
covers "ate," "was eating," "had eaten," and "had been eating" — Indonesian
relies entirely on context words (*kemarin*, *sedang*, *sudah*) to convey
when things happened.

In English, the verb form **must change** to show:
- Was the action completed? → simple past
- Was it ongoing at a moment? → past continuous
- Did it finish before another past event? → past perfect
- How long was it ongoing before a past point? → past perfect continuous
