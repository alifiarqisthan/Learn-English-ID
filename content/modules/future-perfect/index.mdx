## Explanation

The **future perfect** describes an action that will be **complete before a
specific point in the future**. You are looking forward to a future deadline,
and you are saying the action will be done by then.

> "**By Friday**, I **will have finished** the report." — it will be done before Friday arrives.
> "**By the time** you read this, I **will have left**." — I'll be gone before you read it.
> "In 2030, she **will have been** a doctor for 10 years." — counting from the start to a future date.

The key structure: **by + future time point** + **will have + V3**.

Think of it as a future version of the past perfect — instead of "had done by a past point," you're saying "will have done by a future point."

> Compare with [Future Simple →](/modules/future-simple) for basic future events, and [Past Perfect →](/modules/past-perfect) for the past equivalent.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Completed before a future deadline | **By** next month, she **will have saved** enough money. |
| Before another future event occurs | **By the time** the guests arrive, I **will have cooked** dinner. |
| Duration reaching a future milestone | **By** 2030, we **will have lived** here for 20 years. |
| Prediction about completion | Don't worry — they **will have fixed** it by the time you need it. |

### Form — will have + V3

```
subject + will have + V3
```

All subjects use the same form. The key is the **V3** (past participle) — the same form used in present perfect and past perfect.

#### Positive

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| All subjects | By tonight, I **will have finished** this book. |

> She **will have graduated** by the end of this year. They **will have built** the new bridge by 2027.

Common V3 forms needed here — same as past perfect:

| V1 | V3 |
|---|---|
| finish | finished |
| write | written |
| complete | completed |
| read | read |
| go | gone |
| eat | eaten |
| take | taken |
| make | made |
| do | done |
| leave | left |
| build | built |
| save | saved |
| graduate | graduated |

#### Negative

```
subject + will not have / won't have + V3
```

> I **won't have finished** the report by 5pm — I need more time.
> She **will not have arrived** by the time the meeting starts.

#### Question

```
Will + subject + have + V3 ?
Wh-word + will + subject + have + V3 ?
```

> **Will** you **have completed** the project by Friday?
> **Will** she **have left** by the time we get there?
> **How many chapters will** you **have read** by the end of the week?

### Signal words

These almost always appear with future perfect:

| Signal | Example |
|---|---|
| **by** + time | **By** next Friday, I **will have submitted** the application. |
| **by the time** + clause | **By the time** he arrives, we **will have eaten**. |
| **before** + time/event | **Before** the year ends, she **will have published** her research. |
| **in** + time (counting forward) | **In** ten years, I **will have worked** here for two decades. |

### Future Perfect vs Future Simple

| Aspect | Future Simple | Future Perfect |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | The action will happen | The action will be **complete** by a point |
| Example | I **will finish** the report. | I **will have finished** the report **by** 5pm. |
| Time reference | Not anchored to a deadline | Always anchored to a **deadline** |

> "I **will finish** the report" = I plan to finish it (sometime).
> "I **will have finished** the report by 5pm" = it will be done before 5pm.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### *akan sudah* and *pada saat itu*

Indonesian expresses "complete by a future point" using combinations like *akan sudah*, *sudah selesai*, or *sebelum/pada saat + future event*:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Pada saat kamu tiba, saya **sudah selesai** memasak. | By the time you arrive, I **will have finished** cooking. |
| Tahun depan, dia **akan sudah** lulus kuliah. | Next year, she **will have graduated**. |
| Sebelum akhir bulan, proyek ini **harus sudah selesai**. | Before the end of the month, this project **will have been completed**. |
| Pada 2030, saya **sudah bekerja** di sini selama 10 tahun. | By 2030, I **will have worked** here for 10 years. |

### The *akan sudah* pattern

When Indonesian uses **akan** (will) + **sudah** (already/completed), that combination is a strong signal for **future perfect** in English:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Dia **akan sudah** pergi sebelum kamu datang. | She **will have left** before you arrive. |
| Kami **akan sudah** makan malam ketika film dimulai. | We **will have had** dinner by the time the film starts. |

### Side-by-side: all four future tenses

| Indonesian | English tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Saya **akan** pergi besok. | Future Simple | I **will go** tomorrow. |
| Besok jam 9, saya **sedang** rapat. | Future Continuous | I **will be having** a meeting at 9am. |
| Pada saat kamu tiba, saya **sudah selesai** memasak. | Future Perfect | I **will have finished** cooking by the time you arrive. |
| Pada saat kamu tiba, saya **sudah memasak selama** 2 jam. | Future Perfect Continuous | I **will have been cooking** for 2 hours by the time you arrive. |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using future simple instead of future perfect for deadline-completion**
- ❌ By Friday, I **will finish** the project.
- ✅ By Friday, I **will have finished** the project.

  *Why it happens:* Indonesian often uses simple future (*akan selesai*) where English needs future perfect to show completion before a deadline.

**2. Using present perfect instead of future perfect**
- ❌ By the time you read this, I **have left**.
- ✅ By the time you read this, I **will have left**.

  *Why it happens:* present perfect focuses on "now," future perfect focuses on a future deadline. If the reference point is in the future, you need *will have + V3*.

**3. Forgetting V3 — using V2 instead**
- ❌ She **will have went** home.
- ❌ I **will have wrote** the letter.
- ✅ She **will have gone** home.
- ✅ I **will have written** the letter.

  *Why it happens:* V3 and V2 are different for many irregulars. After *will have*, always use V3.

**4. Using "will has" instead of "will have"**
- ❌ She **will has** finished by then.
- ✅ She **will have** finished by then.

  *Why it happens:* learners apply the he/she/it → *has* rule. But after *will*, the auxiliary is always **have**, never *has*.

**5. Omitting "by" — forgetting the deadline anchor**
- ❌ I will have finished the project tomorrow. *(grammatically possible but unusual without "by")*
- ✅ I **will have finished** the project **by** tomorrow.

  *Why:* future perfect almost always needs a deadline marker. Without *by/by the time/before*, the sentence sounds incomplete.

## Quick Summary

- Form: **will have + V3** (same for all subjects — never *will has*).
- Use for: an action **completed before a future deadline**.
- Key signal: **by, by the time, before** + future point.
- Negative: **won't have + V3**.
- Question: **Will + subject + have + V3?**
- V3 ≠ V2: *went* (V2) vs *gone* (V3), *wrote* (V2) vs *written* (V3), *ate* (V2) vs *eaten* (V3).
- Indonesian *akan sudah / sudah selesai + sebelum/ketika* → **will have + V3**.
- Future perfect = future version of past perfect — instead of "had done before a past point," you say "will have done before a future point."
