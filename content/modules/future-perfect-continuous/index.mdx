## Explanation

The **future perfect continuous** is the most advanced tense in English. It
combines the "will have" of future perfect with the "been + Ving" of continuous
tenses to express **how long an action will have been in progress by a future
point in time**.

> "**By December**, I **will have been studying** English for five years." — counting the duration up to a future point.
> "**By the time** she retires, she **will have been teaching** for 35 years." — accumulated duration reaching a future milestone.
> "**At midnight**, we **will have been driving** for twelve hours." — measuring the stretch of time at a future moment.

The focus is on **duration + continuity reaching a future deadline**. You are
emphasizing how long the process will have been going on, not just that it will
be done.

> Compare with [Future Perfect →](/modules/future-perfect) — which shows *completion* by a deadline. Future perfect continuous shows *duration* up to that deadline.
> Compare with [Present Perfect Continuous →](/modules/present-perfect-continuous) — same logic, but anchored to now rather than a future point.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Duration of ongoing activity by a future point | By June, she **will have been training** for six months. |
| Emphasise effort or process leading to a future milestone | By the time I finish this degree, I **will have been studying** for four years. |
| Duration at a specific future moment | At 3pm, they **will have been waiting** for two hours. |
| Explain the reason for a future state | When you see her, she**'ll be exhausted** — she **will have been working** all night. |

### Form — will have been + Ving

```
subject + will have been + Ving
```

Four parts. Same for all subjects.

#### Positive

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| All subjects | By next year, I **will have been learning** guitar for a decade. |

> She **will have been working** here for 20 years by the time she retires.
> They **will have been building** that bridge for three years by the opening date.

#### Negative

```
subject + won't have been + Ving
→ will not have been + Ving
```

> By next month, I **won't have been living** here for a full year yet.
> She **will not have been sleeping** enough by the time exams are over.

#### Question

```
Will + subject + have been + Ving ?
Wh-word + will + subject + have been + Ving ?
```

> **Will** you **have been waiting** long by the time I arrive?
> **How long will** she **have been studying** when she graduates?
> **Why will** he **have been working** non-stop for three days?

### Stative verbs — same rule

Stative verbs (know, want, love, believe, understand) cannot use continuous. Use future perfect simple instead:

- ❌ By next year, I **will have been knowing** him for a decade.
- ✅ By next year, I **will have known** him for a decade.

### Future Perfect vs Future Perfect Continuous

| Aspect | Future Perfect | Future Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Completion of an action | Duration of an ongoing action |
| Example | I **will have written** the report. | I **will have been writing** for three hours. |
| Meaning | The report will be done. | I'll have been in the process of writing for 3h. |
| Stative verbs | ✅ I will have known her for years. | ❌ I will have been knowing her. |

**Short test:** Can you ask "how long by then?" → future perfect continuous. Is completion the point? → future perfect simple.

| Sentence | Which? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| By 5pm, she **will have finished**. | Future perfect | Focus: it will be done |
| By 5pm, she **will have been working** for 8 hours. | Future perfect continuous | Focus: duration |
| By 2030, I **will have lived** here 10 years. | **Either** | Both accepted with state verbs + for |

### Time markers

| Marker | Example |
|---|---|
| **by** + time | **By** graduation day, she **will have been studying** for 4 years. |
| **by the time** + clause | **By the time** you land, we **will have been waiting** for 3 hours. |
| **for** | I **will have been practicing** this song **for** months by the concert. |
| **how long** | **How long will** you **have been running** this business by then? |
| **at** + future time | **At** midnight, they **will have been travelling** for 24 hours. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### No direct equivalent — a combination of markers

As with past perfect continuous and present perfect continuous, Indonesian
expresses this concept through combinations of time words rather than a
dedicated verb form:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Pada bulan Desember, saya **sudah belajar** bahasa Inggris selama 5 tahun. | By December, I **will have been studying** English for 5 years. |
| Saat dia pensiun, dia **sudah mengajar** selama 35 tahun. | By the time she retires, she **will have been teaching** for 35 years. |
| Pada tengah malam, kami **sudah berkendara** selama 12 jam. | At midnight, we **will have been driving** for 12 hours. |

### The *akan sudah + selama* pattern

The Indonesian combination of **akan/sudah** (will/already) + **selama** (for [duration]) + a future time marker (*pada/saat/ketika*) signals future perfect continuous:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Pada 2030, saya **akan sudah belajar** di sini **selama** 10 tahun. | By 2030, I **will have been studying** here for 10 years. |
| Saat proyek selesai, tim **sudah bekerja** keras **selama** 2 tahun. | By the time the project is done, the team **will have been working** hard for 2 years. |

### Why this tense is hard for Indonesian learners

1. **Four-part structure** (will + have + been + Ving) — Indonesian has no equivalent, so all four parts feel "extra."
2. **"been" feels redundant** — learners who know future perfect (*will have + V3*) often forget to add *been + Ving* for the continuous aspect.
3. **Rarely needed in everyday speech** — this tense appears most often in formal writing, academic English, and IELTS/TOEFL C1+ contexts. Understanding it matters more than actively producing it.

### Side-by-side: all four future tenses

| Indonesian | English tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Saya **akan** pergi besok. | Future Simple | I **will go** tomorrow. |
| Besok jam 9, saya **sedang** rapat. | Future Continuous | I **will be having** a meeting at 9am. |
| Sebelum kamu tiba, saya **sudah selesai** memasak. | Future Perfect | I **will have finished** cooking before you arrive. |
| Saat kamu tiba, saya **sudah memasak selama** 2 jam. | Future Perfect Continuous | I **will have been cooking** for 2 hours when you arrive. |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Forgetting "been"**
- ❌ By July, I **will have studying** English for a year.
- ❌ She **will have working** at this company for a decade by then.
- ✅ By July, I **will have been studying** English for a year.
- ✅ She **will have been working** here for a decade by then.

  *Why it happens:* four-part structure — learners drop *been*, collapsing it to future perfect simple.

**2. Using future perfect where duration is the focus**
- ❌ By graduation, I **will have studied** for four years. *(implies it's done — graduation is over)*
- ✅ By graduation, I **will have been studying** for four years. *(emphasises the ongoing journey)*

  *Not wrong, but future perfect continuous is more natural when the process/effort is the point.*

**3. Using "will has been" instead of "will have been"**
- ❌ She **will has been** working here for 20 years.
- ✅ She **will have been** working here for 20 years.

  *Why it happens:* applying he/she/it → *has* rule. After *will*, always use **have**, never *has*.

**4. Stative verbs in continuous form**
- ❌ By next year, I **will have been knowing** her for five years.
- ✅ By next year, I **will have known** her for five years.

**5. Forgetting the deadline marker**
- ❌ I will have been studying English for five years.
- ✅ **By December**, I **will have been studying** English for five years.

  *Why:* future perfect continuous needs a future deadline anchor (*by, by the time, at [future time]*). Without it, the sentence is grammatically possible but unusual and incomplete in meaning.

**6. Wrong -ing spelling**
- ❌ She will have been **runing**, he will have been **writeing**
- ✅ She will have been **running**, he will have been **writing**

## Quick Summary

- Form: **will have been + Ving** (four parts — all subjects, never *will has been*).
- Use for: **duration of an ongoing action up to a future point**. Almost always with *for*, *by*, or *by the time*.
- Focus is on **process and duration**, not completion. Future perfect = completion.
- Not for stative verbs (know, want, love) → use future perfect simple: *will have known*.
- Indonesian *akan sudah + selama + future time* → **will have been + Ving**.
- Key question: **How long will + subject + have been + Ving + by/when…?**
- This is the most complex tense — recognizing it in TOEFL reading/listening is as important as producing it.
