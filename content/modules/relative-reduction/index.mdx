## Explanation

A **reduced relative clause** is a shortened form of a full relative clause. The relative pronoun and the auxiliary verb are removed, leaving a **participle phrase** (present or past participle) or an **infinitive phrase**.

Reduced relative clauses are common in formal and academic English — they create more compact, professional-sounding sentences.

### When can you reduce?

A relative clause can be reduced when:
1. The relative pronoun is the **subject** of the clause.
2. The clause uses a **continuous** tense, a **passive** form, or an **infinitive**.

You **cannot** reduce when the relative pronoun is the **object** of the clause.

### Type 1 — Active continuous → Present participle (-ing)

When the relative clause uses an active continuous or active form where the subject = the antecedent:

```
Full:    The man who is sitting by the door is the manager.
Reduced: The man sitting by the door is the manager.
```

Remove *who is* → leave the **present participle** (-ing form):

| Full clause | Reduced |
|---|---|
| The woman **who is** wearing a red dress | The woman **wearing** a red dress |
| The students **who were** waiting outside | The students **waiting** outside |
| The company **that is** expanding rapidly | The company **expanding** rapidly |

### Type 2 — Passive → Past participle (-ed / irregular)

When the relative clause is passive:

```
Full:    The letter that was written by the president was short.
Reduced: The letter written by the president was short.
```

Remove *that was* → leave the **past participle**:

| Full clause | Reduced |
|---|---|
| The documents **that were** submitted | The documents **submitted** |
| The building **that was** destroyed in the fire | The building **destroyed** in the fire |
| The results **that were** obtained from the experiment | The results **obtained** from the experiment |
| Items **that are** sold here are handmade. | Items **sold** here are handmade. |

### Type 3 — Infinitive (first, last, only, etc.)

When the antecedent is modified by superlatives or ordinals like *first, last, only, next*, the relative clause can become a **to-infinitive**:

```
Full:    She was the first person who crossed the finish line.
Reduced: She was the first person to cross the finish line.
```

| Full clause | Reduced |
|---|---|
| the first student **who** arrived | the first student **to arrive** |
| the only person **who** survived | the only person **to survive** |
| the next train **that will** leave | the next train **to leave** |
| the last researcher **who** spoke | the last researcher **to speak** |

### Position of the reduced clause

The participle phrase stays **directly after** the noun it modifies, just like the full relative clause.

> The professor **teaching this course** has won several awards.
> The data **collected last year** supports this hypothesis.

When the phrase is long, it may come **after the main verb** (postposed):

> We studied the samples **collected from three different sites**.

### What you CANNOT reduce

| Cannot reduce | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Object relative clauses | The relative pronoun is not the subject | The book **that I read** → cannot → "the book read" is ambiguous |
| Stative verbs in present simple | No continuous form | The man **who knows** the answer → cannot reduce to "the man knowing" *(unnatural)* |
| Non-defining clauses with "which" referring to a whole clause | The reference is clausal, not nominal | She passed, **which surprised us** → cannot reduce |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Participle phrases exist in Indonesian (dengan, yang sedang, yang telah)

Indonesian uses several strategies:
- **yang + verb**: *orang yang duduk* (the person sitting)
- **yang sedang + verb**: *mahasiswa yang sedang belajar* (students studying)
- **yang telah + verb** (passive): *dokumen yang telah dikirim* (documents submitted / that have been sent)

The English reduced clause maps naturally to these, but the English form is more compact.

| Bahasa Indonesia | Full English | Reduced English |
|---|---|---|
| Pria yang duduk di pojok itu adalah dosen. | The man who is sitting in the corner is a lecturer. | The man **sitting** in the corner is a lecturer. |
| Data yang dikumpulkan tahun lalu akurat. | The data that was collected last year is accurate. | The data **collected** last year is accurate. |
| Orang pertama yang tiba mendapat hadiah. | The first person who arrived received a prize. | The first person **to arrive** received a prize. |

### Common Indonesian-to-English reduction errors

Indonesian learners sometimes try to reduce clauses that cannot be reduced in English, or use the wrong participle form.

| Error | Correct |
|---|---|
| The book read by me was great. *(object clause, ambiguous)* | The book **that I read** was great. *(keep full clause)* |
| The man knowing the answer raised his hand. *(stative — unnatural)* | The man **who knows** the answer raised his hand. |
| The letter writing by the president... *(wrong participle)* | The letter **written** by the president... *(past participle for passive)* |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using the wrong participle — active vs. passive**
- ❌ The results **obtaining** from the test were significant. *(active -ing, but results don't obtain themselves)*
- ✅ The results **obtained** from the test were significant. *(past participle — passive)*

**2. Reducing an object relative clause — creating ambiguity**
- ❌ The book read was interesting. *(unclear — who read it?)*
- ✅ The book **that I read** was interesting. *(keep full clause)*

**3. Using a present participle for a passive meaning**
- ❌ The documents **submitting** last week were approved.
- ✅ The documents **submitted** last week were approved.

**4. Leaving out "to" in the infinitive form**
- ❌ She was the first person arrive.
- ✅ She was the first person **to** arrive.

**5. Reducing a non-subject relative clause**
- ❌ The professor teaching → *(fine if the professor is doing the teaching)*
- ❌ The professor teaching by Dr Santos → *(wrong — this is passive; should be "taught")*
- ✅ The course **taught** by Dr Santos... *(passive)*
- ✅ Dr Santos, **teaching** three courses this semester, ... *(active — Dr Santos does the teaching)*

**6. Moving the reduced clause away from its noun**
- ❌ The man is the manager **sitting by the door**.
- ✅ The man **sitting by the door** is the manager.

## Quick Summary

- **Reduce active continuous** → drop *who/which + is/was* → leave **-ing** participle. *The woman **wearing** a red dress.*
- **Reduce passive** → drop *who/which + was/were* → leave **past participle**. *The data **collected** last year.*
- **Infinitive form** after *first, last, only, next*: *the first person **to arrive**.*
- Cannot reduce **object** relative clauses (the relative pronoun is not the subject).
- Cannot reduce **stative verbs** in present simple (awkward/unnatural).
- The participle phrase stays **directly after the noun** it modifies.
- Active → **-ing** | Passive → **-ed / past participle** | Ordinal/superlative → **to-infinitive**.
