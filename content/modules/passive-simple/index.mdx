## Explanation

The **passive voice** shifts focus from the *doer* (agent) to the *receiver* of the action, or to the action itself. The doer is either unknown, unimportant, or deliberately omitted.

**Formula: to be (conjugated) + V3 (past participle)**

### Present Simple Passive

```
Subject + am / is / are + V3
```

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They **make** cars in this factory. | Cars **are made** in this factory. |
| Someone **cleans** the office every day. | The office **is cleaned** every day. |
| The government **announces** new policies regularly. | New policies **are announced** regularly. |

Subject → verb "to be" in present → past participle (V3).

**"To be" conjugation for present simple passive:**

| Subject | To be |
|---|---|
| I | am |
| He / She / It / singular noun | is |
| You / We / They / plural noun | are |

### Past Simple Passive

```
Subject + was / were + V3
```

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The architect **designed** the building in 1990. | The building **was designed** in 1990. |
| They **built** the bridge last year. | The bridge **was built** last year. |
| Workers **damaged** the machinery. | The machinery **was damaged**. |

**"To be" for past simple passive:**

| Subject | To be |
|---|---|
| I / He / She / It / singular noun | was |
| You / We / They / plural noun | were |

### Using "by" to name the agent

Add **by + agent** when the doer is relevant:

> The Eiffel Tower **was designed by** Gustave Eiffel.
> The report **is reviewed by** the manager every week.

Omit "by" when the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant:
> My wallet **was stolen**. *(we don't know who)*
> The law **was passed** in 2015. *(agent = parliament, not relevant)*

### Active → Passive transformation steps

1. Object of active → subject of passive
2. Conjugate "to be" to match the new subject and tense
3. Main verb → V3 (past participle)
4. Original subject → "by + agent" (optional)

> Active: **The committee** reviews **the proposals** every month.
> Passive: **The proposals** are reviewed (by the committee) every month.

### Negative and question forms

**Negative:**
```
Subject + am/is/are/was/were + not + V3
```
> The results **were not published** on time.
> This form **is not accepted** online.

**Question:**
```
Am/Is/Are/Was/Were + subject + V3?
```
> **Was** the contract **signed**?
> **Are** applicants **informed** of the decision?

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian passive — a fundamentally different system

Indonesian has its own passive system using **di-** prefix on verbs:

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Mereka **membuat** mobil di sini. | Mobil **dibuat** di sini. |
| Seseorang **mencuri** dompet saya. | Dompet saya **dicuri**. |

The di- passive is very common in Indonesian, but the structure differs from English in two critical ways:

**1. No "to be" in Indonesian passive:**
- Indonesian: Mobil **dibuat** di sini. *(no auxiliary)*
- English: Cars **are made** here. *(must have "are")*

**2. Word order — Indonesian often keeps subject before verb:**
- Indonesian: *Laporan itu **ditinjau** setiap minggu.* (subject first)
- English: The report **is reviewed** every week. ✅ (same order works)

### The "to be" omission error

Because Indonesian passive has no auxiliary, Indonesian learners frequently drop "to be":
- ❌ The building **designed** by a famous architect.
- ✅ The building **was designed** by a famous architect.

The V3 alone is not a passive — it needs "to be".

### "Oleh" vs. "by"

Indonesian uses *oleh* (by) to introduce the agent, same concept as English "by":
> *Menara Eiffel dirancang **oleh** Gustave Eiffel.*
> The Eiffel Tower **was designed by** Gustave Eiffel.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Missing "to be" — V3 alone is not passive**
- ❌ The report submitted yesterday.
- ✅ The report **was** submitted yesterday.

**2. Wrong form of "to be" for plural subject**
- ❌ The results **was** published last week.
- ✅ The results **were** published last week.

**3. Using V1 (base verb) instead of V3**
- ❌ The building was **design** by an Italian architect.
- ✅ The building was **designed** by an Italian architect.

**4. Using active "by" construction with the wrong word order**
- ❌ Was designed the building by Gustave Eiffel.
- ✅ The building was designed by Gustave Eiffel.

**5. Adding "to be" twice**
- ❌ The letter was **been** written by the CEO.
- ✅ The letter **was** written by the CEO.

**6. Wrong tense of "to be" — present when past needed**
- ❌ The bridge **is built** in 1920.
- ✅ The bridge **was built** in 1920.

**7. Forgetting subject-verb agreement in present passive**
- ❌ The documents **is** reviewed daily.
- ✅ The documents **are** reviewed daily.

## Quick Summary

- Passive formula: **subject + to be + V3**
- Present simple passive: **am/is/are + V3** — *Cars are made here.*
- Past simple passive: **was/were + V3** — *The bridge was built in 1990.*
- Agent: **by + doer** — add only when relevant. Omit when unknown/unimportant.
- Negative: **was/were + not + V3**
- Question: **Was/Were + subject + V3?**
- Common errors: missing "to be", wrong tense of "to be", V1 instead of V3, wrong agreement.
