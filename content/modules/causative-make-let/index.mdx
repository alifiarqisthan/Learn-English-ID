## Explanation

**Make**, **let**, **help**, and **get** are causative verbs. They express that one person causes or enables another person to do something. Each verb uses a different structure.

### Core structure table

| Verb | Meaning | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **make** | force / compel | make + object + **base verb** | The teacher made us rewrite the essay. |
| **let** | allow / permit | let + object + **base verb** | She let me leave early. |
| **help** | assist | help + object + **(to)** base verb | He helped me (to) move the boxes. |
| **get** | arrange / persuade | get + object + **to** + base verb | She got her assistant to book the flights. |

> **Key pattern:** After *make* and *let*, use the **bare infinitive** (base verb, no *to*).
> After *get*, use the **full infinitive** (with *to*).
> After *help*, both forms are correct.

### make — compulsion or cause

*Make* expresses that someone has no choice. The subject forces the action.

- The boss **made** the team **work** overtime. (They had no choice.)
- The spicy food **made** me **drink** a lot of water.
- What **makes** you **think** that?

Do NOT use *to* after *make* in the active voice:
> ❌ *She made him **to apologise**.* → ✅ *She made him **apologise**.*

### let — permission

*Let* expresses that the subject gives permission for the action.

- My parents **let** me **stay** up late on weekends.
- The security guard **didn't let** us **enter** the building.
- Will you **let** me **explain**?

Do NOT use *to* after *let*:
> ❌ *She let me **to go**.* → ✅ *She let me **go**.*

### help — assistance

*Help* can be followed by either the bare or full infinitive. Both are correct. The bare infinitive is more common in informal speech.

- He **helped** me **(to) carry** the luggage.
- Can you **help** me **(to) understand** this rule?

### get — arrangement or persuasion

*Get* is more informal than *make*. It implies arranging something or persuading someone.

- She **got** her neighbour **to** water the plants.
- I finally **got** him **to** agree with the plan.
- How did you **get** the children **to** eat their vegetables?

### Passive of make — takes "to"

When *make* is used in the passive voice, the infinitive must include *to*:

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They **made** us **work** late. | We **were made to work** late. |
| The coach **made** the players **run**. | The players **were made to run**. |

> Active: *make + object + base verb*
> Passive: *be made + **to** + base verb*

### Contrast: make/let vs. have/get + past participle

The *have* and *get* causatives (from the passive-causative module) use a past participle and mean arranging for someone else to do the action for you:

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| have/get + object + **V3** | arrange for sth to be done | She **had** her car **repaired**. |
| make + object + **base V** | force sb to do sth | She **made** him **repair** the car. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Menyuruh", "mengizinkan", dan "membantu"

Bahasa Indonesia menggunakan kata kerja yang berbeda untuk menyatakan paksaan, izin, dan bantuan, tetapi susunan kalimatnya lebih sederhana — tidak ada perbedaan infinitive dengan atau tanpa *to*.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English structure | Contoh |
|---|---|---|
| **menyuruh** (someone to do sth) | make / tell + obj + **base V** | The teacher **made** us **rewrite** it. |
| **mengizinkan** (someone to do sth) | let + obj + **base V** | She **let** me **go**. |
| **membantu** (someone to do sth) | help + obj + **(to)** base V | He **helped** me **(to) move**. |
| **menyuruh/meminta** (arrange) | get + obj + **to** base V | She **got** him **to** fix it. |

### Kesalahan khas pelajar Indonesia

Pelajar Indonesia tidak terbiasa dengan perbedaan "bare infinitive" dan "full infinitive" karena Bahasa Indonesia tidak mengenal perbedaan ini.

| ❌ Salah | ✅ Benar |
|---|---|
| She let me **to go**. | She let me **go**. |
| He made her **to apologise**. | He made her **apologise**. |
| The teacher **let** us **to use** a dictionary. | The teacher **let** us **use** a dictionary. |
| I **got** him **call** me. | I **got** him **to call** me. |

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *She let me **to go** early.* → ✅ *She let me **go** early.*
   *Why:* After *let*, use the bare infinitive — no *to*.

2. ❌ *The teacher made us **to rewrite** the essay.* → ✅ *The teacher made us **rewrite** the essay.*
   *Why:* After *make* in the active voice, use the bare infinitive — no *to*.

3. ❌ *We **were made rewrite** it.* → ✅ *We **were made to rewrite** it.*
   *Why:* In the passive of *make*, the infinitive must include *to*.

4. ❌ *I **got** him **call** me back.* → ✅ *I **got** him **to call** me back.*
   *Why:* After *get*, always use the full *to*-infinitive.

5. ❌ *She **was let to leave** early.* → ✅ *She **was allowed to leave** early.*
   *Why:* *Let* has no standard passive. Use *allowed to* instead.

6. ❌ *He **helped** me **moved** the furniture.* → ✅ *He **helped** me **(to) move** the furniture.*
   *Why:* After *help*, use the base verb or *to*-infinitive — not a past tense form.

7. ❌ *The noise **made** me **to feel** anxious.* → ✅ *The noise **made** me **feel** anxious.*
   *Why:* *Make* as a causative always takes the bare infinitive in the active voice.

8. ❌ *She **made** me **doing** extra homework.* → ✅ *She **made** me **do** extra homework.*
   *Why:* After causative *make*, use the base verb, not the gerund (*-ing*).

## TOEFL Practice

Causative verbs are tested in TOEFL grammar sections through:
- Bare vs. full infinitive choices after *make*, *let*, *get*
- Active vs. passive structures (*made us do* vs. *were made to do*)
- Confusion between *let* (permission) and *make* (compulsion)

> **Strategy:** Identify the causative verb, then apply the rule. *Make/let* → bare infinitive. *Get* → *to*-infinitive. Passive *make* → *be made to* + base verb.

2. Gurunya mengizinkan mereka menggunakan kamus. → *The teacher **let** them **use** a dictionary.*

## Quick Summary

- **make + object + base verb** = force/compel someone to do something (no *to*).
- **let + object + base verb** = allow/permit someone to do something (no *to*).
- **help + object + (to) base verb** = assist; both bare and full infinitive are correct.
- **get + object + to + base verb** = arrange or persuade; always uses *to*.
- Passive of *make*: **be made to + base verb** — the *to* reappears in the passive.
- *Let* has no passive — use *be allowed to* instead.
- *Make* = compulsion; *let* = permission — these are opposite concepts.
- Indonesian uses *menyuruh/mengizinkan/membantu* but no bare vs. full infinitive distinction — avoid adding *to* after *make* and *let*.
