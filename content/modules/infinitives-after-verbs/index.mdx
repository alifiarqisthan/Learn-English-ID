## Explanation

An **infinitive** is **to + base verb** (to go, to study, to understand). It expresses purpose, intention, or a future-oriented action. Many verbs in English must be followed by a to-infinitive — never a gerund.

### Core formula

| Pattern | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb + to-infinitive | V + to V1 | She **wants to study** medicine. |
| Verb + object + to-infinitive | V + O + to V1 | He **told her to wait**. |
| Bare infinitive (no "to") | V + V1 | She **made him apologise**. |

---

### Verbs that ONLY take a to-infinitive

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| want | I **want to learn** English. |
| need | She **needs to rest**. |
| decide | They **decided to move** to Jakarta. |
| plan | We **plan to visit** Bali next month. |
| hope | He **hopes to get** a scholarship. |
| agree | She **agreed to help** us. |
| refuse | He **refused to sign** the contract. |
| manage | She **managed to finish** on time. |
| offer | They **offered to pay** for dinner. |
| promise | He **promised to call** her back. |
| expect | I **expect to pass** the exam. |
| afford | We can't **afford to buy** a new car. |
| fail | He **failed to submit** the assignment. |
| intend | They **intend to expand** the business. |
| tend | She **tends to arrive** late. |
| pretend | He **pretended to sleep**. |
| seem | She **seems to know** the answer. |
| appear | He **appears to be** nervous. |

> **Pattern note:** Many of these verbs express intention, expectation, or a future action — they look forward in time. This contrasts with gerund-only verbs, which often refer to actions already in progress or imagined.

---

### Verb + object + to-infinitive

These verbs take a person (object) between them and the infinitive.

| Verb | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| want | want + O + to V1 | I **want you to listen** carefully. |
| ask | ask + O + to V1 | She **asked him to repeat** the question. |
| tell | tell + O + to V1 | He **told us to wait** outside. |
| advise | advise + O + to V1 | The teacher **advised them to revise** daily. |
| allow | allow + O + to V1 | They **allowed her to present** first. |
| encourage | encourage + O + to V1 | He **encouraged me to apply**. |
| force | force + O + to V1 | The storm **forced us to cancel** the trip. |
| help | help + O + to V1 | She **helped him to understand** the concept. |

> **Note on "help":** "Help" can take either a to-infinitive or a bare infinitive: *She helped him **understand*** or *She helped him **to understand*** — both are correct.

---

### Bare infinitive (verb without "to")

After **modals** (can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must) and after **make** and **let**, use a bare infinitive (no "to").

| Context | Example |
|---|---|
| After modal | You **should study** more. |
| After "make" | She **made him apologise**. |
| After "let" | They **let us leave** early. |
| After "help" (optional) | He **helped me finish** the task. |

> Do not confuse this with the to-infinitive: ❌ *She made him **to apologise*.* → ✅ *She made him **apologise*.*

## Bahasa Indonesia

Indonesian has no to-infinitive structure. Verbs appear in their base or prefixed form after another verb, often with *untuk* (for/in order to) when purpose is explicit.

| English | Indonesian equivalent | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I **want to study**. | Saya **ingin belajar**. | No "to" — just base verb |
| She **decided to move**. | Dia **memutuskan untuk pindah**. | *untuk* = "to/in order to" |
| He **refused to sign**. | Dia **menolak menandatangani**. | Base verb, no infinitive |
| They **told us to wait**. | Mereka **meminta kami menunggu**. | No object + to structure |

**Key interference errors:**

| Error | Correct |
|---|---|
| ❌ I **want to going** abroad. | ✅ I **want to go** abroad. |
| ❌ She **decided to went** home early. | ✅ She **decided to go** home early. |
| ❌ He **made her to apologise**. | ✅ He **made her apologise**. |
| ❌ They **asked him studying** harder. | ✅ They **asked him to study** harder. |

The most common error: learners conjugate the verb after "to" (went, going) because Indonesian does not have a strict base-verb rule after these patterns.

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *I want **to going** to the library.* → ✅ *I want **to go** to the library.* — *Why:* After "to" in a to-infinitive, always use the base verb (V1).

2. ❌ *She decided **to went** home.* → ✅ *She decided **to go** home.* — *Why:* The infinitive is always to + base verb, never past tense.

3. ❌ *He made her **to leave**.* → ✅ *He made her **leave**.* — *Why:* "Make" takes a bare infinitive — no "to".

4. ❌ *They asked us **waiting** outside.* → ✅ *They asked us **to wait** outside.* — *Why:* "Ask + object" requires a to-infinitive: asked us to wait.

5. ❌ *She **hopes passing** the exam.* → ✅ *She **hopes to pass** the exam.* — *Why:* "Hope" is an infinitive-only verb.

6. ❌ *He let her **to borrow** the book.* → ✅ *He let her **borrow** the book.* — *Why:* "Let" takes a bare infinitive.

7. ❌ *We can't afford **buying** a new laptop.* → ✅ *We can't afford **to buy** a new laptop.* — *Why:* "Afford" is an infinitive-only verb.

8. ❌ *She seems **knowing** the answer.* → ✅ *She seems **to know** the answer.* — *Why:* "Seem" takes a to-infinitive: seems to know.

---

## TOEFL Practice

TOEFL tests verb patterns heavily. Watch for:
- A gerund incorrectly used after an infinitive-only verb (hopes passing → hopes to pass)
- "Make" or "let" incorrectly followed by "to" (made her to leave → made her leave)
- Object + gerund instead of object + to-infinitive (told him going → told him to go)

## Quick Summary

- The to-infinitive is **to + base verb** — never to + V-ing or to + past tense.
- Infinitive-only verbs include: want, need, decide, plan, hope, agree, refuse, manage, offer, promise, expect, afford, fail, intend, tend, pretend, seem, appear.
- **Verb + object + to-infinitive:** want/ask/tell/advise/allow/encourage/force/help + person + to V1.
- **Bare infinitive** (no "to") after modals, make, and let.
- Indonesian learners frequently use the wrong verb form after "to" — always use the base verb.
- "Help" is flexible: *help him (to) understand* — both forms are correct.
- "Make" and "let" never use "to": *made him apologise*, *let her leave*.
