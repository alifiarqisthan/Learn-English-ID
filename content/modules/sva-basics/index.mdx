## Explanation

**Subject-verb agreement** means the verb must match its subject in **number** (singular or plural). If the subject is singular, the verb is singular. If the subject is plural, the verb is plural.

This module covers the foundational rules. Tricky cases (collective nouns, intervening phrases, indefinite pronouns) are in the next module.

### The basic rule

| Subject | Verb form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | singular verb (adds -s/-es in present) | The dog **runs** every morning. |
| Plural | plural verb (no -s) | The dogs **run** every morning. |

> The student **works** hard. *(singular)*
> The students **work** hard. *(plural)*
> She **is** a doctor. *(singular)*
> They **are** doctors. *(plural)*

### Subject-verb agreement with "to be"

The verb "to be" has distinct forms for each person:

| Subject | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| I | **am** | was |
| You / We / They | **are** | were |
| He / She / It | **is** | was |

> I **am** a student. / I **was** late.
> She **is** a nurse. / She **was** tired.
> They **are** ready. / They **were** absent.

### Compound subjects with "and"

Two subjects joined by **"and"** take a **plural verb**:

> **The manager and the assistant** **are** both available.
> **Rice and fish** **form** the basis of many Indonesian meals.

**Exception**: when "and" joins two things that are thought of as a single unit:

> **Bread and butter** **is** a classic combination. *(one food concept)*
> **The president and CEO** **has** approved the plan. *(same person)*

### Indefinite pronouns — singular and plural

This is a major source of errors. Many indefinite pronouns look plural but take **singular** verbs:

#### Always singular

> **Everyone** **is** ready.
> **Someone** **has** called.
> **Nobody** **knows** the answer.
> **Each** student **needs** to sign.
> **Either** option **works**.
> **Neither** answer **is** correct.
> **Anyone** **is** welcome.
> **No one** **has** arrived yet.
> **Somebody** **was** asking for you.
> **Everything** **seems** fine.
> **Nothing** **was** found.

These are: **everyone, everyone, someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, no one, nobody, everyone, everybody, each, either, neither, everything, something, anything, nothing**.

#### Always plural

> **Both** **were** selected.
> **Many** **have** applied.
> **Several** **are** waiting.
> **Few** **have** finished.
> **Others** **disagree**.

#### Context-dependent (singular or plural)

> **Some** of the water **has** evaporated. *(uncountable → singular)*
> **Some** of the students **have** arrived. *(plural → plural)*
> **All** the evidence **points** to one conclusion. *(uncountable → singular)*
> **All** the students **have** registered. *(plural → plural)*
> **None** of the money **was** found. *(uncountable → singular)*
> **None** of the students **were** ready. *(plural → plural, informal)*

### Subjects joined by "or" / "nor"

When subjects are joined by **or** or **nor**, the verb agrees with the **subject closest to it** (the proximity rule):

> **Either the teacher or the students** **are** responsible. *(students — plural)*
> **Either the students or the teacher** **is** responsible. *(teacher — singular)*
> **Neither the manager nor the employees** **were** informed. *(employees — plural)*
> **Neither the employees nor the manager** **was** informed. *(manager — singular)*

### There is / There are

The verb agrees with the **real subject** that follows "there":

> **There is** a problem with the system. *(a problem = singular)*
> **There are** several reasons for this. *(several reasons = plural)*
> **There is** some milk in the fridge. *(milk = uncountable → singular)*

Common spoken error: "There's many reasons..." — in formal writing, use **"There are** many reasons."

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian has no verb conjugation for number

In Bahasa Indonesia, the verb **never changes** based on whether the subject is singular or plural:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English (wrong) | English (correct) |
|---|---|---|
| Anjing itu berlari. | The dog run. ❌ | The dog **runs**. |
| Anjing-anjing itu berlari. | The dogs runs. ❌ | The dogs **run**. |
| Dia adalah dokter. | She are a doctor. ❌ | She **is** a doctor. |
| Mereka adalah dokter. | They is doctors. ❌ | They **are** doctors. |

Every English number agreement error that Indonesian speakers make comes from this root cause: the Indonesian verb doesn't tell you about the subject's number, so learners forget to apply the rule.

### "Semua orang" and "setiap orang" — singular in English

Indonesians correctly feel that *semua orang* (everyone) refers to many people — and so write "everyone **are**." In English, these words are grammatically singular despite the plural meaning:

| Bahasa Indonesia | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Semua orang sudah siap. | Everyone **are** ready. ❌ | Everyone **is** ready. ✅ |
| Tidak ada yang tahu. | Nobody **know** the answer. ❌ | Nobody **knows** the answer. ✅ |
| Setiap orang harus hadir. | Every person **have** to attend. ❌ | Every person **has** to attend. ✅ |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Plural verb with singular subject**
- ❌ The report **are** ready.
- ✅ The report **is** ready.

**2. Singular verb with plural subject**
- ❌ The students **needs** to submit by Friday.
- ✅ The students **need** to submit by Friday.

**3. "Everyone/everybody/nobody" with plural verb**
- ❌ Everyone **are** invited.
- ✅ Everyone **is** invited.

**4. "There's" with a plural subject in formal writing**
- ❌ There's many reasons for this. *(informal/spoken)*
- ✅ There **are** many reasons for this.

**5. Wrong "to be" form**
- ❌ She **are** very talented.
- ✅ She **is** very talented.

**6. Compound subject with "and" — forgetting plural verb**
- ❌ The CEO and the board **has** approved the plan.
- ✅ The CEO and the board **have** approved the plan.

**7. "Or/nor" — not applying proximity rule**
- ❌ Either the students or the teacher **are** responsible.
- ✅ Either the students or the teacher **is** responsible. *(closest = teacher = singular)*

## Quick Summary

- Singular subject → singular verb. Plural subject → plural verb.
- Compound subject with **"and"** → plural verb.
- **"Or/nor"** → verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.
- Always singular: **everyone, somebody, nobody, no one, each, either, neither, anything, nothing, everything**.
- Always plural: **both, many, several, few, others**.
- "Some / all / none / most" → depends on the noun (countable plural → plural; uncountable → singular).
- **"There is"** + singular/uncountable | **"There are"** + plural.
- "Everyone **is**" — not "are" — even though it refers to many people.
