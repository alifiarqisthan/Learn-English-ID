## Explanation

The **future continuous** describes an action that will be **in progress at a
specific point in the future**. The action will have started before that
moment and will still be going at that moment.

> "At this time tomorrow, I **will be sitting** on the beach." — in progress at that future moment.
> "Don't call at 9pm — I **will be watching** the match." — ongoing at that specific time.
> "**Will** you **be using** the car this evening?" — polite inquiry about plans.

Think of it as a **window into a future moment** — the action is already going
inside the window.

> Compare with [Future Simple →](/modules/future-simple) — which is a completed event or decision. Future continuous is the **stretch**, future simple is the **point**.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Action in progress at a specific future moment | At 8am tomorrow, she **will be giving** her presentation. |
| Planned or expected ongoing activity in the future | Don't worry — the team **will be working** on it all week. |
| Polite inquiry about someone's plans (softer than "will you") | **Will** you **be attending** the meeting tomorrow? |
| Future action that will happen as a matter of course | I**'ll be seeing** my doctor next week anyway — I'll ask him then. |
| Two simultaneous future actions | While you **are sleeping**, I **will be driving** to the airport. |

### Form — will be + Ving

```
subject + will be + Ving
```

All subjects use the same form — no am/is/are variation like in present or past continuous.

#### Positive

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| I / You / He / She / It / We / They | I **will be working** late tonight. |

> She **will be flying** over the Pacific at midnight. They **will be waiting** for you at the gate.

#### Negative

```
subject + will not be / won't be + Ving
```

> I **won't be attending** the conference this year.
> She **will not be joining** us for dinner.
> They **won't be using** that office anymore.

#### Question

```
Will + subject + be + Ving ?
Wh-word + will + subject + be + Ving ?
```

> **Will** you **be coming** to the party on Saturday?
> **Where will** she **be staying** during the conference?
> **What will** they **be doing** at this time next year?

### -ing spelling rules

Same rules as always — see [Present Continuous →](/modules/present-continuous) for the full table:
- Drop silent -e: live → **living**, write → **writing**
- CVC double: run → **running**, sit → **sitting**
- -ie → -y: lie → **lying**

### Polite inquiry — a key TOEFL / real-world use

One distinctive use of future continuous is **polite or indirect questioning** about plans. It sounds less demanding than *will you*:

| Direct (will) | Polite (will be + Ving) |
|---|---|
| **Will** you **come** to the meeting? | **Will** you **be coming** to the meeting? |
| **Will** you **use** the conference room? | **Will** you **be using** the conference room? |
| **Will** he **attend**? | **Will** he **be attending**? |

The continuous form implies "is this part of your existing plans?" rather than "I'm asking you to do this." This is important in professional and academic English contexts tested in TOEFL/IELTS.

### Future Continuous vs Future Simple

| Aspect | Future Simple | Future Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | A point event or decision | An ongoing activity at a future moment |
| Example | I **will call** you at 9pm. | At 9pm, I **will be calling** clients. |
| Meaning | I'll make one call to you. | I'll be in the middle of calling people at 9pm. |
| Spontaneous decision? | ✅ will can be spontaneous | ❌ will be Ving is never spontaneous |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Sedang" in a future frame

Future continuous mirrors what Indonesian does with *sedang* + a future time marker:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Besok jam 8, saya **sedang** presentasi. | Tomorrow at 8am, I **will be giving** my presentation. |
| Saat kamu tiba, saya masih **sedang** bekerja. | When you arrive, I **will still be working**. |
| Nanti malam dia **sedang** menonton pertandingan. | Tonight he **will be watching** the match. |

Indonesian can often drop *sedang* entirely (just saying "besok saya presentasi"), relying on context. English must have the full **will be + Ving** structure.

### Why Indonesian learners simplify

1. **Collapsing to future simple** — "I will work late tonight" instead of "I will be working late." This isn't always wrong, but it loses the "in progress at that moment" meaning.
2. **Using present continuous** — "I am working late tonight" (which is actually OK for arranged plans, but future continuous is more precise for "in progress at a moment").

### Side-by-side

| Indonesian | English tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Saya **akan** pergi besok. | Future Simple | I **will go** tomorrow. |
| Besok jam 9, saya **sedang** rapat. | Future Continuous | I **will be having** a meeting at 9am tomorrow. |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Forgetting "be"**
- ❌ I **will working** all evening.
- ❌ She **will sitting** in the exam at 10am.
- ✅ I **will be working** all evening.
- ✅ She **will be sitting** in the exam at 10am.

  *Why it happens:* learners think "will + Ving" is enough, forgetting the essential *be* in the middle.

**2. Using future simple instead of future continuous for "in progress" context**
- ❌ At midnight, I **will sleep**. *(sounds like the action starts at midnight)*
- ✅ At midnight, I **will be sleeping**. *(in progress at midnight — already asleep)*

  *Why it matters:* "I will sleep" = I'll go to sleep (starting point). "I will be sleeping" = I'll be in the middle of sleeping.

**3. Adding am/is/are instead of just "will"**
- ❌ She **is will be** working tomorrow.
- ✅ She **will be** working tomorrow.

  *Why it happens:* mixing present continuous structure (is + Ving) with future. Drop the *is/are* — *will* covers the future on its own.

**4. Using future continuous for spontaneous decisions**
- ❌ "The phone's ringing." — "I **will be getting** it."
- ✅ "The phone's ringing." — "I**'ll** get it."

  *Why it happens:* future continuous is never for snap decisions. Use *will* (simple) for those.

**5. Wrong -ing spelling**
- ❌ She will be **runing**, he will be **writeing**
- ✅ She will be **running**, he will be **writing**

## Quick Summary

- Form: **will be + Ving** (three parts — never drop *be*).
- Use for: action **in progress at a specific future moment**.
- All subjects use **will be** — no am/is/are variation.
- Negative: **won't be + Ving**.
- Question: **Will + subject + be + Ving?**
- Polite inquiry use: "**Will you be joining** us?" (softer than "will you join?").
- Indonesian *sedang* + future time → **will be + Ving**.
- Never for spontaneous decisions — use *will* (simple) for those.
- Stative verbs (know, want, love) → use **will** (simple future), not future continuous.
