## Explanation

A **gerund** is a verb ending in **-ing** that functions as a noun. It can act as a subject, object, or complement in a sentence.

### Core formula

| Position | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| After certain verbs | V + gerund | She **enjoys swimming**. |
| After prepositions | Prep + gerund | He is good **at solving** problems. |
| As subject | Gerund + predicate | **Reading** improves vocabulary. |

---

### Verbs that ONLY take a gerund

These verbs must be followed by a gerund — never a to-infinitive.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| enjoy | I enjoy **listening** to music. |
| avoid | She avoids **driving** at night. |
| mind | Do you mind **waiting** a moment? |
| consider | We considered **moving** abroad. |
| suggest | He suggested **taking** the train. |
| finish | They finished **writing** the report. |
| keep | Keep **practising** your pronunciation. |
| practise | She practises **speaking** English daily. |
| admit | He admitted **making** a mistake. |
| deny | She denied **stealing** the documents. |
| imagine | Imagine **living** in Paris. |
| miss | I miss **seeing** my friends. |
| postpone | They postponed **signing** the contract. |
| recommend | The doctor recommended **resting** for a week. |
| risk | Don't risk **losing** your passport. |
| involve | The job involves **travelling** frequently. |
| include | The tour includes **visiting** three museums. |
| resist | She couldn't resist **eating** the cake. |

> **Memory tip:** These are often verbs about feelings, habits, or mental attitudes toward an action already real or known.

---

### Gerunds after prepositions

Any verb that follows a preposition **must** be a gerund — no exceptions.

| Preposition phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| good at | She is good **at explaining** ideas. |
| interested in | He is interested **in learning** Spanish. |
| instead of | Go for a walk instead **of watching** TV. |
| before / after | **Before leaving**, check your bag. |
| without | He left **without saying** goodbye. |
| by | She improved **by practising** every day. |
| look forward to | I look forward **to meeting** you. |

> **Warning — "look forward to":** "to" here is a preposition, not part of an infinitive. So the verb after it must be a gerund: *look forward to **meeting*** (not *look forward to meet*).

---

### Gerund as subject

When a gerund heads a noun phrase that acts as the subject of a sentence, it is always singular.

| Gerund subject | Predicate |
|---|---|
| **Swimming** | is great exercise. |
| **Learning** grammar | takes time and patience. |
| **Making** mistakes | is part of language learning. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

In Indonesian, verbs appear in their base form whether they act as nouns, subjects, or objects. There is no gerund form and no "-ing" ending for nouns.

| English | Indonesian equivalent | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I enjoy **swimming**. | Saya suka **berenang**. | Base verb, no -ing |
| **Reading** is fun. | **Membaca** itu menyenangkan. | "Me-" prefix, not -ing |
| She avoids **driving**. | Dia menghindari **mengemudi**. | Base form with prefix |
| He admitted **taking** it. | Dia mengakui **mengambilnya**. | Base verb, no gerund form |

**Key interference error:** Indonesian learners often use an infinitive (to + verb) after gerund-only verbs because English "to + verb" feels natural as a translation of *untuk + verb* (in order to).

| Error | Correct |
|---|---|
| ❌ I enjoy **to swim**. | ✅ I enjoy **swimming**. |
| ❌ She avoids **to drive** at night. | ✅ She avoids **driving** at night. |
| ❌ He suggested **to take** the train. | ✅ He suggested **taking** the train. |

---

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *I enjoy **to play** tennis.* → ✅ *I enjoy **playing** tennis.* — *Why:* "enjoy" always takes a gerund.

2. ❌ *She avoids **to drive** in heavy traffic.* → ✅ *She avoids **driving** in heavy traffic.* — *Why:* "avoid" is a gerund-only verb.

3. ❌ *He suggested **to leave** early.* → ✅ *He suggested **leaving** early.* — *Why:* "suggest" cannot take a to-infinitive.

4. ❌ *They finished **to write** the report.* → ✅ *They finished **writing** the report.* — *Why:* "finish" takes a gerund; the action is complete.

5. ❌ *I look forward **to meet** you.* → ✅ *I look forward **to meeting** you.* — *Why:* "to" in "look forward to" is a preposition, so the following verb must be a gerund.

6. ❌ *She is good **at to solve** problems.* → ✅ *She is good **at solving** problems.* — *Why:* after a preposition, only a gerund is grammatical.

7. ❌ ***To swim** is healthy.* → ✅ ***Swimming** is healthy.* — *Why:* gerunds, not infinitives, are the standard subject form in modern English.

8. ❌ *He denied **to take** the money.* → ✅ *He denied **taking** the money.* — *Why:* "deny" is a gerund-only verb.

## TOEFL Practice

TOEFL Structure questions test whether you can identify correct verb patterns. Watch for gerund-only verbs and preposition + gerund constructions in both the Structure section and the Reading passages.

**Typical TOEFL trap:** A sentence will use an infinitive after a gerund-only verb. Identify the error and select the corrected version.

## Quick Summary

- A gerund is a verb + **-ing** used as a noun.
- Gerund-only verbs include: enjoy, avoid, mind, consider, suggest, finish, keep, practise, admit, deny, imagine, miss, postpone, recommend, risk, involve, include, resist.
- After **any preposition**, always use a gerund — including "look forward **to**".
- A gerund can be the **subject** of a sentence: *Swimming is fun.*
- Indonesian uses a base verb or prefix form — there is no gerund; this causes learners to use infinitives incorrectly.
- Never use *to + verb* after gerund-only verbs.
- Memorise the gerund-only verb list — each verb must be learned individually.
