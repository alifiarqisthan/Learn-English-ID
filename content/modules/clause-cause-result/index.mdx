## Explanation

English has two sides to causation: **cause clauses** (explaining *why* something happened) and **result clauses** (explaining *what* happened as a consequence). These are distinct structures — and each has formal and informal options.

### Part A — Cause Clauses: expressing reason

**Cause clauses** answer the question "Why?" by providing the reason for the main clause.

#### 1. "Because" — the most direct cause conjunction

```
Main clause + because + cause clause
Because + cause clause , + main clause
```

> She passed the exam **because** she studied hard.
> **Because** she studied hard, she passed the exam.

**Comma rule**: cause clause **first** → comma. Cause clause **second** → no comma (especially with "because").

> She studied hard **because** she wanted to pass. *(no comma — cause second)*

#### 2. "Since" and "as" — reason already known to listener

**Since** and **as** signal that the reason is **already known** or **assumed**. They are more formal than "because" and almost always come **first**.

> **Since** the data was incomplete, the researchers revised their approach.
> **As** she is the most experienced, she will lead the project.

⚠️ "Since" can mean time (since 2020) or reason (since the data was incomplete). Context usually clarifies, but in academic writing, use **because** to avoid ambiguity when expressing cause.

#### 3. Cause expressed through other structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| due to + noun | **Due to** bad weather, the flight was delayed. |
| owing to + noun | **Owing to** budget cuts, the project was cancelled. |
| because of + noun | **Because of** the strike, trains were delayed. |
| as a result of + noun | **As a result of** the merger, 50 jobs were cut. |
| on account of + noun | **On account of** illness, she missed the meeting. |

All of these take a **noun or noun phrase**, not a full clause:
- ❌ Due to **that** it was raining, the match was cancelled.
- ✅ Due to **the rain**, the match was cancelled.
- ✅ **Because** it was raining, the match was cancelled.

### Part B — Result Clauses: expressing consequence

**Result clauses** show the **outcome or effect** of the main clause.

#### 1. "So" — simple result coordinator

**"So"** joins two independent clauses where the second is the result of the first.

```
Cause , so result
```

> It was raining, **so** we stayed inside.
> She trained hard, **so** she won the competition.

⚠️ "So" is a coordinating conjunction — use a **comma before it**.

#### 2. "So…that" — intensified result

**"So…that"** expresses that a quality was **to such a degree** that a result followed.

```
so + adjective/adverb + that + result clause
```

> It was **so cold that** they cancelled the outdoor event.
> She spoke **so quietly that** nobody could hear her.
> He drove **so fast that** he got a speeding ticket.

The adjective/adverb directly follows **"so"**; **"that"** introduces the result.

#### 3. "Such…that" — intensified result with a noun

**"Such…that"** works like "so…that" but modifies a **noun** (with or without an adjective):

```
such + (a/an) + (adjective) + noun + that + result clause
```

> It was **such a difficult exam that** many students failed.
> She has **such talent that** everyone admires her.
> There was **such heavy traffic that** we missed the flight.

**Rule for articles:**
- Singular countable noun → **such a/an**: *such **a** good book*, *such **an** interesting idea*
- Plural or uncountable noun → no article: *such **good books***, *such **enthusiasm***

#### 4. "So…that" vs. "such…that"

| Structure | Modifies | Example |
|---|---|---|
| so + adjective/adverb + that | adjective or adverb | It was **so cold** that we stayed inside. |
| such + (a) + noun + that | noun | It was **such a cold night** that we stayed inside. |

Test: if there is a **noun** after the descriptor → **such**. If no noun → **so**.

> **So** tired **that** she fell asleep immediately. *(tired = adjective, no noun)*
> **Such** a tired person **that** she... *(tired person = adjective + noun)*

#### 5. Formal result connectors

| Connector | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| therefore | ; therefore, | The data was insufficient**; therefore,** the study was repeated. |
| consequently | ; consequently, | She was late**; consequently,** she missed the announcement. |
| as a result | ; as a result, | The budget was cut**; as a result,** the project was delayed. |
| thus | ; thus, | The experiment failed**; thus,** new methods were developed. |
| hence | ; hence, (formal/academic) | The sample size was small**; hence,** the findings are preliminary. |

All use **semicolon + connector + comma** when connecting two independent clauses.

### The cause-result word map

| Function | Word | Takes a... |
|---|---|---|
| Cause (reason) | because, since, as | full clause |
| Cause (noun only) | due to, because of, owing to | noun / -ing phrase |
| Result (connector) | so | independent clause (after comma) |
| Result (degree) | so...that | adjective/adverb + clause |
| Result (degree) | such...that | noun + clause |
| Result (formal) | therefore, consequently, thus | after semicolon |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian cause and result expressions

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| karena / sebab | because / since |
| karena itu / oleh karena itu | therefore / consequently |
| sehingga / maka | so (that) / as a result |
| sangat...sehingga | so...that |
| begitu...sehingga | such...that |
| akibat / akibatnya | as a result / consequently |
| akibat dari / disebabkan oleh | due to / because of |

### "Sehingga" — result or purpose?

*Sehingga* can mean both **so that** (purpose) and **so... that** (result) in Indonesian context. In English, these are distinct:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English (result) | English (purpose) |
|---|---|---|
| Dia belajar keras sehingga dia lulus. | She studied so hard that she passed. | She studied hard so that she could pass. |

Decide based on context: does the second clause express a **deliberate goal** (purpose) or an **actual outcome** (result)?

### "Due to" — Indonesian *karena* overgeneralization

Indonesians tend to translate *karena* as "because" in all contexts. In formal English:
- *karena* + noun → use **due to / because of**
- *karena* + clause → use **because / since**

| Bahasa Indonesia | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Karena hujan, pertandingan dibatalkan. | Because of that it rained... ❌ | **Because** it rained, ... ✅ OR **Due to** the rain, ... ✅ |
| Karena cuaca buruk... | Because the bad weather... ❌ | **Because of** the bad weather... ✅ OR **Because** the weather was bad... ✅ |

## Common Mistakes

**1. "Due to" + clause (should be + noun)**
- ❌ Due to **it was raining**, the match was cancelled.
- ✅ Due to **the rain**, the match was cancelled.
- ✅ **Because** it was raining, the match was cancelled.

**2. "So" without a comma**
- ❌ It rained **so** we stayed inside.
- ✅ It rained**,** **so** we stayed inside.

**3. "So...that" without "that"**
- ❌ She was **so** tired she fell asleep.
- ✅ She was **so** tired **that** she fell asleep.

  *Note: omitting "that" is acceptable in informal speech, but not in formal/academic writing.*

**4. Using "so" instead of "such" before a noun**
- ❌ It was **so** a difficult exam.
- ✅ It was **such a** difficult exam.

**5. Article error in "such a/an"**
- ❌ It was such **a** interesting idea. *(vowel sound → "an")*
- ✅ It was such **an** interesting idea.

**6. "Therefore" with only a comma**
- ❌ The data was limited, **therefore** more research is needed.
- ✅ The data was limited**; therefore,** more research is needed.

**7. Confusing "because" and "because of"**
- ❌ **Because** the rain, the flight was delayed. *(rain is a noun)*
- ✅ **Because of** the rain, the flight was delayed.
- ✅ **Because** it rained, the flight was delayed.

## Quick Summary

- **because** + full clause: She left **because** she was tired.
- **since / as** + full clause: known reason, usually first. **Since** she was tired, she left.
- **due to / because of / owing to** + noun: **Due to** fatigue, she left.
- **so** + comma between clauses: She was tired**, so** she left.
- **so + adj/adv + that**: She was **so tired that** she fell asleep.
- **such + (a/an) + noun + that**: It was **such a** long day **that** she fell asleep.
- **so** = adj/adv only | **such** = before nouns (with article for singular countable).
- **therefore / consequently / thus / hence**: formal result — use "; connector,".
