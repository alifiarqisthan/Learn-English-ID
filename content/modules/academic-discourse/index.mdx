## Explanation

Advanced discourse markers operate at the level of argument structure, emphasis, and rhetorical effect. They distinguish C1–C2 academic writing from B2 prose.

### 1. Not Only … But Also (Correlative Connector)

*Not only … but also* adds a second point that is more surprising or emphatic than the first.

**Basic structure (affirmative sentence):**
```
She not only passed the exam but also achieved the highest score.
The policy not only reduced costs but also improved efficiency.
```

**With subject-auxiliary inversion (when "not only" comes first):**

When *not only* begins the sentence, the subject and auxiliary are inverted — just like in questions.

```
Statement:     She not only passed the exam but also achieved the highest score.
Fronted form:  Not only did she pass the exam, but she also achieved the highest score.
               └──── inversion: did + she ────┘

More examples:
Not only was the experiment successful, but it also opened new avenues for research.
Not only had the team finished early, but they had also exceeded the targets.
Not only will costs decrease, but quality will also improve.
```

**Common inversion patterns:**

| Tense | Inversion form |
|---|---|
| Present simple | Not only does / do + subject |
| Past simple | Not only did + subject |
| Present perfect | Not only has / have + subject |
| Past perfect | Not only had + subject |
| Future | Not only will + subject |
| Be (all tenses) | Not only is / was / were + subject |

**Critical error:** failing to invert when *not only* fronts the sentence.
```
❌ Not only she passed, but she also scored top marks.
✅ Not only did she pass, but she also scored top marks.
```

### 2. Notwithstanding

*Notwithstanding* means **despite / in spite of** — it acknowledges an obstacle or contrary fact while asserting the main point. It does NOT mean *however*.

```
Notwithstanding the difficulties, the project was completed on time.
  = Despite the difficulties, the project was completed on time.

The evidence was disputed. Notwithstanding, the court upheld the verdict.
  = Despite this dispute, the court upheld the verdict.
```

**Position:**
- Before a noun phrase: *Notwithstanding the opposition, ...*
- After a clause (standalone): *The budget was cut. Notwithstanding, research continued.*

```
❌ The report was delayed, notwithstanding the results were excellent.
   (Cannot introduce a full clause directly after "notwithstanding" — use "notwithstanding the delay" or "although the results were excellent")

✅ Notwithstanding the delay, the results were excellent.
```

### 3. Insofar As / Inasmuch As

*Insofar as* (also written *in so far as*) introduces a qualification or limitation — meaning "to the extent that" or "in the degree that."

```
Insofar as the data is reliable, the conclusions can be trusted.
  = To the extent that the data is reliable, the conclusions can be trusted.

The law applies insofar as the conduct occurs within this jurisdiction.
This is true insofar as it relates to first-time offenders.
```

*Inasmuch as* is similar but carries a causal nuance: "because / given that":
```
Inasmuch as no evidence was presented, the case was dismissed.
  = Given that / Because no evidence was presented, the case was dismissed.
```

### 4. Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

Cleft sentences highlight one element of a sentence by restructuring it.

**"It is/was … that/who" cleft:**
```
Normal:   The committee rejected the proposal at the final meeting.
Cleft:    It was the committee that rejected the proposal.  ← emphasises who
           It was at the final meeting that the proposal was rejected.  ← emphasises when
           It was the proposal that the committee rejected.  ← emphasises what
```

**"What … is/are" (pseudo-cleft / wh-cleft):**
```
Normal:   The government needs transparency.
Wh-cleft: What the government needs is transparency.

Normal:   She lacked motivation.
Wh-cleft: What she lacked was motivation.
```

**"What … does/did is" — for verbs:**
```
Normal:   This policy reduces inequality.
Cleft:    What this policy does is reduce inequality.
           (Note: base form after "is" — not "reduces")
```

### 5. Fronting for Emphasis

Fronting moves a non-subject element to the beginning of the sentence for emphasis. It is common in academic writing to highlight key information.

```
Normal:    Such extensive data is particularly important.
Fronted:   Particularly important is such extensive data.

Normal:    The methodology deserves special note.
Fronted:   Of particular note is the methodology.

Normal:    The limitation of this study is the small sample size.
Fronted:   A key limitation of this study is the small sample size.
           Of primary concern is the small sample size.
```

**Fronting with negative adverbs (requires inversion):**
```
Rarely has such a finding been reported.
Never before had the country experienced such rapid growth.
Under no circumstances should the data be modified.
```

### 6. Discourse Markers for Academic Argumentation

| Marker | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| admittedly | Concede a point you will then qualify | *Admittedly, the sample was small; however, the effect size was large.* |
| granted | Concede a point (stronger than "admittedly") | *Granted, costs are high, but the long-term benefits outweigh them.* |
| to be sure | Concede formally | *To be sure, the model has limitations; nonetheless, it provides useful insights.* |
| to reiterate | Restate a key point for emphasis | *To reiterate, the central argument is that early intervention is essential.* |
| in other words | Paraphrase / clarify | *The results were insignificant — in other words, the hypothesis was not supported.* |
| that is (to say) | Clarify or specify | *The policy was ineffective, that is, it failed to achieve its stated objectives.* |
| by contrast | Compare two different things | *Urban areas saw growth. By contrast, rural regions stagnated.* |

### Interferensi Bahasa Indonesia

**Masalah 1 — "Not only" tanpa inversi:**

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "tidak hanya … tetapi juga …" tidak memerlukan inversi:
*Tidak hanya dia lulus, tetapi juga mendapat nilai tertinggi.*

Pelajar langsung menerjemahkan tanpa inversi:
```
❌ Not only she passed, but she also got the highest mark.
✅ Not only did she pass, but she also got the highest mark.
```

**Masalah 2 — "Notwithstanding" digunakan seperti "however":**

Karena "meskipun demikian" (nevertheless) sering dipadankan dengan "notwithstanding," pelajar memakai keduanya secara bergantian:
```
❌ The findings were significant, notwithstanding more research is needed.
   (Notwithstanding tidak berarti "however" — tidak bisa menggantikan nevertheless di sini)

✅ The findings were significant. However, more research is needed.
✅ Notwithstanding the significance of the findings, more research is needed.
```

**Masalah 3 — Kalimat cleft jarang ada dalam Bahasa Indonesia:**

Bahasa Indonesia biasanya menekankan dengan intonasi atau kata seperti "yang paling penting adalah." Pelajar ragu menggunakan cleft sentences dalam tulisan akademik:
*Yang paling penting adalah transparansi.* → *What is most important is transparency.*

## Common Mistakes

1. **"Not only" fronted without subject-auxiliary inversion**
   ❌ *Not only the policy failed, but it also caused damage.*
   ✅ *Not only did the policy fail, but it also caused damage.*
   *Why:* When *not only* begins the sentence, English requires inversion (auxiliary + subject), identical to question word order.

2. **"Notwithstanding" used to mean "however"**
   ❌ *The experiment was a success. Notwithstanding, the results need verification.*
   ✅ *The experiment was a success. Nevertheless, the results need verification.*
   ✅ *Notwithstanding its success, the experiment's results need verification.*
   *Why:* "Notwithstanding" means "despite" — it is a concession marker, not a contrast/pivot marker like "however."

3. **Cleft sentence with wrong verb form**
   ❌ *What this policy does is reduces inequality.*
   ✅ *What this policy does is reduce inequality.*
   *Why:* After "What [subject] does is," the verb appears in the base (infinitive) form — not the third-person singular inflected form.

4. **"It is … which" instead of "that" in cleft sentences**
   ❌ *It was the budget cuts which caused the delay.* (acceptable in relative clauses but non-standard in clefts)
   ✅ *It was the budget cuts that caused the delay.*
   *Why:* In formal cleft sentences, *that* (not *which*) is the standard connector.

5. **"Admittedly, but" (double concession marker)**
   ❌ *Admittedly the approach has weaknesses, but however further research is warranted.*
   ✅ *Admittedly, the approach has weaknesses; however, further research is warranted.*
   *Why:* Do not stack concession markers (*admittedly*) with contrast markers (*but*, *however*) redundantly.

6. **Fronting without subject-verb agreement adjustment**
   ❌ *Of particular importance are the methodology.*
   ✅ *Of particular importance is the methodology.*
   *Why:* When fronting, the verb agrees with the real subject (which follows the verb after fronting).

7. **"Insofar as" used as a simple reason clause**
   ❌ *Insofar as the weather was bad, we cancelled the event.*
   ✅ *Since / Because the weather was bad, we cancelled the event.*
   *Why:* "Insofar as" qualifies or limits a claim — it means "to the degree/extent that," not simply "because." Use *because/since* for straightforward cause.

8. **"In other words" at the start of a new argument (not a paraphrase)**
   ❌ *The economy is struggling. In other words, we need a new trade policy.*
   ✅ *The economy is struggling. That is to say, growth has stalled for three consecutive quarters.*
   ✅ *The economy is struggling, which means a new trade policy may be necessary.*
   *Why:* "In other words" signals a restatement or clarification of the same idea — not a new argumentative point.

## TOEFL Practice

At C1 level, TOEFL and IELTS examiners award the highest scores when candidates demonstrate:

- **Syntactic variety**: cleft sentences and fronting show command of complex structures.
- **Rhetorical control**: *admittedly*, *granted*, *to be sure* signal awareness of counterarguments — a key feature of high-scoring essays.
- **Precision**: *insofar as* and *notwithstanding* show you can qualify claims precisely.
- **Not only…but also** (with inversion when fronted) is a direct indicator of C1+ grammar control.

Practice tip: In your next practice essay, deliberately include one cleft sentence (*It is X that…*), one fronted element (*Of particular importance is…*), and one concessive discourse marker (*admittedly*, *granted*).

## Quick Summary

- **Not only…but also** (fronted): requires subject-auxiliary inversion — *Not only did she pass, but she also…*
- **Notwithstanding** means *despite* — it is a concession marker, not a synonym for *however*.
- **Insofar as** means *to the extent that* — use it to qualify or limit a claim, not as a simple cause.
- **Cleft sentences** (*It is X that…* / *What X does is…*) emphasise a specific element; use base verb form after *What X does is*.
- **Fronting** (*Particularly important is…* / *Of note is…*) creates academic emphasis; remember subject-verb agreement follows the real subject.
- **Admittedly / granted / to be sure** concede a point before a rebuttal — do not combine with *but* or *however* in the same clause.
- **In other words / that is to say** paraphrase the same idea — do not use them to introduce a new argument.
- These devices are C1+ markers — examiners notice them and reward their correct use.
