## Explanation

The **third conditional** imagines how the **past could have been different**. It describes situations that did NOT happen and explores the consequence if they had. It is often used to express **regret, relief, or criticism**.

### Core Structure

| Clause | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| If-clause (condition) | If + past perfect (had + V3) | If I had studied harder… |
| Main clause (result) | would have + past participle (V3) | …I would have passed the exam. |

**Formula:** `If + subject + had + V3, subject + would have + V3`

> If she **had left** earlier, she **would have caught** the train.
> They **would have won** the match if they **had practised** more.

The if-clause can come first or second. When it comes first, use a comma.

---

### Use Cases

| Purpose | Example |
|---|---|
| Expressing regret | If I had saved more money, I would have bought that car. |
| Imagining a better outcome | If he had apologised, she would have forgiven him. |
| Criticising a past action | If you had listened to me, this wouldn't have happened. |
| Expressing relief | If they hadn't called an ambulance, he would have died. |

---

### Negatives

Negate either clause independently:

| Clause negated | Example |
|---|---|
| If-clause | If she **hadn't overslept**, she would have arrived on time. |
| Main clause | If he had tried, he **wouldn't have failed**. |
| Both | If they **hadn't argued**, the project **wouldn't have collapsed**. |

---

### Other Modals in the Result Clause

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| would have | certain hypothetical result | I would have helped you. |
| could have | ability or possibility in the past | You could have warned me. |
| might have | less certain past possibility | She might have agreed. |

> If you had asked earlier, I **could have** arranged it.
> If we had left sooner, we **might have** made it in time.

---

### Questions

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Yes/No | Would + subject + have + V3 + if + past perfect? | Would you have taken the job if they had offered it? |
| Wh- | Wh- word + would + subject + have + V3 + if…? | What would you have done if you had missed the flight? |

> **Would she have come** if we had invited her earlier?
> **How would you have reacted** if you had seen that?

---

### Contrast: Second vs Third Conditional

| Feature | Second Conditional | Third Conditional |
|---|---|---|
| Time frame | Present / future hypothetical | Past hypothetical |
| If-clause | If + past simple | If + past perfect |
| Result clause | would + V1 | would have + V3 |
| Example | If I had more time, I would help. | If I had had more time, I would have helped. |

Note: "If I had had" looks unusual but is grammatically correct — the first "had" is the auxiliary for past perfect; the second is the main verb.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Seandainya Sudah… / Andaikan Telah…"

Bahasa Indonesia menggunakan **seandainya sudah** atau **andaikan telah** untuk kondisi hipotetis di masa lampau.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Seandainya saya sudah belajar lebih keras, saya pasti lulus. | If I had studied harder, I would have passed. |
| Andaikan dia telah datang lebih awal, dia tidak akan ketinggalan. | If he had arrived earlier, he wouldn't have missed it. |
| Kalau kamu sudah bilang, aku akan membantu. | If you had told me, I would have helped. |

### Perbedaan Penting

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, kata "sudah" atau "telah" memberi sinyal bahwa peristiwa itu terjadi di masa lalu — namun bentuk kata kerja tidak berubah seperti di bahasa Inggris. Dalam bahasa Inggris, **wajib** menggunakan **had + V3** di if-clause dan **would have + V3** di result clause.

**Kesalahan umum pelajar Indonesia:**

> ❌ If I studied harder, I would have passed. *(mixes second + third)*
> ✅ If I **had studied** harder, I would have passed.

> ❌ If I would have known, I would have told you.
> ✅ If I **had known**, I would have told you.

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *If I **would have known**, I would have called you.* → ✅ *If I **had known**, I would have called you.* *Why:* Never use "would have" in the if-clause — use "had + V3" (past perfect).

2. ❌ *If she **had went** to the doctor earlier, she would have recovered faster.* → ✅ *If she **had gone** to the doctor earlier…* *Why:* After "had," use the past participle (V3), not the simple past (V2). "Went" is V2; "gone" is V3.

3. ❌ *If they **studied** harder, they would have passed.* → ✅ *If they **had studied** harder, they would have passed.* *Why:* Third conditional requires past perfect (had + V3) in the if-clause, not just past simple.

4. ❌ *She would have been promoted if she **worked** harder.* → ✅ *She would have been promoted if she **had worked** harder.* *Why:* The if-clause needs "had + V3" to signal the unreal past.

5. ❌ *If I had known, I **would tell** you.* → ✅ *If I had known, I **would have told** you.* *Why:* The result clause needs "would have + V3," not just "would + V1."

6. ❌ *If we **had left** earlier, we **would arrive** on time.* → ✅ *If we had left earlier, we **would have arrived** on time.* *Why:* Both halves must match — past perfect condition → "would have + V3" result.

7. ❌ *Would you **help** me if I **had asked** you?* → ✅ *Would you **have helped** me if I **had asked** you?* *Why:* The result clause question also needs "would have + V3."

8. ❌ *If I had had more time, I would have **finishing** the report.* → ✅ *…I would have **finished** the report.* *Why:* After "would have," use the past participle, not the -ing form.

## TOEFL Practice

Third conditional is tested in TOEFL Structure questions on:
- Correct use of "had + V3" vs "V2" in if-clauses
- "Would have + V3" vs "would + V1" in result clauses
- Distinguishing V2 and V3 forms (irregular verbs: went/gone, saw/seen, did/done)

**Strategy:** If the if-clause has "had," the result clause must have "would have." Check irregular past participles carefully.

## Quick Summary

- Third conditional = If + **had + V3**, **would have + V3**
- Use it to imagine a **different past** — the event did NOT actually happen
- Common uses: expressing **regret**, imagining a better outcome, criticising a past action
- **Never** put "would have" in the if-clause
- After "had," always use the **past participle (V3)**, not simple past (V2)
- Result clause can use **could have** or **might have** for weaker certainty
- Compare: Second conditional (present/future hypothetical) vs Third (past hypothetical)
- Indonesian equivalent: **seandainya sudah / andaikan telah** + verb
