## Explanation

Beyond modal verbs and adverbs, **hedging verbs** and **hedging phrases** are core tools for academic writing. They distance claims from absolute truth and signal that conclusions are tentative or limited.

### Hedging verbs

Hedging verbs replace absolute verbs ("prove", "show") with more cautious alternatives:

| Absolute | Hedged |
|---|---|
| This **proves** that... | This **suggests** that... |
| The data **shows** a link. | The data **indicates** / **implies** a link. |
| Students **fail** because of this. | Students **tend to** struggle with this. |
| He **is** wrong. | He **appears** to be mistaken. |

**Key hedging verbs:**

| Verb | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| suggest | cautious claim from evidence | The results **suggest** a correlation. |
| indicate | cautious evidence-based interpretation | The data **indicates** a trend. |
| imply | logical but unstated conclusion | The findings **imply** further investigation. |
| seem | subjective/appearance of truth | It **seems** that the effect is small. |
| appear | formal version of "seem" | The approach **appears** to be effective. |
| tend to | generalised pattern | Students **tend to** underestimate workload. |

### "Seem" and "appear" — structure

Both follow two patterns:

```
Subject + seem / appear + to-infinitive
Subject + seem / appear + that-clause
```

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| seem + to-infinitive | The approach **seems to be** effective. |
| seem + that-clause | It **seems that** the approach is effective. |
| appear + to-infinitive | Results **appear to support** the hypothesis. |
| appear + that-clause | It **appears that** results support the hypothesis. |

### "Tend to" — generalised patterns

"Tend to + V1" expresses a generalised tendency without claiming universality:

> Learners **tend to** rely on their L1 when under pressure.
> Prices **tend to** rise in the fourth quarter.
> Students **tend to** perform better with structured feedback.

### Hedging phrases — sentence-level devices

These phrases introduce hedged claims at the sentence level:

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| It appears that... | **It appears that** the treatment is effective. |
| It seems that... | **It seems that** further work is needed. |
| It is possible that... | **It is possible that** the results are skewed. |
| It is likely that... | **It is likely that** the trend will continue. |
| To some extent... | **To some extent**, these findings confirm earlier research. |
| In many cases... | **In many cases**, the approach fails to generalise. |
| Under certain conditions... | **Under certain conditions**, the effect may be significant. |
| For the most part... | **For the most part**, the data is consistent. |

### Approximator phrases

Used to hedge numerical or quantitative claims:

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| approximately | **Approximately** 60% of participants improved. |
| around / about | **Around** half of the cases showed improvement. |
| roughly | Results **roughly** align with earlier findings. |
| in the region of | The effect size is **in the region of** 0.4. |

### Scope-limiting phrases

Used to acknowledge that findings apply in limited contexts:

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| to some extent | This is true **to some extent**. |
| in some cases | **In some cases**, the intervention had no effect. |
| under certain conditions | The method works **under certain conditions**. |
| within this sample | Results apply **within this sample** only. |

### Summary of hedging tools

| Tool | Example |
|---|---|
| Modal verbs | The data **may suggest** a link. |
| Hedging adverbs | This **probably** reflects bias. |
| Hedging verbs | The results **indicate** a trend. |
| Seem/appear | Results **appear to support** the claim. |
| Tend to | Learners **tend to** over-generalise rules. |
| Sentence-level phrases | **It is possible that** further research is needed. |
| Approximators | **Approximately** 40% showed improvement. |
| Scope limiters | These findings apply **to some extent**. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Tampaknya" / "Cenderung" / "Kemungkinan"

| Bahasa Indonesia | English hedging |
|---|---|
| Tampaknya metode ini efektif. | This method **appears to be** / **seems to be** effective. |
| Mahasiswa cenderung kesulitan dengan ini. | Students **tend to** struggle with this. |
| Kemungkinan hasilnya kurang akurat. | The results **may not be** accurate. / It is **possible that** the results are less accurate. |
| Sebagian besar data konsisten. | **For the most part**, the data is consistent. |
| Sekitar 60% responden setuju. | **Approximately** 60% of respondents agreed. |

### Why Indonesian academic writers struggle with hedging verbs

In Indonesian academic writing, strong verbs like *membuktikan* (prove) and *menunjukkan* (show) are common. Students directly translate these:
- ❌ This study **proves** that stress affects memory.
- ✅ This study **suggests** / **indicates** that stress may affect memory.

### "Tend to" — no Indonesian direct equivalent

*Cenderung* = tend to. Students sometimes omit it:
- ❌ Students struggle with modal verbs.
- ✅ Students **tend to** struggle with modal verbs. *(pattern, not universal)*

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "prove" where "suggest" is appropriate**
- ❌ The data proves that exercise is beneficial.
- ✅ The data **suggests** that exercise may be beneficial.
- *Why:* In academic writing, data rarely "proves" — it "suggests", "indicates", or "implies". Reserve "prove" for mathematical proofs.

**2. "Seem" without "to" in the to-infinitive pattern**
- ❌ The results seem work well.
- ✅ The results **seem to work** well.
- *Why:* "Seem" takes either "seem + to-infinitive" or "it seems + that-clause". Not "seem + V1".

**3. "It appears that" vs "it appears to be" — mixing the patterns**
- ❌ It appears that the method to be effective.
- ✅ It **appears that** the method **is** effective. *(that-clause)*
- ✅ The method **appears to be** effective. *(to-infinitive)*
- *Why:* Don't mix the two patterns in a single sentence.

**4. Omitting approximators for numbers**
- ❌ 60% of participants improved.
- ✅ **Approximately** 60% of participants improved.
- *Why:* In academic writing, quantitative claims should be hedged with approximators unless the number is exact.

**5. Using scope limiters redundantly**
- ❌ The results are, to some extent, somewhat inconclusive.
- ✅ The results are **somewhat** inconclusive. / The results are **inconclusive to some extent**.
- *Why:* One scope limiter is enough. Stacking makes writing verbose.

**6. "Tend to" + V-ing (wrong form)**
- ❌ Students tend to struggling with this structure.
- ✅ Students **tend to** struggle with this structure.
- *Why:* "Tend to" is followed by V1 (base form), not V-ing.

**7. Over-using "it is possible that" for every claim**
- ❌ It is possible that this is true. It is possible that the results are valid. It is possible that the method works.
- ✅ Vary hedging devices: *suggest, appear, tend to, may, likely, probably*
- *Why:* Repetition of the same hedge reduces academic register quality.

## Quick Summary

- **Hedging verbs**: suggest, indicate, imply, seem, appear, tend to (replace "prove", "show", "is")
- **Seem/appear**: "seem to + V1" or "it seems that + clause" — don't mix the patterns
- **Tend to + V1**: expresses a generalised tendency — not "tend to + V-ing"
- **Sentence-level phrases**: "It appears that…", "It is possible that…", "To some extent…"
- **Approximators**: approximately, around, roughly, in the region of
- **Scope limiters**: to some extent, in some cases, under certain conditions, within this sample
- Avoid stacking hedges; vary hedging devices across sentences
- "Prove" ≠ academic hedging — use "suggest" or "indicate" instead
