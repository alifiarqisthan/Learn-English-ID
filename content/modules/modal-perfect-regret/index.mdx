## Explanation

Modal perfects can also express **regret** (something you wish had happened differently) and **criticism** (something someone else did wrong or didn't do). These are different from deduction and require careful tense awareness.

### Should have + V3 — regret and criticism

```
Subject + should have + V3   (but didn't)
Subject + shouldn't have + V3   (but did)
```

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Regret (you didn't do it) | I **should have studied** harder. *(I didn't)* |
| Regret (you did it) | I **shouldn't have eaten** so much. *(I did)* |
| Criticism of others | You **should have called** me. *(you didn't)* |
| Criticism of others (negative) | She **shouldn't have said** that. *(she did)* |

> "Should have" = the opposite happened — it implies the action did NOT take place.
> "Shouldn't have" = the action DID take place and it was wrong.

### Could have + V3 — unrealised past possibilities

"Could have" describes something that was **possible in the past but didn't happen**:

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Unrealised option (regret) | I **could have helped** you — why didn't you ask? |
| Missed opportunity | She **could have been** a great athlete. |
| Reproach (missed option) | You **could have told** me earlier. |
| Suggestion about the past | We **could have taken** the bus instead. |

> Compare:
> - "She **should have called**" = it was the right thing to do but she didn't *(criticism)*
> - "She **could have called**" = calling was an option she didn't take *(missed opportunity/reproach)*

### Needn't have + V3 — unnecessary past action

"Needn't have + V3" = the action happened but it wasn't necessary:

> You **needn't have brought** flowers — but thank you!
> I **needn't have rushed** — the meeting was cancelled.

Compare with "didn't have to" (which means the necessity was known in advance):

| Structure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| needn't have + V3 | Did the action, but it turned out unnecessary |
| didn't have to + V1 | Knew it wasn't necessary, so didn't do it |

> "I **needn't have worried** — everything went smoothly." *(I did worry, but it was unnecessary)*
> "I **didn't have to** attend — so I stayed home." *(I knew in advance, so I didn't go)*

### Might have + V3 — mild suggestion or reproach

"Might have" in the context of regret/criticism is softer than "should have":

> You **might have told** me it was cancelled! *(mild complaint)*
> She **might have mentioned** the change in plans.

### Summary table

| Structure | Action happened? | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| should have + V3 | ❌ (it didn't) | It was the right thing to do |
| shouldn't have + V3 | ✅ (it did) | It was the wrong thing to do |
| could have + V3 | ❌ (it didn't) | It was possible but you didn't do it |
| needn't have + V3 | ✅ (it did) | It happened, but it wasn't necessary |
| might have + V3 | ❌ (it didn't) | Mild reproach — you could have done it |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Seharusnya" / "Harusnya sudah"

Indonesian uses **seharusnya** (should have) and **tidak perlu / tidak seharusnya** (shouldn't have / needn't have):

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Seharusnya kamu belajar lebih keras. | You **should have studied** harder. |
| Kamu seharusnya tidak mengatakan itu. | You **shouldn't have said** that. |
| Kamu seharusnya memberitahu saya. | You **could have / should have told** me. |
| Tidak perlu kamu membawa itu. | You **needn't have brought** that. |

### Common interference errors

**1. Confusing "should have" (regret) with "must have" (deduction)**

*Seharusnya* can mean both. But:
- "Should have" = regret/criticism (the action didn't happen)
- "Must have" = deduction (the action almost certainly happened)
- ❌ I must have studied harder. *(sounds like a deduction, not regret)*
- ✅ I **should have studied** harder. *(regret)*

**2. Missing V3 — using V2 instead**

*Seharusnya saya belajar* → students write "should have studied" correctly, but sometimes write "should have study" or "should have studied✓":
- ❌ I should have study harder.
- ✅ I **should have studied** harder.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Omitting "have" in regret/criticism**
- ❌ You should called me earlier.
- ✅ You **should have called** me earlier.
- *Why:* The fixed structure is modal + **have** + V3. "Have" cannot be dropped.

**2. Using V2 after "have" (should have went)**
- ❌ She should have went to the doctor sooner.
- ✅ She **should have gone** to the doctor sooner.
- *Why:* "Have" is followed by V3 (past participle). "Go" → V3 = "gone", not "went".

**3. Confusing "should have" (positive regret) with "shouldn't have" (negative regret)**
- ❌ I should have eaten so much — I feel sick.
- ✅ I **shouldn't have eaten** so much — I feel sick. *(the eating happened — regret)*
- *Why:* "Should have" = the action didn't happen. "Shouldn't have" = the action did happen but was wrong.

**4. Confusing "needn't have" and "didn't have to"**
- Incorrect: I didn't have to prepare so much — they cancelled the presentation. *(implies you didn't prepare)*
- Correct: I **needn't have prepared** so much — they cancelled. *(you did prepare, but it was unnecessary)*
- *Why:* "Needn't have + V3" = you did the action; "didn't have to + V1" = you didn't do the action (because it wasn't required).

**5. "Could have" vs "should have" for criticism**
- "Could have" = it was possible *(reproach, missed option)*
- "Should have" = it was the right/expected thing to do *(stronger criticism)*
- You **could have** told me. *(you had the option but didn't use it)*
- You **should have** told me. *(it was your duty/obligation to tell me)*

**6. Using "might have" where "should have" is expected for strong criticism**
- Mild: You **might have** mentioned the meeting was moved.
- Stronger: You **should have** told me about the meeting.
- *Why:* "Might have" is a softer reproach; "should have" is a clearer criticism.

## Quick Summary

- **Should have + V3** = regret/criticism — the action did NOT happen
- **Shouldn't have + V3** = negative regret — the action DID happen and was wrong
- **Could have + V3** = unrealised past option — possible but didn't happen
- **Needn't have + V3** = the action happened but was unnecessary
- **Might have + V3** = mild reproach — softer than "should have"
- Structure: modal + **have** + **V3** — never drop "have", never use V2
- "Should have went" ❌ → "should have **gone**" ✅ — always V3 after "have"
- Key distinction: *seharusnya* → "should have" (didn't do it) vs "shouldn't have" (did do it and was wrong)
