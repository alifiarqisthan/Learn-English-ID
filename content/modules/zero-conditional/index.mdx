## Explanation

The **zero conditional** expresses **universal truths, scientific facts, and things that are always true**. Both the if-clause and the result clause use present simple.

### Structure

```
If + present simple, present simple
```

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| If you heat ice, it melts. | Scientific fact |
| If it rains, the ground gets wet. | Always true |
| If you press this button, the light turns on. | Cause and effect |
| If students don't study, they fail. | General truth |

> "When" can replace "if" in zero conditionals with the same meaning:
> - If you heat water to 100°C, it boils. = When you heat water to 100°C, it boils.

### Clause order

The if-clause can come first or second — the meaning is the same:

| If-clause first | Result clause first |
|---|---|
| If you mix blue and yellow, you get green. | You get green if you mix blue and yellow. |
| If it's cold, water freezes. | Water freezes if it's cold. |

> When the if-clause comes **first**, use a comma after it. When the result clause comes first, **no comma** is needed.

### Negative zero conditional

```
If + don't/doesn't + V1, subject + don't/doesn't + V1
```

> If you **don't** water plants, they **die**.
> If the temperature **doesn't** drop, the ice **doesn't** melt.
> If students **don't** practise, their skills **don't** improve.

### Questions in zero conditionals

> What happens if you **mix** acid and water?
> What do plants **do** if they **don't** get enough light?

### When vs if

In zero conditionals, "when" and "if" are interchangeable:

| If version | When version |
|---|---|
| If you heat metal, it expands. | When you heat metal, it expands. |
| If bacteria enter the body, they multiply. | When bacteria enter the body, they multiply. |

"When" is often preferred for facts that are very reliable/inevitable. "If" adds a slight sense of condition.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Jika" / "Kalau" + present

Indonesian uses **jika** (formal) or **kalau** (informal) for conditionals, but doesn't distinguish between zero and first conditionals the way English does.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English (zero conditional) |
|---|---|
| Jika kamu memanaskan es, ia mencair. | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| Kalau cuacanya dingin, air membeku. | If the weather is cold, water freezes. |
| Jika tanaman tidak disiram, mereka layu. | If plants aren't watered, they wilt. |

### Why students mix zero and first conditionals

Indonesian *jika/kalau* can be used for both general truths AND future possibilities. Students sometimes use "will" in zero conditionals:
- ❌ If you heat water, it will boil.
- ✅ If you heat water, it **boils**. *(zero: scientific fact)*
- ✅ If you heat water, it **will boil**. *(first: a specific future event)*

The zero conditional uses only present simple — no "will".

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "will" in the result clause of a zero conditional**
- ❌ If you mix red and blue, you will get purple.
- ✅ If you mix red and blue, you **get** purple.
- *Why:* Zero conditional = universal truth → present simple in both clauses. "Will" changes it to a first conditional prediction.

**2. Using past tense instead of present simple**
- ❌ If you heated ice, it melted.
- ✅ If you heat ice, it **melts**.
- *Why:* Zero conditionals express timeless truths, always using present simple.

**3. Missing comma after the if-clause when it comes first**
- ❌ If the temperature drops water freezes.
- ✅ If the temperature drops**,** water freezes.
- *Why:* When the if-clause comes first, it is a subordinate clause and must be followed by a comma.

**4. Using "when" and thinking it changes the meaning**
- ❌ "When" makes it more certain than "if".
- ✅ In zero conditionals, "when" and "if" mean the same thing.
- *Why:* Both introduce the condition for a universal truth.

**5. Treating zero conditionals as hypothetical**
- ❌ If I were you, I study more. *(mixing conditional types)*
- ✅ If you study more, you improve. *(zero: general truth)*
- *Why:* Zero conditionals are about facts, not hypothetical situations.

## Quick Summary

- **Zero conditional**: If + present simple, present simple
- Expresses **universal truths, scientific facts, and cause-effect relationships**
- Both clauses always use **present simple** — never "will"
- **When** and **if** are interchangeable in zero conditionals
- If-clause first → **comma** needed; result clause first → no comma
- "If you heat ice, it melts" — fact, not a prediction
- Negative: "If you **don't** water plants, they **die**."
