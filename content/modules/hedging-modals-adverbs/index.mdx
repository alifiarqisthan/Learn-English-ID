## Explanation

**Hedging** is the practice of expressing claims with appropriate caution — avoiding absolute statements. In academic writing, hedging signals intellectual honesty and is expected at IELTS band 7+ and TOEFL writing score 24+.

### Why hedge?

| Absolute (too strong) | Hedged (appropriate) |
|---|---|
| Exercise **causes** improved mood. | Exercise **may cause** improved mood. |
| Students **always** struggle with this. | Students **often** struggle with this. |
| The data **proves** this theory. | The data **suggests** this theory. |

Absolute claims invite counter-argument. Hedging acknowledges complexity and uncertainty, which is more academically appropriate.

### Hedging with modal verbs

| Modal | Degree of certainty | Example |
|---|---|---|
| will | ~100% (certain) | This **will** affect outcomes. |
| should | ~80% (expected) | This **should** improve results. |
| may | ~50% (possible) | This **may** affect outcomes. |
| might | ~30% (tentative) | This **might** be related. |
| could | variable (possible) | This **could** suggest a pattern. |

In academic writing, prefer **may**, **might**, and **could** over unhedged statements.

> The results **may** indicate a correlation between diet and performance.
> This **could** be attributed to socioeconomic factors.
> Further research **might** be needed to confirm this finding.

### Hedging adverbs — frequency and certainty

**Frequency adverbs** avoid claiming something always happens:

| Absolute | Hedged with frequency |
|---|---|
| Students fail because of this. | Students **often** fail because of this. |
| This causes problems. | This **frequently** causes problems. |
| Teachers use this method. | Teachers **generally** use this method. |

**Certitude adverbs** soften claims about truth:

| Absolute | Hedged with certainty |
|---|---|
| This is correct. | This is **probably** correct. |
| The trend will continue. | The trend **will possibly** continue. |
| These factors explain it. | These factors **largely** explain it. |

### Common hedging adverbs by category

| Category | Adverbs |
|---|---|
| High likelihood | **probably**, **likely**, **generally** |
| Moderate possibility | **possibly**, **perhaps**, **conceivably** |
| Frequency | **often**, **frequently**, **typically**, **commonly**, **usually** |
| Degree | **largely**, **mainly**, **primarily**, **broadly**, **somewhat** |
| Scope limitation | **in most cases**, **for the most part**, **to a large extent** |

### Position of hedging adverbs

Hedging adverbs usually appear before the main verb or before the adjective they modify:

> The results **probably** reflect sample bias.
> This is **largely** due to differences in methodology.
> The effect is **generally** considered significant.
> Outcomes **typically** improve with intervention.

### Combining hedges

Academic writing often stacks hedging devices carefully:

> This finding **may possibly** indicate… *(redundant — avoid stacking)*
> The data **suggests** that outcomes **may** differ… *(good — verb + modal)*

> Avoid stacking two modals or two certainty adverbs ("may possibly", "might perhaps") — this is often viewed as redundant.

### Hedging vs over-hedging

| Too much hedging | Appropriate |
|---|---|
| It **might perhaps possibly** be the case that... | It **may** be the case that... |
| This **could possibly** sometimes occur. | This **sometimes** occurs. |

Over-hedging makes writing unclear and tentative. Aim for one hedging device per claim.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Mungkin" and "Kemungkinan"

Indonesian uses **mungkin** (possibly/perhaps), **kemungkinan besar** (probably), **sering** (often), and **biasanya** (usually/generally):

| Bahasa Indonesia | English academic hedging |
|---|---|
| Mungkin ada hubungannya. | There **may** be a connection. |
| Kemungkinan besar hal ini disebabkan oleh... | This is **likely** caused by... |
| Hal ini sering terjadi. | This **often** / **frequently** occurs. |
| Biasanya hal ini berdampak pada... | This **generally** / **typically** affects... |

### Why Indonesian academic writers over-state

Academic writing in Indonesian sometimes accepts stronger claims (*terbukti bahwa* = "it is proven that") that English academic writing would hedge (*it **has been suggested** that*). This leads to:

- ❌ Exercise **proves** better mental health.
- ✅ Exercise **may contribute to** better mental health.

### Adverb placement interference

In Indonesian, adverbs like *mungkin* typically come before the subject. In English, hedging adverbs come before the main verb or after the auxiliary:

- ❌ **Probably** the results reflect bias.
- ✅ The results **probably** reflect bias.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using absolute statements where hedging is expected**
- ❌ Stress causes lower academic performance.
- ✅ Stress **may cause** lower academic performance.
- *Why:* Academic writing requires hedging causal claims unless they are established facts.

**2. Placing hedging adverbs at the start of the sentence (like Indonesian)**
- ❌ Possibly the findings are significant.
- ✅ The findings are **possibly** significant. / The findings **may be** significant.
- *Why:* Hedging adverbs usually appear in mid-position (before main verb or before adjective).

**3. Stacking two hedges (over-hedging)**
- ❌ This may possibly indicate a trend.
- ✅ This **may indicate** a trend. / This **possibly indicates** a trend.
- *Why:* One hedging device per claim is standard. Two makes it redundant.

**4. Using "will" where "may" is needed (over-certainty)**
- ❌ This method will improve outcomes.
- ✅ This method **may improve** outcomes. / This method **is likely to** improve outcomes.
- *Why:* In academic writing, future certainty needs strong evidence. "Will" for uncertain outcomes is too assertive.

**5. Using "generally" where "always" would be wrong but the context is still too strong**
- ❌ Students generally always fail without support. *(redundant: "generally" + "always")*
- ✅ Students **generally** struggle without support. / Students **often** fail without support.
- *Why:* Never combine "generally"/"often" with "always"/"never".

**6. Hedging established facts (unnecessary hedging)**
- ❌ Water may boil at 100°C at standard pressure.
- ✅ Water **boils** at 100°C at standard pressure.
- *Why:* Hedge uncertain claims; state established facts directly.

## Quick Summary

- Hedge with **modals**: may, might, could (prefer over "will" for uncertain academic claims)
- Hedge with **frequency adverbs**: often, frequently, generally, typically, commonly
- Hedge with **certainty adverbs**: probably, possibly, largely, mainly, broadly
- **Adverb position**: before the main verb or before the adjective — not at the start of a sentence
- **Don't stack** two hedges: not "may possibly" → use either "may" or "possibly"
- Hedge uncertain claims — state established facts directly
- Scale: **will** (certain) → **should** (expected) → **may** (~50%) → **might/could** (tentative)
