## Explanation

Comparative adjectives are used to compare **two** things, people, or ideas. There are two main patterns depending on the length of the adjective.

### Formation Rules

| Adjective type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 syllable | add **-er** | tall → **taller** |
| 1 syllable ending in -e | add **-r** | large → **larger** |
| 1 syllable ending in consonant-vowel-consonant | double final consonant + **-er** | big → **bigger**, hot → **hotter** |
| 2 syllables ending in -y | change y → i + **-er** | happy → **happier**, early → **earlier** |
| 2+ syllables (most) | **more** + adjective | expensive → **more expensive** |

### Irregular Forms

| Base | Comparative |
|---|---|
| good | **better** |
| bad | **worse** |
| far | **farther / further** |
| little (amount) | **less** |
| many / much | **more** |

### Using "than"

Always use **than** after the comparative when mentioning the second item:

- This phone is **cheaper than** that one.
- She is **more confident than** her brother.
- Today is **worse than** yesterday.

You can omit *than* when the comparison is already clear from context:
- "Which bag is lighter?" — "This one is **lighter**."

### Common Structure

> **Subject + verb + comparative adjective + than + second item**

- Jakarta is **bigger than** Bandung.
- The exam was **more difficult than** I expected.
- He speaks **better English than** his classmates.

### Errors to Avoid

| Error | Correct form |
|---|---|
| more bigger | bigger |
| more taller | taller |
| gooder | better |
| more good | better |
| badder | worse |
| more bad | worse |
| taller as him | taller **than** him |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Perbedaan dengan Bahasa Indonesia

Dalam bahasa Indonesia, kata "**lebih...dari/daripada**" digunakan untuk semua perbandingan:
- *Dia lebih tinggi daripada saya.* → He is **taller than** me.
- *Harganya lebih mahal dari yang itu.* → It is **more expensive than** that one.

Bahasa Indonesia tidak mengubah bentuk kata sifat untuk perbandingan. Ini sering menyebabkan kesalahan dalam bahasa Inggris.

### Tabel Kontras

| Bahasa Indonesia | ❌ Kesalahan umum | ✅ Inggris yang benar |
|---|---|---|
| lebih besar | more bigger | bigger |
| lebih bagus | more good | better |
| lebih jelek | more bad / badder | worse |
| lebih mahal | more expensive ✅ | more expensive |
| lebih cepat | more fast | faster |
| lebih mudah | more easy | easier |

### Interferensi Khas

Karena pola "lebih + kata sifat" dalam bahasa Indonesia selalu menggunakan "lebih" di depan, pelajar Indonesia cenderung menambahkan "more" di depan **semua** kata sifat Inggris, bahkan kata sifat satu suku kata. Ingat: satu suku kata → pakai **-er**, bukan **more**.

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *This mountain is more higher than that one.* → ✅ *This mountain is **higher** than that one.* *Why:* "High" is one syllable — use -er, not more + -er.

2. ❌ *He is more good at math than his sister.* → ✅ *He is **better** at math than his sister.* *Why:* "Good" is irregular — the comparative is "better."

3. ❌ *The result was more bad than we expected.* → ✅ *The result was **worse** than we expected.* *Why:* "Bad" is irregular — the comparative is "worse."

4. ❌ *She is taller as me.* → ✅ *She is taller **than** me.* *Why:* Comparatives use "than," not "as."

5. ❌ *This bag is more cheap than that one.* → ✅ *This bag is **cheaper** than that one.* *Why:* "Cheap" is one syllable — add -er.

6. ❌ *Today is more hot than yesterday.* → ✅ *Today is **hotter** than yesterday.* *Why:* "Hot" is one syllable, consonant-vowel-consonant → double the t + -er.

7. ❌ *My phone is more newer than yours.* → ✅ *My phone is **newer** than yours.* *Why:* Never combine "more" with an -er adjective.

8. ❌ *He drives more faster than she does.* → ✅ *He drives **faster** than she does.* *Why:* "Fast" is one syllable — use -er only.

## TOEFL Practice

TOEFL Structure questions test whether you can identify the correct comparative form in a sentence. Focus on:
- Choosing between **-er** and **more + adjective**
- Identifying double comparative errors (*more bigger*)
- Using **than** correctly after a comparative

## Quick Summary

- Use **-er** for most 1-syllable adjectives: tall → taller, fast → faster
- Use **more** + adjective for most 2+ syllable adjectives: more careful, more important
- Adjectives ending in -y: change y → i + -er (happy → happier)
- Irregular forms: good → better, bad → worse, far → farther/further
- Always use **than** (not "as") after a comparative when naming the second item
- Never combine more + -er: "more bigger," "more taller" are always wrong
- Indonesian "lebih...dari" maps to comparative + than — but the adjective form changes in English
