## Explanation

English has many words and phrases that express conditional relationships — not just "if." Each alternative has a specific meaning and grammatical behaviour. Using them correctly makes your writing more precise and academic.

---

### Unless = If Not

**Unless** means "if … not" or "except if." It introduces a negative condition using an **affirmative verb** (because the negative is already built into "unless").

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| Unless + affirmative verb | Unless you study, you won't pass. |
| = If + negative verb | If you don't study, you won't pass. |

> **Unless** it rains, we'll have the picnic outside. *(= If it doesn't rain…)*
> She won't agree **unless** you explain the full plan. *(= …if you don't explain…)*

**Critical rule:** Never say "unless you don't" — this is a double negative.

> ❌ Unless you **don't** come, we'll cancel the meeting.
> ✅ Unless you **come**, we'll cancel the meeting. / If you **don't** come, we'll cancel.

---

### Provided That / Providing That = Only If

**Provided (that)** and **providing (that)** mean the condition is the **only requirement** for the result. They are more formal than "if."

> You can borrow my car, **provided that** you return it by 6 p.m.
> The event will go ahead **providing** the weather is acceptable.
> Students may retake the exam, **provided that** they register by Friday.

---

### As Long As = On the Condition That

**As long as** is similar to "provided that" but slightly less formal. It emphasises that the condition must be maintained throughout.

> I'll support you **as long as** you're honest with me.
> You can stay here **as long as** you follow the rules.
> **As long as** you have a valid ticket, you can enter.

---

### On Condition That = Formal Version of Provided That

**On condition that** is more formal and often used in legal or official contexts.

> The contract was signed **on condition that** both parties agreed to the terms.
> The loan will be approved **on condition that** you provide collateral.

---

### Whether Or Not = Regardless of Whether

**Whether or not** shows that the result happens **in both cases** — condition met or not.

> I'll go to the conference **whether or not** you come with me.
> **Whether or not** he apologises, the damage is done.

Compare with "if": "If you come" means the result only happens when the condition is met. "Whether or not you come" means the result happens either way.

---

### Even If = Despite the Condition

**Even if** introduces a condition that does NOT prevent the result. It expresses that the outcome remains the same even under an extreme or unlikely condition.

> **Even if** it rains, we'll still have the match.
> I wouldn't accept that job **even if** they paid me double.
> **Even if** you apologise now, she won't forgive you.

**Contrast: Even if vs Even though**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Even if | Hypothetical condition | Even if it rains (it may or may not rain) |
| Even though | Real/factual concession | Even though it rained, we played (it did rain) |

---

### In Case = As a Precaution

**In case** means "because something might happen" — it is about taking a **precaution**, not stating a condition. Do not use it the same way as "if."

> Take an umbrella **in case** it rains. *(= because it might rain — precaution)*
> I'll write down the address **in case** I forget. *(= in anticipation of forgetting)*

**Contrast: In case vs If**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| If | Condition: do X when Y happens | Take an umbrella if it rains. *(= when/if the rain starts)* |
| In case | Precaution: do X because Y might happen | Take an umbrella in case it rains. *(= before you know if it rains)* |

> ❌ I'll call you **in case** I need help. *(sounds like a precaution — but probably means "if I need help")*
> ✅ I'll call you **if** I need help. *(correct for a conditional)*
> ✅ I'll take my phone **in case** I need to call you. *(correct precaution)*

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Padanan dalam Bahasa Indonesia

| English | Bahasa Indonesia | Contoh |
|---|---|---|
| Unless | Kecuali (jika tidak) | Kamu tidak akan lulus kecuali kamu belajar. |
| Provided that / As long as | Asalkan | Kamu boleh pergi asalkan kamu sudah selesai. |
| Whether or not | Entah … atau tidak | Aku akan pergi entah dia datang atau tidak. |
| Even if | Seandainya pun / Meskipun sekalipun | Aku tidak akan menerima itu seandainya pun gaji dobel. |
| In case | Kalau-kalau / Siapa tahu | Bawa payung kalau-kalau hujan. |

### Perbedaan Penting

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "kecuali" (unless) sering diikuti kata kerja negatif — berbeda dengan bahasa Inggris di mana "unless" sudah mengandung makna negatif dan harus diikuti kata kerja positif.

> ❌ Unless you **don't** bring ID, you can't enter. *(double negative — wrong)*
> ✅ Unless you **bring** ID, you can't enter.

Pelajar Indonesia sering menerjemahkan "kecuali kamu tidak" → "unless you don't" — ini adalah kesalahan umum.

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *Unless you **don't** pay, they will cancel the service.* → ✅ *Unless you **pay**, they will cancel the service.* *Why:* "Unless" already contains the negative — the verb in the unless-clause must be affirmative.

2. ❌ *I'll go **in case** you invite me.* → ✅ *I'll go **if** you invite me.* *Why:* "In case" expresses a precaution taken before an uncertain event, not a condition for an action.

3. ❌ *Take notes **if** you forget the details.* → ✅ *Take notes **in case** you forget the details.* *Why:* "In case" is needed here — you take notes as a precaution against forgetting, not as a response to already forgetting.

4. ❌ ***Even though** it might rain, I'll bring an umbrella.* → ✅ ***Even if** it might rain, I'll bring an umbrella.* / ***Even if** it rains, I'll bring an umbrella.* *Why:* Use "even if" for hypothetical conditions; "even though" is for facts already known to be true.

5. ❌ *You can leave **unless** you finish your work.* → ✅ *You can leave **as long as** you finish your work. / You can't leave unless you finish your work.* *Why:* The original sentence creates a double meaning with "unless." "As long as" fits a positive condition better.

6. ❌ *Provided that you will study, you'll pass.* → ✅ *Provided that you study, you'll pass.* *Why:* Like all conditional clauses, "provided that" takes a present simple verb (not "will") for future meaning.

7. ❌ ***Whether or not** you come, it depends on your schedule.* → ✅ *I'll go **whether or not** you come.* *Why:* "Whether or not" must connect to a result that happens in both cases — it cannot replace "it depends on."

8. ❌ *I'll support you **unless** you work hard.* → ✅ *I'll support you **as long as** you work hard.* *Why:* "Unless you work hard" means "if you don't work hard" — the opposite of the intended meaning. "As long as" sets a positive ongoing condition.

## TOEFL Practice

Conditional alternatives are commonly tested in TOEFL Writing and Reading tasks:
- "Unless" vs "if not" substitution questions
- Choosing the correct connector based on meaning (provided that, as long as, whether or not)
- Distinguishing "even if" (hypothetical) from "even though" (factual)
- Choosing "in case" vs "if" based on whether the action is precautionary or responsive

**Strategy:** Ask yourself: Is the condition positive or negative? Is it a precaution or a response? Is it hypothetical or factual?

## Quick Summary

- **Unless** = if not — use an **affirmative** verb in the unless-clause; never "unless you don't"
- **Provided (that) / As long as** = only if — sets a positive condition that must be met
- **Whether or not** = regardless of whether — the result happens in both cases
- **Even if** = despite the (hypothetical) condition — contrasts with **even though** (factual)
- **In case** = as a precaution — action is taken before the uncertain event, not in response to it
- All conditional clauses use **present simple for future meaning** (no "will" inside the clause)
- Indonesian equivalents: **kecuali** (unless), **asalkan** (as long as), **kalau-kalau** (in case), **seandainya pun** (even if)
- "Unless you don't" is always wrong — a classic interference error from Indonesian
