## Explanation

When we report what someone said, we usually shift the tense one step into the past. This is called **tense backshift**.

### Core tense backshift table

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| Present Simple: *"I work here."* | Past Simple: *She said she **worked** there.* |
| Present Continuous: *"I am studying."* | Past Continuous: *He said he **was studying**.* |
| Present Perfect: *"I have finished."* | Past Perfect: *She said she **had finished**.* |
| Past Simple: *"I called you."* | Past Perfect: *He said he **had called** me.* |
| Past Continuous: *"I was sleeping."* | Past Perfect Continuous: *She said she **had been sleeping**.* |
| **will** → | **would** |
| **can** → | **could** |
| **may** → | **might** |
| **must** → | **had to** |
| **shall** → | **should** |

> **Formula:** Direct verb + one step back in time → Reported verb

### Said vs. told

| | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **said** | No object needed | She *said* (that) she was tired. |
| **told** | Always needs an object | She *told me* (that) she was tired. |

> "She **told** that she was tired." ❌ — *told* always needs a person after it.

### Pronoun changes

Pronouns shift to match the reporter's perspective:

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| I | he / she |
| we | they |
| you | I / we / he / she |
| my | his / her |
| our | their |

*"I love **my** job."* → He said he loved **his** job.

### Time expression changes

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the week before / the previous week |
| next year | the following year |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |

### When NOT to backshift

Backshift is **optional** (and often omitted) when:

1. **The reporting verb is present tense:** She *says* she is busy. (no backshift needed)
2. **The fact is still true:** Galileo said the Earth *orbits* the Sun. (scientific fact)
3. **The situation is still current:** He told me the meeting *is* tomorrow. (still in the future)

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Bahasa Indonesia tidak mengenal tense backshift

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, tidak ada perubahan bentuk kata kerja saat melaporkan ucapan orang lain.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Dia bilang dia **lelah**. | She said she **was tired**. |
| Dia bilang dia **sudah selesai**. | She said she **had finished**. |
| Dia bilang dia **akan pergi**. | She said she **would go**. |

Karena di Bahasa Indonesia kata kerja tidak berubah, pelajar Indonesia sering **tidak melakukan backshift** dalam bahasa Inggris.

### Kesalahan khas pelajar Indonesia

| ❌ Salah (gaya Indonesia) | ✅ Benar (English) |
|---|---|
| She said she **is** tired. | She said she **was** tired. |
| He told that he **will** come. | He told me (that) he **would** come. |
| She said she **can** help. | She said she **could** help. |

### "Bilang" vs "memberitahu"

- **bilang** = *said* (tidak perlu objek)
- **memberitahu** = *told* (selalu perlu objek orang)

*"Dia memberitahu **aku**..."* → *She told **me**...*

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *She said she **is** tired.* → ✅ *She said she **was** tired.*
   *Why:* The reporting verb *said* is past, so the verb in the reported clause must backshift.

2. ❌ *He **told that** he would be late.* → ✅ *He **told me** (that) he would be late.*
   *Why:* *Told* always requires an object (a person); use *said* if there's no object.

3. ❌ *She said she **will** finish it.* → ✅ *She said she **would** finish it.*
   *Why:* *Will* → *would* in reported speech when the main verb is past.

4. ❌ *He said he **can** do it.* → ✅ *He said he **could** do it.*
   *Why:* *Can* → *could* in reported speech.

5. ❌ *She told **that** she had passed.* → ✅ *She **said** (that) she had passed.*
   *Why:* *Told* needs a personal object. Without one, use *said*.

6. ❌ *He said he finished the work **yesterday**.* → ✅ *He said he had finished the work **the day before**.*
   *Why:* Time expressions also shift when reporting past speech.

7. ❌ *She said she **must** leave.* → ✅ *She said she **had to** leave.*
   *Why:* *Must* becomes *had to* in reported speech.

8. ❌ *He said he **had called** me **today**.* → ✅ *He said he had called me **that day**.*
   *Why:* *Today* in direct speech becomes *that day* in reported speech.

## TOEFL Practice

Reported speech questions on the TOEFL test your ability to:
- Choose the correct backshifted verb form
- Identify correct use of *said* vs. *told*
- Recognise correct time-expression shifts

> **Strategy:** Check the reporting verb tense first. If it is past (*said*, *told*, *mentioned*, *explained*), apply backshift. If there is no personal object after the verb, use *said* not *told*.

## Translation Drill

Translate these sentences into English using reported speech. Apply tense backshift and change pronouns / time expressions as needed.

1. Dia bilang dia lelah hari itu. → *She said she **was** tired **that day**.*
2. Mereka bilang mereka akan datang besok. → *They said they **would** come **the next day**.*
3. Dia memberitahu saya bahwa dia sudah selesai. → *He told me that he **had finished**.*
4. Guru itu bilang murid-murid bisa pulang lebih awal. → *The teacher said the students **could** go home early.*
5. Dia bilang dia harus pergi sekarang. → *She said she **had to** go **then**.*

## Quick Summary

- Tense backshift: present → past, past → past perfect, will → would, can → could, may → might, must → had to.
- *Said* needs no object; *told* always needs a personal object (*told me*, *told him*).
- Pronouns shift to match the reporter's perspective: I → he/she, we → they.
- Time expressions shift: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day.
- Do NOT backshift when the reporting verb is present tense or the fact is still true.
- *That* after *said/told* is optional: *She said (that) she was busy.*
- Indonesian has no tense shift in reported speech — watch out for this interference error.
