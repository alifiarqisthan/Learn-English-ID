## Explanation

The **past perfect continuous** is the past-in-the-past in motion. Where the
past perfect says *"an action was complete before another past event,"* the
past perfect continuous says *"an action had been ongoing for a stretch of
time, and then a past event occurred."*

> "She **had been studying** for three hours when I called." — studying was in progress for 3 hours before the call.
> "He looked exhausted — he **had been running** all morning." — visible result of an extended past activity.
> "By the time the rescue team arrived, they **had been waiting** in the cold for six hours." — duration before a past point.

The key idea: **duration + ongoing → cut off by a past event**.

> Compare with [Past Perfect →](/modules/past-perfect) — which focuses on completion, not duration.
> Compare with [Present Perfect Continuous →](/modules/present-perfect-continuous) — same idea, but the reference point is now, not a past moment.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Duration of an action before a past point | I **had been waiting** for 30 minutes when the bus finally came. |
| Evidence/result visible at a past moment | Her eyes were red — she **had been crying**. |
| Repeated action over a period before a past event | He **had been calling** her all week before she answered. |
| Explain the cause of a past state | They were tired because they **had been travelling** for 20 hours. |
| "How long … before/when" questions | How long **had** you **been waiting** before the doctor saw you? |

### Form — had been + Ving

Three parts: **had** + **been** + **V-ing**. Same for all subjects — no had/has split.

#### Positive

```
subject + had been + Ving
```

| Subject | Auxiliary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| All subjects | **had been** | She **had been working** all night. |

> I **had been living** here for five years before I moved. They **had been arguing** for an hour before anyone noticed.

Watch the -ing spelling rules — same as present continuous. See [Present Continuous →](/modules/present-continuous) for the full table.

#### Negative

```
subject + hadn't been + Ving
→ had not been + Ving
```

> She **hadn't been sleeping** well for weeks before she saw the doctor.
> The engine **hadn't been working** properly for months.
> They **hadn't been communicating** since the disagreement.

#### Question

```
Had + subject + been + Ving ?
Wh-word + had + subject + been + Ving ?
```

> **Had** you **been waiting** long when she arrived?
> **How long had** he **been studying** before the exam?
> **Why had** they **been arguing** all evening?

### Stative verbs — same rule applies

Stative verbs (know, want, love, believe, understand, have = own) cannot use continuous form. Use **past perfect simple** instead.

- ❌ I **had been knowing** her for years.
- ✅ I **had known** her for years.

### Past Perfect vs Past Perfect Continuous

This is the key B2 distinction:

| Aspect | Past Perfect | Past Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Completion of an action | Duration of an ongoing action |
| Example | I **had written** the report. | I **had been writing** the report for two hours. |
| Meaning | The report was done (result). | I'd been busy writing (process/duration). |
| Stative verbs | ✅ She had known him for years. | ❌ She had been knowing him. |

**Quick test:** Can you ask "how long before that point?" → use past perfect continuous. Is the result/completion the point? → use past perfect simple.

| Sentence | Which? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| By noon, she **had eaten**. | Past perfect | Focus: eating was complete |
| She **had been eating** for an hour when I arrived. | Past perfect continuous | Focus: duration of eating |
| He **had lived** there his whole life. | **Either** | With state verbs + for/since, both accepted |
| They **had finished** by the time I called. | Past perfect | The result (finished) is the point |
| They **had been working** nonstop for days. | Past perfect continuous | Emphasizes the exhausting ongoing process |

### Time markers

| Marker | Example |
|---|---|
| **for** | She **had been crying** for an hour. |
| **since** | He **had been living** there since childhood. |
| **all day / all morning / all week** | They **had been driving** all night. |
| **when** (the later past event) | I **had been waiting** for 20 minutes **when** she finally arrived. |
| **by the time** | **By the time** the party ended, we **had been dancing** for hours. |
| **before** | She was exhausted **before** the race even started — she **had been training** since 5am. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### No direct equivalent

Like the present perfect continuous, Indonesian has no single grammatical
structure for past perfect continuous. The closest patterns combine duration
words with past context:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Dia sudah menunggu selama dua jam sebelum bis datang. | She **had been waiting** for two hours before the bus came. |
| Saya sudah berlari selama sejam ketika hujan turun. | I **had been running** for an hour when it started to rain. |
| Matanya merah karena dia sudah menangis. | Her eyes were red because she **had been crying**. |
| Mereka sudah bekerja tanpa henti selama berhari-hari sebelum proyek selesai. | They **had been working** nonstop for days before the project was done. |

### The "sudah + selama/sejak + sebelum/ketika" pattern

When Indonesian uses **sudah** (already) + a duration (*selama* / *sejak*) + a past event (*sebelum* / *ketika*), that is a strong signal for past perfect continuous:

| Indonesian clue | English signal |
|---|---|
| sudah … selama … ketika/sebelum | **had been** + Ving + for … when/before |
| sudah … sejak … ketika | **had been** + Ving + since … when |
| sudah berlangsung + masih (past) | **had been** + Ving (still ongoing at that point) |

### Why Indonesian learners collapse this tense

Because there's no direct structure, learners tend to use:

1. **Simple past** — "She waited for two hours before the bus came." (loses the duration emphasis)
2. **Past continuous** — "She was waiting for two hours..." (wrong — ignores the past reference point)
3. **Past perfect simple** — "She had waited for two hours..." (acceptable, but loses emphasis on the ongoing process)

### Side-by-side: all four past tenses

| Indonesian | English tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Saya makan (kemarin) | Simple Past | I **ate** yesterday. |
| Saya **sedang** makan ketika dia datang | Past Continuous | I **was eating** when she came. |
| Saya **sudah** makan sebelum dia datang | Past Perfect | I **had eaten** before she came. |
| Saya **sudah makan selama** 2 jam ketika dia datang | Past Perfect Continuous | I **had been eating** for 2 hours when she came. |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Forgetting "been"**
- ❌ She **had working** all night.
- ❌ They **had studying** for hours.
- ✅ She **had been working** all night.
- ✅ They **had been studying** for hours.

  *Why it happens:* three-word structure (had + been + Ving) is unusual in Indonesian. Learners drop "been" just as they do in present perfect continuous.

**2. Using past continuous instead of past perfect continuous**
- ❌ She **was crying** for an hour before he apologised.
- ✅ She **had been crying** for an hour before he apologised.

  *Why it happens:* past continuous (*was crying*) doesn't anchor the duration to the "before a past event" timeline. When you need to show duration *before* a specific past point, use past perfect continuous.

**3. Using present perfect continuous instead of past perfect continuous**
- ❌ I **have been waiting** for an hour when she finally arrived.
- ✅ I **had been waiting** for an hour when she finally arrived.

  *Why it happens:* "have been" is present-anchored. "Had been" is past-anchored. The later event (*arrived*) is already in the simple past, so the longer action must use past perfect continuous.

**4. Using stative verbs in continuous form**
- ❌ He **had been knowing** her for years when they married.
- ✅ He **had known** her for years when they married.

**5. Using "did" or confusing auxiliaries**
- ❌ She **didn't been working** when I called.
- ✅ She **hadn't been working** when I called.

**6. Wrong -ing spelling**
- ❌ She had been **runing**, he had been **writeing**
- ✅ She had been **running** (CVC double), he had been **writing** (drop -e)

## Quick Summary

- Form: **had been + Ving** (three parts — same for all subjects, never drop "been").
- Use for: **duration of an ongoing action before a past point**. Almost always with *for*, *since*, or *when/by the time*.
- Focus is on **process and duration**, not completion. Past perfect simple = completion.
- Not for stative verbs (know, want, love) → use past perfect simple: *had known*, *had wanted*.
- Indonesian *sudah…selama…ketika/sebelum* = past perfect continuous signal.
- Key question form: **How long had + subject + been + Ving + before/when…?**
- Don't confuse with past continuous (*was/were + Ving*) — past perfect continuous has *had been*, anchoring the duration to a past reference point.
