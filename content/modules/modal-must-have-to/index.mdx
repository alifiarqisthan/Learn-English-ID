## Explanation

**Must** and **have to** both express obligation, but they differ in source: **must** comes from the speaker's authority or strong feeling; **have to** comes from external rules or circumstances. **Must** is also used for logical deduction.

### Obligation — must vs have to

| Structure | Source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| must + V1 | Speaker's authority / strong personal feeling | You **must** read this — it's brilliant. |
| have to + V1 | External rule, circumstance, or necessity | I **have to** wear a uniform at work. |
| don't have to + V1 | No obligation (NOT "must not") | You **don't have to** come if you're tired. |
| must not + V1 | Prohibition (something is forbidden) | You **must not** use your phone during the exam. |

> **Critical distinction**: "Don't have to" = not necessary / optional. "Must not" = forbidden.

### Have to — tense flexibility

"Must" has no past or future form. Use "have to" for those tenses:

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | have/has to + V1 | She **has to** submit the form today. |
| Past | had to + V1 | I **had to** redo the entire report. |
| Future | will have to + V1 | They **will have to** retake the exam. |
| Present perfect | have had to + V1 | We **have had to** reschedule twice. |

### Must — logical deduction (positive)

When the evidence strongly points to a conclusion:

```
must + be / V1
```

| Evidence | Deduction |
|---|---|
| Her car is outside. | She **must be** home. |
| He's been working since 5am. | He **must be** exhausted. |
| They've sold 10 million copies. | It **must be** a great book. |

### Must not — prohibition vs don't have to

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| You **must not** park here. | Parking is **forbidden**. |
| You **don't have to** park here. | You can park elsewhere — it's **optional**. |

This is one of the most common errors for Indonesian learners.

### Needn't (British English)

"Needn't + V1" = "don't have to" — no obligation. Common in British English:

> You **needn't** worry — everything is arranged.
> She **needn't** submit both copies.

### Summary table

| Function | Modal |
|---|---|
| Strong personal obligation | must |
| External obligation (all tenses) | have to / had to / will have to |
| No obligation (optional) | don't have to / needn't |
| Prohibition (forbidden) | must not |
| Logical deduction (positive) | must |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Harus" — the Indonesian bridge

Indonesian uses **harus** for both obligation and expectation, without distinguishing who imposes the obligation.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Kamu harus datang. *(personal request)* | You **must** come. |
| Kamu harus memakai seragam. *(external rule)* | You **have to** wear a uniform. |
| Kamu tidak perlu datang. | You **don't have to** come. |
| Kamu tidak boleh merokok di sini. | You **must not** smoke here. |

### The "tidak harus" → "must not" interference error

*Tidak harus* in Indonesian means "not obligatory" (= don't have to), not "forbidden". Students translate it as "must not":

- ❌ You must not bring a dictionary. *(sounds like it's banned)*
- ✅ You **don't have to** bring a dictionary. *(it's optional)*

### "Harus" for deduction

Indonesian *pasti* or *sepertinya* is used for deductions, not *harus*. But English uses **must**:

- *Dia pasti kelelahan.* → He **must be** exhausted.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "must not" when you mean "don't have to"**
- ❌ You must not bring a dictionary — it's optional.
- ✅ You **don't have to** bring a dictionary.
- *Why:* "Must not" = prohibition. "Don't have to" = no obligation.

**2. Using "must" for past obligation**
- ❌ I must go to the hospital yesterday.
- ✅ I **had to** go to the hospital yesterday.
- *Why:* "Must" has no past tense. Use "had to" for past obligation.

**3. Using "must" for future obligation**
- ❌ She must submit the form next week.
- ✅ She **will have to** submit the form next week. *(if emphasising external requirement)*
- *Why:* For future external obligation, "will have to" is more precise.

**4. Confusing "have to" with "has to"**
- ❌ She have to attend all classes.
- ✅ She **has to** attend all classes.
- *Why:* Third person singular → "has to", not "have to".

**5. Adding "to" after "must"**
- ❌ You must to register before Friday.
- ✅ You **must** register before Friday.
- *Why:* Modal verbs are followed directly by V1 — no "to".

**6. Using "must" for logical deduction in the negative (impossibility)**
- ❌ He must not know the answer — he looks confused.
- ✅ He **can't** know the answer. / He **must not** know… *(only in formal contexts)*
- *Why:* "Can't" expresses negative logical deduction (impossibility). "Must not" = prohibition.

**7. "Needn't" + "to"**
- ❌ You needn't to hurry.
- ✅ You **needn't** hurry.
- *Why:* "Needn't" is a modal — followed directly by V1, never "to".

## Quick Summary

- **Must** = strong personal obligation or logical deduction
- **Have to** = external obligation; works in all tenses (had to, will have to)
- **Must not** = prohibition (forbidden) — NOT the same as "don't have to"
- **Don't have to** = no obligation / optional
- "Must" has no past form → use **had to** for past obligation
- "Must" has no future form → use **will have to** for future external obligation
- Logical deduction: *must be* — "She must be tired" (strong evidence → conclusion)
- Third person singular: **has to** (not "have to")
- Modals + V1: never "must to go", "have to went"
