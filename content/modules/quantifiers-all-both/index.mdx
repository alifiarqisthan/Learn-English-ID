## Explanation

**All, both, each,** and **every** are **universal quantifiers** — they refer to the total number of a group. But they differ in how they view the group and what grammar they require.

### All

**All** refers to the **complete group** as a whole — without exception.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| all + plural countable noun | **All** students must register. |
| all + the + plural noun | **All the** students passed. |
| all + uncountable noun | **All** information will be kept confidential. |
| all + of + the + noun | **All of the** results were positive. |

**All** takes a **plural verb** when used with a plural noun:
> **All the** students **are** here. *(plural)*

**All** with uncountable nouns:
> **All** the equipment **was** returned. *(uncountable → singular verb)*

#### "All" in mid-position

"All" can appear between the auxiliary and the main verb, or after "be":
> They **all passed** the exam.
> The students **all** received certificates.
> We **all** need to sign the form.

### Both

**Both** refers to **exactly two** items, treating them as a complete pair.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| both + plural noun | **Both** doors are locked. |
| both + the + plural noun | **Both the** candidates were qualified. |
| both + of + the + plural noun | **Both of the** proposals were rejected. |
| subject + both + verb | **They both** agreed. |

**Both** always takes a **plural verb**:
> **Both** candidates **are** impressive. ✅
> **Both** candidates **is** impressive. ❌

#### "Both" vs. "the two"

"Both" suggests a **complete pair** — often where you expect two. "The two" is more neutral:
> **Both** parents attended. *(natural — there are normally two parents)*
> **The two** proposals were rejected. *(neutral — referring to two specific ones)*

### Each

**Each** refers to members of a group **individually, one by one**. It always uses a **singular noun and singular verb**.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| each + singular noun | **Each** student submitted a form. |
| each + of + the + plural noun | **Each of the** students submitted a form. |
| subject + each | **They each** submitted a form. |

> **Each** student **needs** to sign. *(singular)*
> **Each** country **has** its own laws.

"Each" emphasises individual distribution:
> She gave **each** student a copy. *(one copy to every individual)*

### Every

**Every** also refers to all members individually, but it emphasises **universality** — no exception. It also uses a **singular noun and singular verb**.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| every + singular noun | **Every** student must attend. |
| every + number + plural noun | **every** five minutes, **every** three days |

> **Every** country **has** a different culture.
> The buses run **every** ten minutes.

#### "Every" cannot be followed by "of"

- ❌ **Every of** the students passed.
- ✅ **Every** student passed. / **Each of** the students passed.

### Each vs. Every — the key difference

| Feature | Each | Every |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Individual items, one by one | All without exception (universal) |
| Group size | Usually small / specific | Any size, especially large |
| Can use "of"? | Yes: **each of** the students | No: ❌ **every of** |
| Interchangeable? | Often yes, but not always | Often yes, but see rules below |

**Not interchangeable:**
- "**Every** time" is a fixed phrase (not "each time" in common usage)
- "**Each** other" is fixed *(they helped each other)*
- "**Every** other day" = alternate days *(not "each other day")*
- "**Each**" can refer to two items; "every" usually implies three or more

> She called **every** doctor in the city. *(universal — all without exception)*
> She spoke to **each** doctor individually. *(individual attention)*

### Position of all / both / each with pronouns

These quantifiers can appear **after a pronoun subject** or **after the auxiliary**:

> **They all** worked hard. *(after subject pronoun)*
> **They both** agreed. *(after subject pronoun)*
> **They each** received a prize. *(after subject pronoun)*
> We have **all** finished. *(after auxiliary)*
> We have **both** agreed. *(after auxiliary)*

### "Whole" vs. "all"

| Whole | All |
|---|---|
| the whole + singular noun | all + plural / uncountable |
| **the whole day** | **all day** (fixed expression) |
| **the whole team** | **all the team** / **the whole team** |
| **the whole truth** | **all the truth** |

> She worked **the whole day** / **all day**.
> **The whole team** / **All the team** agreed.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian equivalents

| English | Bahasa Indonesia |
|---|---|
| all | semua / seluruh |
| both | keduanya / kedua |
| each | masing-masing / tiap |
| every | setiap / tiap |

### The singular/plural verb trap

Indonesian does not conjugate verbs for number, so "each student **are**..." sounds natural from an Indonesian perspective. But English requires:

| Quantifier | Verb | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| all (plural noun) | plural verb | All students **are** here. |
| both | plural verb | Both candidates **were** qualified. |
| each | singular verb | Each student **needs** to sign. |
| every | singular verb | Every country **has** its own laws. |

### "Each of" — Indonesian learners often omit "the"

*Masing-masing dari mahasiswa* → "each of the students" — the "the" is required:
- ❌ Each of students submitted a form.
- ✅ Each of **the** students submitted a form.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "every of" — not grammatical**
- ❌ Every of the students passed.
- ✅ Every student passed. / Each of the students passed.

**2. Plural verb with "each" or "every"**
- ❌ Each student **were** given a certificate.
- ✅ Each student **was** given a certificate.

**3. Using "both" for more than two items**
- ❌ **Both** three candidates were qualified.
- ✅ **All three** candidates were qualified.

**4. Using "all" for exactly two items**
- ❌ **All** of her two brothers attended.
- ✅ **Both** of her brothers attended.

**5. Omitting "the" after "each of"**
- ❌ Each of students received feedback.
- ✅ Each of **the** students received feedback.

**6. "Every" with two items**
- ❌ **Every** parent was happy. *(implies more than two — for exactly two, use "both")*
- More natural: **Both** parents were happy.

**7. "All of them is" — wrong verb**
- ❌ All of them **is** ready.
- ✅ All of them **are** ready. *(plural → plural verb)*

## Quick Summary

- **All** = entire group. Plural noun → plural verb. Uncountable → singular verb.
- **Both** = exactly two. Always plural verb.
- **Each** = one by one, individually. Singular noun + singular verb. Can use "each of the".
- **Every** = universal, no exception. Singular noun + singular verb. Cannot use "every of".
- **Each vs. Every**: each = individual focus | every = universal/collective. "Each other" and "every other day" are fixed.
- After pronoun subjects: they **all/both/each** + verb.
- "Each of **the**" — always include "the".
