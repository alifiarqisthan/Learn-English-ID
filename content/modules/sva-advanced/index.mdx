## Explanation

This module covers the most advanced subject-verb agreement patterns: **correlative conjunctions**, **inverted sentences**, **fractions and percentages**, and **agreement with gerunds and infinitives**.

### Advanced Case 1 — Correlative conjunctions

**Correlative conjunctions** are pairs that work together: *both…and*, *either…or*, *neither…nor*, *not only…but also*, *whether…or*.

#### Both…and — always plural

> **Both** the professor **and** the students **were** surprised. *(plural — always)*
> **Both** research **and** experience **are** needed.

"Both…and" always creates a compound subject → **always plural**.

#### Either…or / Neither…nor — proximity rule

The verb agrees with the **subject closest** to it:

> **Either** the manager **or** the employees **are** responsible. *(employees = plural, nearest)*
> **Either** the employees **or** the manager **is** responsible. *(manager = singular, nearest)*
> **Neither** the students **nor** the professor **was** aware. *(professor = singular, nearest)*
> **Neither** the professor **nor** the students **were** aware. *(students = plural, nearest)*

#### Not only…but also — proximity rule

> **Not only** the students **but also** the professor **was** affected. *(professor = singular, nearest)*
> **Not only** the professor **but also** the students **were** affected. *(students = plural, nearest)*

#### Whether…or — proximity rule (same)

> **Whether** the CEO **or** the board members **are** present is irrelevant. *(board members = plural, nearest)*

### Advanced Case 2 — Inverted sentences

In inverted sentences (where the verb comes before the subject), find the **real subject** after the verb and make the verb agree with it.

#### "Here/There" at the beginning

> **Here is** the report you requested. *(report = singular)*
> **Here are** the results of the study. *(results = plural)*
> **There appears** to be a problem. *(problem = singular)*
> **There seem** to be several errors. *(errors = plural)*

#### Prepositional phrase or adjective at the beginning (fronted)

> **Among the participants was** a well-known researcher. *(researcher = singular)*
> **On the wall hang** three large paintings. *(paintings = plural)*
> **Central to the argument is** the assumption that... *(assumption = singular)*

#### Questions

> **Is** the data accurate? *(data = singular in formal use)*
> **Are** the results consistent? *(results = plural)*

### Advanced Case 3 — Gerunds and infinitives as subjects

When a **gerund** (-ing form) or **infinitive** (to + verb) functions as the subject, the verb is always **singular**:

> **Reading** is a good habit.
> **Analysing the data** takes time.
> **To succeed** requires dedication.
> **Swimming every day** improves cardiovascular health.

Even if the gerund phrase sounds complex or "plural", the verb is singular:
> **Collecting samples from multiple sites** **was** the most challenging part. *(singular)*

### Advanced Case 4 — Fractions, percentages, and proportions

The verb agrees with the **noun after "of"**:

```
Fraction/percentage/proportion + of + noun → verb agrees with the noun after "of"
```

| Phrase | Noun after "of" | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Half of the students | students (plural) | **are** |
| Half of the water | water (uncountable) | **is** |
| 30% of the budget | budget (singular/uncountable) | **was** |
| 60% of the respondents | respondents (plural) | **were** |
| The majority of the data | data (treated as uncountable in formal use) | **is** |
| The majority of voters | voters (plural) | **are** |

> **Half of the students have** already registered. *(students = plural)*
> **Half of the water has** evaporated. *(water = uncountable → singular)*
> **Most of the evidence points** to one conclusion. *(evidence = uncountable)*
> **Most of the participants were** satisfied. *(participants = plural)*

### Advanced Case 5 — "The number" vs. "A number"

| Phrase | Meaning | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| **The number** of + plural noun | the count of | **singular** |
| **A number** of + plural noun | many | **plural** |

> **The number** of applications **has** increased. *(the count = singular)*
> **A number** of problems **have** emerged. *(many problems = plural)*

### Advanced Case 6 — Expressions of time, money, distance as single units

When a sum, period, or measurement is viewed as a **single unit**, it takes a **singular verb**:

> **Five years** **is** a long time. *(one period)*
> **One hundred dollars** **is** not enough. *(one sum)*
> **Three kilometres** **is** a short commute. *(one distance)*

But when seen as individual items:
> **Five years** **have** passed since we last spoke. *(individual years)*

### Advanced Case 7 — "More than one"

> **More than one** student **has** failed. *(looks plural but is singular!)*
> **More than one** issue **needs** to be addressed.

"More than one" always takes a **singular** noun and **singular** verb despite its logical meaning.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Correlative conjunctions — no proximity rule in Indonesian

Indonesian *baik...maupun* (both...and), *entah...atau* (either...or) do not require any verb conjugation. The proximity rule in English has no Indonesian equivalent — it must be learned as an English-specific rule.

| Bahasa Indonesia | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Baik manajer maupun karyawan bertanggung jawab. | Both the manager and employees **is** responsible. ❌ | Both the manager and employees **are** responsible. ✅ |
| Entah direktur atau anggota dewan yang hadir... | Either the director or the board members **is** present... ❌ | Either the director or the board members **are** present. ✅ *(members = nearest, plural)* |

### Inverted sentences — uncommon structure in Indonesian

Indonesian uses inversion less systematically. The key for English is to always find the true subject and match the verb to it, regardless of sentence order.

### Fractions — Indonesian doesn't mark agreement

*Setengah dari siswa sudah mendaftar* — Indonesian doesn't change the verb. In English, agreement depends on what follows "of":
- Half of the students **have** (plural)
- Half of the water **has** (uncountable)

## Common Mistakes

**1. "Both…and" with singular verb**
- ❌ Both the manager and the director **was** present.
- ✅ Both the manager and the director **were** present.

**2. "Either…or" — not applying proximity rule**
- ❌ Either the students or the professor **are** responsible.
- ✅ Either the students or the professor **is** responsible. *(professor = nearest = singular)*

**3. Inverted sentence — verb matches wrong noun**
- ❌ Here **is** the results of the study.
- ✅ Here **are** the results of the study.

**4. Gerund subject with plural verb**
- ❌ Collecting data from multiple sites **take** weeks.
- ✅ Collecting data from multiple sites **takes** weeks.

**5. Fraction/percentage — verb matches fraction, not noun after "of"**
- ❌ Sixty percent of the respondents **was** satisfied.
- ✅ Sixty percent of the respondents **were** satisfied. *(respondents = plural)*

**6. "The number of" with plural verb**
- ❌ The number of applications **have** doubled.
- ✅ The number of applications **has** doubled.

**7. "A number of" with singular verb**
- ❌ A number of issues **has** been identified.
- ✅ A number of issues **have** been identified.

**8. "More than one" with plural verb**
- ❌ More than one student **have** failed.
- ✅ More than one student **has** failed.

**9. Time/money as a single unit with plural verb**
- ❌ Three hours **are** not enough time.
- ✅ Three hours **is** not enough time. *(viewed as one period)*

## Quick Summary

- **Both…and** → always **plural**.
- **Either…or / Neither…nor / Not only…but also** → **proximity rule** (nearest subject determines verb).
- **Inverted sentences**: find the real subject after the verb → match verb to it.
- **Gerund / infinitive subjects** → always **singular**.
- **Fractions/percentages + of + noun** → verb agrees with the **noun after "of"**.
- **"The number of"** → singular | **"A number of"** → plural.
- **"More than one"** + singular noun → **singular** verb.
- **Time/money/distance as a unit** → **singular** verb.
