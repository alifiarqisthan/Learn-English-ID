## Explanation

A **time clause** is an adverb clause that tells **when** the action in the main clause happens. It is introduced by a **time subordinating conjunction** and answers the question "When?"

> **Before she left**, she turned off all the lights.
> She was cooking **while he was reading**.
> **As soon as the results came in**, the team celebrated.

### Time conjunctions and their meanings

| Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **when** | at the time that | **When** the alarm rang, she woke up. |
| **while** | during the same time (parallel actions) | She cooked **while** he cleaned. |
| **as** | at the same moment (often literary) | **As** the sun set, the city grew quiet. |
| **before** | earlier than | **Before** you submit, proofread your work. |
| **after** | later than | **After** she graduated, she moved abroad. |
| **until / till** | up to the point when | They waited **until** the rain stopped. |
| **since** | from a past point to now | It has been three years **since** she graduated. |
| **once** | as soon as / after (completion) | **Once** you understand the rule, it becomes easy. |
| **as soon as** | immediately when | **As soon as** she arrived, the meeting began. |
| **by the time** | before or at the latest point | **By the time** he arrived, everyone had left. |
| **whenever** | every time | **Whenever** it rains, she feels calm. |

### Comma rule for time clauses

Same as all adverb clauses:
- Time clause **first** → **comma** after it.
- Time clause **second** → **no comma**.

> **When she arrived,** the meeting started. *(time clause first — comma)*
> The meeting started **when she arrived**. *(time clause second — no comma)*

### Critical tense rule — present tense for future time

When a time clause refers to a **future event**, use **present tense** in the time clause — NOT will.

```
After / when / before / once / as soon as + present tense → future main clause
```

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| When she **will arrive**, we will begin. | When she **arrives**, we will begin. |
| After he **will finish**, he'll call you. | After he **finishes**, he'll call you. |
| As soon as the results **will be ready**... | As soon as the results **are ready**... |

This rule applies even when the action clearly hasn't happened yet. The present tense in the time clause signals future time.

### "When" vs. "while"

| When | While |
|---|---|
| Points in time / sequential events | Parallel / simultaneous ongoing actions |
| **When** the phone rang, she answered it. *(phone rings, then she answers — sequence)* | **While** she was cooking, he was reading. *(both happening at the same time)* |
| Use with simple tenses | Often with continuous tenses |

> **When** I was young, I loved swimming. *(at that period of life)*
> **While** I was swimming, it started to rain. *(parallel action)*

### "By the time" — completion before a reference point

**By the time** + clause means one action will be **completed before or at** another event:

> **By the time** you read this, I will have left. *(I will have already left)*
> **By the time** she arrived, they had already eaten. *(they finished before she arrived)*

This often pairs with **future perfect** or **past perfect**:
- Future context: *by the time + present → future perfect (will have done)*
- Past context: *by the time + past simple → past perfect (had done)*

### "Since" for duration

**Since** in time clauses introduces the starting point of a period that continues to now. It pairs with **perfect tenses**:

> I have lived here **since** I moved from Bandung. *(present perfect — still living here)*
> She had been working there **since** she graduated. *(past perfect — duration in past)*

### The future tense trap

In Indonesian, future time clauses can use future markers (*akan*):
> *Ketika dia **akan** tiba, kita akan mulai.* *(grammatical in Indonesian)*

In English, the time clause uses **present tense**:
> When she **arrives** (NOT *will arrive*), we will begin.

This is the **most common time clause error** for Indonesian speakers in TOEFL writing.

### "Sementara" and "sambil" — simultaneous actions

Indonesian *sementara* / *sambil* (while doing) maps to English *while*, but note:

- *Dia memasak **sementara** dia menonton TV.* → She cooked **while** she watched TV.
- *Dia belajar **sambil** mendengarkan musik.* → She studied **while listening** to music. *(reduced to -ing phrase)*

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "will" in a time clause**
- ❌ When she **will arrive**, the meeting will start.
- ✅ When she **arrives**, the meeting will start.

**2. Confusing "when" and "while"**
- ❌ **While** the phone rang, she answered it.
- ✅ **When** the phone rang, she answered it. *(phone ringing is a point in time, not an ongoing action)*

**3. Missing comma when time clause is first**
- ❌ Before she left she turned off the lights.
- ✅ Before she left**,** she turned off the lights.

**4. Using "since" with simple past (not perfect)**
- ❌ I live here **since** I was born.
- ✅ I **have lived** here since I was born. *(present perfect — ongoing)*

**5. Wrong tense with "by the time"**
- ❌ By the time she arrived, they **already ate**.
- ✅ By the time she arrived, they **had already eaten**. *(past perfect)*

**6. "Until" vs. "by"**
- ❌ She worked **until** she finished the project. *(unclear — could be correct but often confused)*
- ✅ She worked **until** the deadline. *(up to a point — continuous action reaching a limit)*
- ✅ She finished **by** the deadline. *(completed before a point — not "until" for completion)*

**7. "Once" confused with "when"**
- "Once" implies **completion**: *Once you understand this, it gets easy.* *(after understanding is achieved)*
- "When" is neutral: *When she arrives, we'll start.* *(at that moment)*

## Quick Summary

- Time clauses answer **when?** — introduced by when, while, before, after, until, since, once, as soon as, by the time, whenever.
- **Comma rule**: time clause first → comma. Time clause second → no comma.
- **CRITICAL**: Time clause with future meaning → use **present tense**, NOT will.
- **"When"** = sequential / point in time. **"While"** = simultaneous / ongoing.
- **"By the time"** = completion before a reference → pairs with past perfect or future perfect.
- **"Since"** = starting point of a period → pairs with present perfect or past perfect.
- **"Once"** = after completion of the first action.
