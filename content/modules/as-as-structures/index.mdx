## Explanation

The **as…as** structure is used to compare two things that are **equal** in some quality, or to show they are **not equal**. It is different from comparatives (-er/more), which show one item is greater than another.

### Basic Structure: Equality

> **as + adjective/adverb + as**

- She is **as tall as** her father. (They are the same height.)
- This exam is **as difficult as** the last one.
- He speaks English **as fluently as** a native speaker.

### Negative: Inequality (Less Than)

> **not as + adjective/adverb + as**

- This hotel is **not as expensive as** the one downtown.
- He didn't finish **as quickly as** his partner.
- The second film was **not as interesting as** the first.

> **Note:** "not as…as" means the first item has **less** of the quality. It is equivalent to "less…than":
> - *This is not as tall as that.* = *This is less tall than that.*

### Multipliers: Twice/Three Times As…As

> **twice / three times / half + as + adjective/adverb + as**

- This apartment is **twice as expensive as** mine.
- The journey took **three times as long as** expected.
- She earns **half as much as** her manager.

### As Much As / As Many As (with Nouns)

> **as much + uncountable noun + as** / **as many + countable noun + as**

- He drinks **as much coffee as** his colleagues do.
- She has read **as many books as** the librarian.
- We don't have **as much time as** we need.

### Modifiers: Just As / Almost As / Nearly As / Not Quite As

These words modify the degree of equality:

| Modifier | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **just as** | completely equal | She is **just as** capable as her brother. |
| **almost as / nearly as** | slightly less | He is **almost as** tall as his father. |
| **not quite as** | slightly less (formal) | The sequel was **not quite as** good as the original. |

### Structure Summary

| Meaning | Structure |
|---|---|
| Equal | as + adj/adv + as |
| Not equal (less) | not as + adj/adv + as |
| Double | twice as + adj + as |
| Slight difference | almost/nearly as + adj + as |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Perbedaan dengan Bahasa Indonesia

Bahasa Indonesia menggunakan **"sama...dengan"** atau **"se- + kata sifat"** untuk menyatakan kesetaraan:
- *Dia setinggi ayahnya.* → She is **as tall as** her father.
- *Ujian ini sama sulitnya dengan yang kemarin.* → This exam is **as difficult as** yesterday's.

Untuk ketidaksetaraan:
- *Dia tidak setinggi kakaknya.* → She is **not as tall as** her brother.
- *Hotel ini tidak semahal yang di pusat kota.* → This hotel is **not as expensive as** the one downtown.

### Tabel Kontras

| Bahasa Indonesia | ❌ Kesalahan umum | ✅ Inggris yang benar |
|---|---|---|
| sama tingginya dengan | as tall than | **as tall as** |
| tidak secepat | not as fast than | **not as fast as** |
| dua kali lebih mahal dari | two times more expensive as | **twice as expensive as** |
| sama banyaknya dengan | as much than | **as much as** |

### Interferensi Khas

Dua kesalahan paling umum dari pelajar Indonesia:
1. Menggunakan "**than**" setelah "as…" → "as tall **than**" (salah). Harus "as tall **as**."
2. Menggunakan "**more**" dengan struktur as…as → "as **more** expensive as" (salah). Tidak ada "more" dalam struktur ini.

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *She is as tall than her mother.* → ✅ *She is as tall **as** her mother.* *Why:* The as…as structure uses "as" on both sides, never "than."

2. ❌ *This problem is as more difficult as the last one.* → ✅ *This problem is **as difficult as** the last one.* *Why:* Do not add "more" inside an as…as structure.

3. ❌ *He doesn't work as hardly as his boss.* → ✅ *He doesn't work as **hard** as his boss.* *Why:* "Hard" is already an adverb — do not add -ly.

4. ❌ *The new version is not so good as the old one.* → ✅ *The new version is **not as good as** the old one.* *Why:* "Not so…as" is a dated/formal variant; "not as…as" is standard modern English.

5. ❌ *He earns two times as much money than me.* → ✅ *He earns **twice as much money as** me.* *Why:* Multiplier as…as uses "as" not "than"; "two times" is usually expressed as "twice."

6. ❌ *She has as many informations as him.* → ✅ *She has as much **information** as him.* *Why:* "Information" is uncountable — use "as much," not "as many."

7. ❌ *The film was not as interesting than I expected.* → ✅ *The film was not as interesting **as** I expected.* *Why:* "Not as…as" — both words are "as."

8. ❌ *This road is as twice as long as that one.* → ✅ *This road is **twice as long as** that one.* *Why:* "Twice" comes before "as," not between the two "as" words.

## TOEFL Practice

As…as structures appear in TOEFL reading and sentence completion. Watch for:
- Both sides of the structure being "as" (not "as…than")
- Multiplier placement: twice/three times comes before "as"
- Countable vs uncountable: "as many" vs "as much"
- Modifiers: "almost as," "just as," "not quite as"

## Quick Summary

- Equality: **as + adjective/adverb + as** — both words are "as"
- Inequality: **not as + adjective/adverb + as** — means the first item has less
- Multipliers: **twice / three times as + adjective + as**
- With nouns: **as much** (uncountable) or **as many** (countable) + noun + as
- Modifiers: just as (equal), almost/nearly as (slightly less), not quite as (formal, slightly less)
- Never use "than" inside an as…as structure — "as tall **than**" is always wrong
- Never add "more" inside: "as more expensive as" is wrong
- Indonesian "sama…dengan" or "se-…" maps directly to "as…as"
