## Explanation

Beyond simple tenses, passive voice works across **perfect** and **continuous** tenses. Each adds a layer of time or aspect meaning on top of the basic passive formula.

### Master formula reminder

**Passive = to be (conjugated to tense) + V3**

The trick for complex tenses: the "to be" part itself changes tense/aspect, and V3 stays fixed.

### Present Continuous Passive

```
am / is / are + being + V3
```

Meaning: the action is happening **right now** and the subject receives it.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They **are reviewing** the proposal. | The proposal **is being reviewed**. |
| Engineers **are testing** the new system. | The new system **is being tested**. |
| Workers **are repairing** the road. | The road **is being repaired**. |

> "Is being reviewed" = right now, someone is reviewing it.

### Past Continuous Passive

```
was / were + being + V3
```

Meaning: the action was ongoing at a past moment.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They **were building** the bridge when the earthquake struck. | The bridge **was being built** when the earthquake struck. |
| Someone **was following** him. | He **was being followed**. |

### Present Perfect Passive

```
has / have + been + V3
```

Meaning: completed at some unspecified point before now; relevant to the present.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The committee **has approved** the plan. | The plan **has been approved**. |
| Someone **has hacked** the system. | The system **has been hacked**. |
| They **have cancelled** the meeting. | The meeting **has been cancelled**. |

> "Has been approved" = at some point before now, approval happened.

### Past Perfect Passive

```
had + been + V3
```

Meaning: completed before another past event.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They **had collected** all the data before analysis began. | All the data **had been collected** before analysis began. |
| Someone **had already signed** the contract. | The contract **had already been signed**. |

### Summary table

| Tense | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present Continuous | is/are + **being** + V3 | The road **is being repaired**. |
| Past Continuous | was/were + **being** + V3 | He **was being followed**. |
| Present Perfect | has/have + **been** + V3 | The plan **has been approved**. |
| Past Perfect | had + **been** + V3 | The data **had been collected**. |
| Future Perfect | will have + **been** + V3 | It **will have been resolved** by then. |

### The "being" vs "been" distinction — the most common error

| Word | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **being** | continuous tenses | The report **is being** written. *(in progress now)* |
| **been** | perfect tenses | The report **has been** written. *(completed)* |

- ❌ The proposal **is been** reviewed. *(wrong — mixing continuous and perfect)*
- ✅ The proposal **is being** reviewed. *(continuous — happening now)*
- ✅ The proposal **has been** reviewed. *(perfect — already done)*

## Bahasa Indonesia

### No continuous passive distinction in Indonesian

Indonesian passive (di- prefix) does not mark whether the action is ongoing or completed the way English does. *Laporan itu sedang ditinjau* (the report is being reviewed) and *Laporan itu sudah ditinjau* (the report has been reviewed) both use di- but add aspect markers *sedang* (ongoing) and *sudah* (completed).

In English, the tense and the auxiliary carry this meaning:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Proposal itu **sedang** ditinjau. | The proposal **is being** reviewed. *(continuous)* |
| Proposal itu **sudah** disetujui. | The proposal **has been** approved. *(perfect)* |
| Jembatan itu **sedang** dibangun ketika gempa terjadi. | The bridge **was being** built when the earthquake struck. |

### "Sudah" ≠ always past perfect

Indonesian *sudah* can map to either present perfect ("has been") or past perfect ("had been") depending on context. English requires the correct auxiliary:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Kontrak **sudah** ditandatangani. | The contract **has been** signed. *(present perfect — relevant now)* |
| Kontrak **sudah** ditandatangani sebelum dia tiba. | The contract **had been** signed before she arrived. *(past perfect — before another past event)* |

## Common Mistakes

**1. "Is been" — mixing continuous and perfect**
- ❌ The road **is been** repaired.
- ✅ The road **is being** repaired. *(ongoing now)*
- ✅ The road **has been** repaired. *(completed)*

**2. Omitting "being" in continuous passive**
- ❌ The proposal **is reviewed** right now.
- ✅ The proposal **is being** reviewed right now.

**3. Using "have been" when past perfect is needed**
- ❌ All samples **have been** collected before the experiment started.
- ✅ All samples **had been** collected before the experiment started.

**4. Wrong agreement in present perfect passive**
- ❌ The documents **has been** submitted.
- ✅ The documents **have been** submitted. *(plural → have)*

**5. Adding "being" to a perfect tense**
- ❌ The plan **has being** approved.
- ✅ The plan **has been** approved.

**6. Confusing "was being" (past continuous) with "was" (past simple)**
- "The road **was repaired** last week." *(past simple — completed action)*
- "The road **was being repaired** when the storm hit." *(past continuous — ongoing at that moment)*

## Quick Summary

- Present continuous passive: **is/are + being + V3** — *The road is being repaired.*
- Past continuous passive: **was/were + being + V3** — *He was being followed.*
- Present perfect passive: **has/have + been + V3** — *The plan has been approved.*
- Past perfect passive: **had + been + V3** — *The data had been collected.*
- **"being"** = action in progress | **"been"** = action completed.
- ❌ "is been" is always wrong — choose "is being" (ongoing) or "has been" (done).
