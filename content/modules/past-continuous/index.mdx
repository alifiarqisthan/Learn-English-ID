## Explanation

The **past continuous** (also called past progressive) describes an action that
was **in progress at a specific moment in the past**. The action had already
started, and it was still going at that moment — it had not finished yet.

> "I **was reading** when the lights went out." — reading was in progress when something else happened.
> "At 9pm last night, she **was studying** in her room." — in progress at that specific moment.
> "While you **were sleeping**, I made breakfast." — background activity during another action.

The key picture is a **timeline with an arrow inside it** — the action stretches
across a period of time, and we're zooming in on a moment in the middle.

> Compare with [Simple Past →](/modules/simple-past) — which is a completed event, a dot on the timeline. Past continuous is a stretch.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Action in progress at a past moment | At 8am, he **was driving** to work. |
| Background action interrupted by a sudden event | I **was cooking** when the fire alarm went off. |
| Two parallel actions happening at the same time | She **was reading** while he **was watching** TV. |
| Repeated or annoying past action with "always" | He **was always losing** his keys — it drove us crazy. |
| Setting the scene in a story | It **was raining**. The streets **were empty**. |

### Form — was/were + Ving

The past continuous always has **two parts**: **was** or **were** + the **-ing form** of the main verb.

#### Positive

```
subject + was/were + Ving
```

| Subject | "to be" past | + Ving |
|---|---|---|
| I | **was** | working |
| He / She / It | **was** | working |
| You / We / They | **were** | working |

> I **was writing** a letter. She **was cooking** dinner. They **were playing** football.

Memory trick: the same was/were rule from simple past — **I / he / she / it → was**; **you / we / they → were**.

#### Negative

```
subject + was/were + not + Ving
→ wasn't / weren't + Ving
```

> I **wasn't listening** when the teacher explained that.
> She **wasn't working** at that company in 2019.
> They **weren't expecting** the news.

⚠️ **Never use do/does/did** with past continuous — *was/were* makes its own negatives, exactly like present continuous.
- ❌ I **didn't was** sleeping.
- ✅ I **wasn't** sleeping.

#### Question

```
Was/Were + subject + Ving ?
Wh-word + was/were + subject + Ving ?
```

> **Was** she **sleeping** when you called?
> **Were** they **still arguing** at midnight?
> **What were** you **doing** when the earthquake happened?
> **Why was** he **running** so fast?

No *do/did* — invert subject and *was/were* directly.

### The -ing form

Same spelling rules as present continuous. See [Present Continuous →](/modules/present-continuous) for the full table. Key rules:

- Silent -e: drop it → write → **writing**, make → **making**
- CVC (1 syllable): double the final consonant → run → **running**, sit → **sitting**
- -ie: change to y → lie → **lying**, die → **dying**

### The interruption pattern — the core of past continuous

The most important use of past continuous is the **interruption pattern**:

> **was/were + Ving** (background action) + **when** + **simple past** (sudden event)

| Past Continuous | + | When | + | Simple Past |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I **was walking** home | | **when** | | I **saw** her. |
| She **was taking** a bath | | **when** | | the phone **rang**. |
| They **were sleeping** | | **when** | | the storm **hit**. |

The past continuous is the **longer, ongoing action** — the "background." The simple past is the **shorter, sudden event** that interrupts it.

> "I **was having** dinner when my boss **called** me."

You can also reverse the clause order:

> "**When** my boss **called**, I **was having** dinner."

Both are correct. When *when* starts the sentence, the past continuous usually follows in the second clause.

### The "while" pattern — two parallel actions

Use **while** to show two actions happening at the **same time**, both in past continuous:

> "She **was reading** **while** he **was cooking**."
> "**While** I **was studying**, they **were watching** TV."

**While + past continuous** // **past continuous**

You can also mix:

> "**While** I **was waiting**, she **arrived**." — waiting (long) + arrived (short event)

Here the *while* clause uses past continuous, and the main clause uses simple past.

### When vs While — quick comparison

| Word | Pairs with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **when** | simple past (sudden event) | I was reading **when** the light went out. |
| **while** | past continuous (parallel duration) | **While** I was reading, she was cooking. |

Both can start the sentence. Both can appear in the middle. The choice of *when* vs *while* signals the **type** of relationship.

### Common time markers for past continuous

These signal that past continuous is likely:

- **at that moment**, **at that time**, **at 8pm last night**, **at this time yesterday**
- **when** + simple past event (interruption)
- **while** + another ongoing action
- **all day / all morning / all night** yesterday — the whole period
- **still** — "she was **still sleeping** at noon"
- **as** — similar to *while*: "**As** I was leaving, I saw him."

> *At this time yesterday*, I **was sitting** in the exam hall.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Sedang" in the past

Past continuous is the closest English equivalent to Indonesian's *sedang* when
you're talking about **a past moment**. The structure is parallel:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Saya **sedang** makan ketika dia datang. | I **was eating** when he arrived. |
| Dia **sedang** tidur tadi malam. | She **was sleeping** last night. |
| Mereka **sedang** belajar saat gempa terjadi. | They **were studying** when the earthquake happened. |
| Kami **sedang** berbicara ketika manajer masuk. | We **were talking** when the manager walked in. |

Notice what English needs that Indonesian doesn't:

1. The verb "to be" in past form: **was** or **were**
2. The -ing ending on the main verb

Indonesian's *sedang* does both jobs with **one word**. English needs **two changes**.

### "Ketika" and "saat" → when / while

| Indonesian word | Typical English translation | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **ketika** | when (for an event) | Saya tidur ketika dia pergi → I was sleeping **when** she left. |
| **saat** | when / at the time | Saat dia datang, saya sedang makan → **When** he arrived, I was eating. |
| **sementara / selagi / sambil** | while | Sementara dia memasak, saya belajar → **While** she was cooking, I was studying. |

These Indonesian conjunctions map to the *when/while* patterns above. When you see *ketika/saat + an event*, use **when + simple past**. When you see *sementara/selagi*, use **while + past continuous**.

### Why Indonesian speakers make past continuous errors

Two root causes:

1. **One Indonesian word (*sedang*) does what two English structures do.** Learners translate *sedang* with only one change (usually just adding -ing), forgetting *was/were*.
2. **No verb form change in Indonesian for past tense.** "Sedang makan" is the same whether it happened last night or is happening now — the time is carried by context (*tadi malam*, *kemarin*), not the verb. In English, you must change "am eating" (present) to "**was** eating" (past).

### Side-by-side: what changes

| Feature | Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|---|
| Marker for "in progress" | sedang / lagi / tengah | — (no marker word) |
| Verb changes form? | ❌ Never | ✅ Yes — adds -ing |
| Past tense marker on "to be"? | ❌ No | ✅ was/were (not am/is/are) |
| Same word for present and past? | ✅ Yes (sedang makan = now or past) | ❌ No — "is eating" vs "was eating" |

### Translation strategy

When you see *sedang / lagi / tengah* in an Indonesian past-time sentence:

1. **Identify the subject.** → Choose *was* (I/he/she/it) or *were* (you/we/they).
2. **Add -ing** to the main verb. Watch spelling rules.
3. **Check the time context.** If there's a *ketika/saat + event*, that event uses **simple past**.
4. **Check for *sementara/selagi*.** If yes, the other clause probably needs past continuous too.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Forgetting "was/were"**
- ❌ I **eating** dinner when you called.
- ❌ She **studying** when the fire alarm went off.
- ✅ I **was eating** dinner when you called.
- ✅ She **was studying** when the fire alarm went off.

  *Why it happens:* Indonesian's *sedang* is one word. Learners translate it as one thing (usually just -ing) and forget to add the auxiliary.

**2. Using "did" instead of "was/were"**
- ❌ I **didn't sleeping** at midnight.
- ❌ **Did** they **sleeping** when you arrived?
- ✅ I **wasn't sleeping** at midnight.
- ✅ **Were** they **sleeping** when you arrived?

  *Why it happens:* over-applying the *did* rule from simple past to continuous. Past continuous uses *was/were* exclusively — no *did*.

**3. Wrong was/were agreement**
- ❌ They **was** watching TV.
- ❌ We **was** working all morning.
- ✅ They **were** watching TV.
- ✅ We **were** working all morning.

  *Why it happens:* Indonesian has no equivalent subject-verb agreement, so *was/were* feels random.

**4. Using simple past where past continuous is needed (missing the interruption pattern)**
- ❌ I **ate** dinner when you called. *(implies you called while I was starting/finishing — ambiguous)*
- ✅ I **was eating** dinner when you called. *(clearly in progress when you called)*

  *Context matters:* simple past is OK if the dinner was completely finished before the call. But for actions in progress when interrupted, use past continuous.

**5. Using past continuous where simple past is needed**
- ❌ Yesterday I **was going** to the market and **was buying** vegetables.
- ✅ Yesterday I **went** to the market and **bought** vegetables.

  *Why:* completed, sequential events in a narrative use simple past. Past continuous is for ongoing background, not completed events in a list.

**6. Wrong -ing spelling**
- ❌ She was **runing**, he was **writeing**, they were **lieing**
- ✅ She was **running** (CVC double), he was **writing** (drop -e), they were **lying** (-ie → y)

**7. Stative verbs — same rule as present continuous**

Stative verbs (know, want, love, understand, believe, have = own) don't use past continuous either:
- ❌ I **was knowing** the answer.
- ✅ I **knew** the answer. *(simple past of a stative verb)*

## Quick Summary

- Form: **was/were + Ving**. Two parts — never drop *was/were*.
- **was** for I / he / she / it; **were** for you / we / they.
- Negative: **wasn't / weren't + Ving**. Never use *didn't* with past continuous.
- Question: **Was/Were + subject + Ving?** Invert directly — no *did*.
- Core use: action **in progress at a past moment**, especially when interrupted.
- Interruption pattern: **was/were + Ving** (background) + **when** + **simple past** (event).
- Parallel pattern: **while + was/were + Ving** // **was/were + Ving** (two simultaneous actions).
- Indonesian *sedang* + past time = **was/were + Ving** (two English changes, not one).
- Stative verbs (know, want, love) → use **simple past**, not past continuous.
