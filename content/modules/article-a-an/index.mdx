## Explanation

**A** and **an** are the **indefinite articles**. They introduce a singular countable noun that is either:
1. **First mentioned** — the listener doesn't know which one yet.
2. **One of many** — any member of a category, not a specific one.

> I saw **a** cat in the garden. *(first mention — which cat? any cat)*
> She is **an** engineer. *(one of many engineers — her job category)*
> He ate **a** sandwich for lunch. *(introducing the sandwich for the first time)*

### "A" vs "an" — sound, not spelling

The choice between **a** and **an** depends entirely on the **sound** of the next word, not the letter it starts with.

| Rule | Use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Next word starts with a **consonant sound** | **a** | a book, a car, a university, a European, a one-way street |
| Next word starts with a **vowel sound** | **an** | an apple, an hour, an MBA, an honest man, an X-ray |

⚠️ This is about **sound**, not spelling:
- **a university** — "university" starts with the letter U but sounds like "yoo-" (consonant sound /j/) → **a**
- **an hour** — "hour" starts with H but the H is silent, so it sounds like "our" (vowel sound) → **an**
- **a one-way street** — "one" sounds like "wun" (consonant /w/) → **a**
- **an MBA** — M sounds like "em" (vowel sound) → **an**

#### Common "a vs an" traps

| Word | Article | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| university | **a** university | /j/ sound — "yoo-niversity" |
| hour | **an** hour | silent H — sounds like "our" |
| honest | **an** honest person | silent H — sounds like "on-est" |
| European | **a** European | /j/ sound — "yoo-ropean" |
| MBA / MRI / MP | **an** MBA / MRI / MP | vowel sound of the letter name |
| union | **a** union | /j/ sound — "yoo-nion" |
| umbrella | **an** umbrella | clear vowel /ʌ/ |

### When to use "a / an"

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| First mention of a noun | I bought **a** book. *(now we know there is a book)* |
| Job / profession / nationality / role | She is **a** doctor. He is **an** architect. |
| One of a category | A dog is a loyal animal. |
| With singular countable nouns in general statements | **A** child needs love. |
| Exclamations with "what" | **What a** beautiful day! **What an** amazing result! |
| Numbers (one unit) | **a** hundred, **a** thousand, **a** million |

### What "a / an" cannot be used with

| Cannot use a/an | Why | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Plural nouns | ❌ a books | some books / books |
| Uncountable nouns | ❌ a water / an information | some water / information |
| Already specific noun | ❌ Give me a the book | Give me **the** book |

### Nouns — countable vs uncountable

Before choosing an article, you need to know whether a noun is **countable** or **uncountable**:

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| Has plural form | No plural form |
| Can be counted: one book, two books | Cannot count: ❌ one information |
| Can use a/an: **a** book | Cannot use a/an: ❌ **an** information |

Common uncountable nouns Indonesian learners try to use with "a":
- ❌ an advice → ✅ **some** advice / **a piece of** advice
- ❌ an information → ✅ **some** information / **a piece of** information
- ❌ a furniture → ✅ **some** furniture / **a piece of** furniture
- ❌ a luggage → ✅ **some** luggage / **a piece of** luggage
- ❌ a homework → ✅ **some** homework / **a piece of** homework

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian has no articles

Bahasa Indonesia does not use articles at all. The same word *kucing* can mean:
- "a cat" *(first mention)*
- "the cat" *(specific, known)*
- "cats" *(general plural)*

Context and word order determine which meaning is intended. This is why articles are the **single most common English error** for Indonesian speakers — you must learn to make a distinction that doesn't exist in your language.

### The key decision when translating

When you translate an Indonesian sentence, ask:
1. Is the noun **singular and countable**? → Needs either a/an or the.
2. Is this the **first mention** or a **general/unknown one**? → Use **a/an**.
3. Is it **plural or uncountable**? → No a/an (use ∅ or some).

| Bahasa Indonesia | Translation | Article choice |
|---|---|---|
| Saya punya kucing. | I have **a** cat. | singular countable, first mention |
| Dia dokter. | She is **a** doctor. | profession |
| Itu jam yang bagus. | That is **a** beautiful watch. | singular countable, non-specific |
| Tolong berikan saran. | Please give me **some** advice. | uncountable — no a/an |
| Saya butuh informasi. | I need **some** information. | uncountable — no a/an |

### "An" before a silent H — the most confusing case

Indonesian learners often pronounce the H in English words where it should be silent (*hour*, *honest*, *honour*), which leads them to write **a** instead of **an**. Remember: the article follows the **sound you say**, not the sound you think is there.

## Common Mistakes

**1. "An" before consonant sounds**
- ❌ She is working at **an** university.
- ✅ She is working at **a** university. *(sounds like "yoo-niversity")*

**2. "A" before vowel sounds**
- ❌ It took **a** hour to finish.
- ✅ It took **an** hour to finish. *(H is silent)*

**3. Using "a/an" with uncountable nouns**
- ❌ She gave me **an** advice.
- ✅ She gave me **some** advice. / She gave me **a piece of** advice.

**4. Using "a/an" with plural nouns**
- ❌ I saw **a** birds outside.
- ✅ I saw **some** birds outside. / I saw birds outside.

**5. Omitting "a/an" after "be" for professions**
- ❌ She is doctor.
- ✅ She is **a** doctor.

  *Why:* Indonesian drops the article: *Dia dokter.* But English always requires a/an before a singular countable noun.

**6. Using "a" for abbreviations pronounced as letters**
- ❌ She has **a** MBA.
- ✅ She has **an** MBA. *(M sounds like "em" — vowel sound)*

**7. "What a / What an" in exclamations**
- ❌ What beautiful day!
- ✅ What **a** beautiful day!

## Quick Summary

- **a** before **consonant sounds** | **an** before **vowel sounds** — sound, not spelling.
- Sound traps: **a university** (yoo-), **an hour** (silent H), **a European** (yoo-), **an MBA** (em-).
- Use **a / an** for: first mention, professions, one of a category, singular countable nouns.
- **Never** use a/an with: plural nouns, uncountable nouns.
- Common uncountable traps: advice, information, furniture, luggage, homework, equipment, news.
- After "be" for jobs: She is **a** teacher. He is **an** engineer.
- Exclamations: What **a** great idea! What **an** honour!
