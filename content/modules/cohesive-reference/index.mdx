## Explanation

Cohesive reference and substitution devices allow writers to refer back to previously mentioned ideas, nouns, or verb phrases without repeating them. Mastering these is the difference between C1-level cohesion and mechanical repetition.

### 1. Pronoun Reference: This / That / These / Those / It / They

These pronouns can refer to a **noun** or an entire **idea/clause** stated earlier.

| Pronoun | Refers to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| this | singular idea/noun (nearby, just stated) | *The experiment failed. This was unexpected.* |
| that | singular idea/noun (more distant or formal) | *The hypothesis was refuted. That surprised everyone.* |
| these | plural noun/ideas | *Two problems emerged. These were addressed in the revision.* |
| those | plural noun/ideas (more distant) | *The early studies had flaws. Those have since been corrected.* |
| it | singular noun (non-human, already established) | *The policy was revised. It took effect in January.* |
| they/them | plural noun | *The researchers published their findings. They concluded that…* |

**Avoiding ambiguous reference:**

```
❌ The committee reviewed the report and the proposal.
   This was approved.
   (Which one? The report or the proposal?)

✅ The committee reviewed both documents. The report was approved.
✅ The committee reviewed the report and the proposal.
   The latter was approved. (→ the proposal)
```

### 2. "Such" as a Reference Device

*Such* refers back to a type, category, or set of characteristics just described. It is formal and academic.

```
The city experienced severe flooding and infrastructure collapse.
Such disasters require coordinated government response.
  └── "Such disasters" = the kind of disasters just described

Many scholars have argued for policy reform.
Such arguments, however, have been largely ignored.
```

**Structure**: *such + noun* (not *such a* when referring back to a category)

```
✅ Such findings suggest a need for further research.
✅ Such behaviour is unacceptable.
❌ Such a findings suggest... (wrong — "such a" is used for introducing, not referring back)
```

### 3. The Former / The Latter

*The former* refers to the **first** of two previously mentioned items.
*The latter* refers to the **second** (last) of two previously mentioned items.

```
The company chose between expansion and consolidation.
The former would require significant investment; the latter offered more stability.
  └── "the former" = expansion (mentioned first)
  └── "the latter" = consolidation (mentioned second)
```

**Critical rule: only with exactly two items.**

```
❌ Three strategies were proposed: innovation, cost-cutting, and expansion.
   The former proved most effective.
   (Cannot use "the former" with more than two items — which one is "former"?)

✅ Three strategies were proposed: innovation, cost-cutting, and expansion.
   The first proved most effective. / Innovation proved most effective.
```

### 4. Do So / Does So / Did So (Verb Phrase Substitution)

*Do so* substitutes for a previously mentioned **verb phrase**. It is more formal than repeating the verb.

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | does so / do so | *Students who wish to apply must do so by Friday.* |
| Past simple | did so | *She planned to resign, and she did so quietly.* |
| Future | will do so | *The board will review the proposal and will do so next week.* |
| Modal | may/should do so | *If you wish to appeal, you may do so within 30 days.* |

**Critical rule: do not use "do so" with stative verbs.**

```
✅ She decided to leave, and she did so immediately.   (action verb)
❌ He seemed confident, and he did so throughout.     (stative: "seemed")
✅ He seemed confident throughout.

✅ They agreed to cooperate, and they did so willingly.
❌ The results appeared significant, and they did so clearly. (stative: "appeared")
```

### 5. One / Ones (Noun Substitution)

*One/ones* substitutes for a countable noun to avoid repetition.

```
I need a pen. Do you have one?             ← one = a pen
These chairs are broken. Please use those ones over there.
Of the two reports, the shorter one is more persuasive.
```

**"One" vs "it":**
- *It* refers to a **specific** thing already identified.
- *One* refers to **a member of a type** — not the specific same item.

```
I lost my umbrella. I need to buy one.     ← one = an umbrella (not the specific lost one)
I lent you my umbrella. Please return it.  ← it = that specific umbrella
```

### Summary Table

| Device | What it replaces | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| this / that | singular noun or idea | Check clarity — avoid ambiguous reference |
| these / those | plural noun or idea | Check clarity |
| such + noun | a category just described | Formal; use without *a* when referring back |
| the former / the latter | first / second of TWO items | Only with exactly two items |
| do so | a verb phrase | Not with stative verbs |
| one / ones | a countable noun (non-specific) | Specific item → use *it* |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Padanan dalam Bahasa Indonesia

| Bahasa Inggris | Bahasa Indonesia | Catatan |
|---|---|---|
| this / that (referring back to idea) | ini / itu, hal ini / hal itu | "Hal ini" lebih formal |
| such | demikian, seperti itu | Jarang setepat "such" dalam BI |
| the former / the latter | yang pertama / yang terakhir / yang disebutkan terdahulu | Tidak ada padanan langsung yang ringkas |
| do so | melakukannya | Kurang sistematis dalam Bahasa Indonesia |
| one (substitution) | salah satunya, sebuah | |
| hal tersebut | this matter / that matter | Sangat formal dalam Bahasa Indonesia |

### Interferensi Bahasa Indonesia

**Masalah 1 — Referensi "ini" yang ambigu:**

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "ini" dan "hal ini" sering digunakan secara longgar. Dalam Bahasa Inggris formal, *this* harus jelas merujuk ke apa:

```
❌ The policy was amended and the budget was revised. This was controversial.
   (This what? The amendment or the revision?)

✅ The policy was amended. This amendment proved controversial.
✅ Both changes were made. These proved controversial.
```

**Masalah 2 — "The former/latter" dengan lebih dari dua item:**

Bahasa Indonesia tidak memiliki padanan formal "the former/latter," sehingga pelajar menggunakannya secara salah untuk semua referensi:

```
❌ Three methods were used: surveys, interviews, and observation.
   The former was most reliable.
   (Ambigu — tidak bisa dipakai untuk tiga item)

✅ Of the three methods, surveys proved most reliable.
```

**Masalah 3 — "Do so" dengan kata kerja statis:**

Tidak ada padanan langsung "do so" dalam Bahasa Indonesia, sehingga pelajar tidak menyadari batasannya:

```
❌ The professor appeared knowledgeable, and he did so confidently.
✅ The professor appeared knowledgeable and spoke confidently.
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Ambiguous "this" (unclear referent)**
   ❌ *The company restructured and launched a new product. This increased profits.*
   ✅ *The company restructured and launched a new product. This restructuring increased efficiency.*
   *Why:* When "this" could refer to multiple things, specify: "this decision," "this strategy," "this outcome."

2. **"The former/latter" with more than two items**
   ❌ *The study examined three factors: age, income, and education. The latter had the greatest impact.*
   ✅ *Education had the greatest impact among the three factors.*
   *Why:* "The latter" only works with exactly two items. Use ordinal words (the first, the third) or repeat the noun otherwise.

3. **"Do so" with stative verbs**
   ❌ *The results seemed reliable, and they did so from the start.*
   ✅ *The results seemed reliable from the start.*
   *Why:* "Do so" substitutes for action verbs only. Stative verbs (seem, appear, know, have, believe) cannot use "do so."

4. **"It" instead of "one" (non-specific reference)**
   ❌ *I need a password. Please give me it.*
   ✅ *I need a password. Please give me one.*
   *Why:* "It" refers to a specific already-identified item. "One" refers to a non-specific member of a category.

5. **"Such a findings" (wrong article with such for back-reference)**
   ❌ *Such a findings confirm the hypothesis.*
   ✅ *Such findings confirm the hypothesis.*
   *Why:* When "such" refers back to a type or category, it does not take an indefinite article.

6. **Using "these ones" redundantly**
   ❌ *Of the two options, these ones are better.*
   ✅ *Of the two options, these are better.*
   *Why:* "These" alone functions as a pronoun. "These ones" is redundant (though common in informal speech).

7. **"That" referring to a very distant idea**
   ❌ *[10 sentences ago] The project was delayed. …[many sentences]… That caused problems.*
   ✅ *The delay caused problems.* / *This disruption caused problems.*
   *Why:* "That" for idea reference works across short distances. For distant reference, repeat the key noun (often modified) for clarity.

8. **"Such" without a following noun**
   ❌ *The data was flawed. Such was the main problem.*
   ✅ *The data was flawed. Such errors were the main problem.*
   *Why:* In academic writing, "such" typically precedes a noun — it does not stand alone as a pronoun.

## TOEFL Practice

At C1 level, TOEFL and IELTS examiners assess **lexical resource** and **coherence and cohesion**. Reference and substitution devices directly affect these scores. In your writing:

- Replace repeated nouns with *this/these + [noun label]* to show control of reference.
- Use *do so* to replace verb phrases elegantly in formal responses.
- Use *the former/latter* only when you have introduced exactly two items — examiners notice misuse.
- Use *such* before category nouns to create a formal, academic tone.

## Quick Summary

- **This / that / these / those** can refer to a noun or an entire idea — always check the referent is unambiguous.
- **Such + noun** is a formal back-reference to a type or category of things just described.
- **The former / the latter** refer to the first and second of exactly two previously mentioned items — never use with three or more.
- **Do so** substitutes for a verb phrase in formal writing — never use with stative verbs (seem, appear, know, believe).
- **One / ones** replaces a non-specific countable noun; *it* replaces a specific already-identified item.
- **Ambiguous "this"** is the most common reference error — fix it by adding a noun: *this decision*, *this approach*.
- **"Such a findings"** is wrong — drop the article when using *such* for back-reference.
- Mastering these devices transforms paragraph-level prose from repetitive to cohesive C1-level writing.
