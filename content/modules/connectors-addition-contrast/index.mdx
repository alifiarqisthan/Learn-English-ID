## Explanation

Addition and contrast connectors join ideas across sentences and clauses. Getting the punctuation right is just as important as choosing the correct word.

### Addition Connectors

| Connector | Register | Position | Punctuation |
|---|---|---|---|
| and | neutral | between clauses | no comma (short clauses); comma (long clauses) |
| also | neutral | mid-sentence | no special punctuation |
| in addition | semi-formal | start of sentence | comma after |
| furthermore | formal | start of sentence or after semicolon | comma after |
| moreover | formal | start of sentence or after semicolon | comma after |
| besides | semi-formal | start of sentence | comma after |
| what is more | formal | start of sentence | comma after |

**Punctuation rules for furthermore / moreover / in addition:**

```
Option A — New sentence:
  The data is unreliable. Furthermore, the sample size is too small.

Option B — After a semicolon:
  The data is unreliable; furthermore, the sample size is too small.

❌ WRONG (comma splice):
  The data is unreliable, furthermore the sample size is too small.
```

### Contrast Connectors

| Connector | Type | Position | Punctuation |
|---|---|---|---|
| but | coordinating conjunction | between clauses | comma before |
| yet | coordinating conjunction | between clauses | comma before |
| however | conjunctive adverb | start of sentence or mid-sentence | semicolon before OR new sentence; comma after |
| nevertheless | conjunctive adverb | start of sentence | semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| on the other hand | conjunctive adverb | start of sentence | comma after |
| in contrast | conjunctive adverb | start of sentence | comma after |
| by contrast | conjunctive adverb | start of sentence | comma after |

**Punctuation rules for however / nevertheless:**

```
Option A — New sentence:
  The exam was difficult. However, most students passed.

Option B — After a semicolon:
  The exam was difficult; however, most students passed.

❌ WRONG (comma splice — very common error):
  The exam was difficult, however most students passed.
  The exam was difficult, however, most students passed.
```

### Register Summary

| Formal (Academic / IELTS) | Informal (Everyday) |
|---|---|
| furthermore, moreover, what is more | also, and |
| however, nevertheless, in contrast | but, yet |
| in addition, besides (formal) | also, plus |

### Key Distinctions

- **and** vs **furthermore**: both add, but *furthermore* emphasises a stronger or additional point, often reserved for formal writing.
- **but** vs **however**: both contrast, but *however* cannot join two clauses with only a comma — it needs a semicolon or a new sentence.
- **in contrast / by contrast**: compare two things that are clearly different. *On the other hand* concedes a point from the opposite perspective.

### Interferensi Bahasa Indonesia

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "namun" dan "tetapi" dapat memulai kalimat baru secara bebas tanpa aturan tanda baca khusus. Karena itu, pelajar Indonesia sering menerapkan logika yang sama pada "however":

```
❌ The results were poor, however the team continued.
   (Penutur Indonesia meniru pola "namun" — hanya pakai koma)

✅ The results were poor. However, the team continued.
✅ The results were poor; however, the team continued.
```

Juga, "selain itu" dalam Bahasa Indonesia tidak memerlukan tanda titik koma sebelumnya, sehingga pelajar salah memakai "furthermore" dengan hanya koma:

```
❌ The project was late, furthermore the budget was exceeded.
✅ The project was late. Furthermore, the budget was exceeded.
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Comma splice with "however"**
   ❌ *The plan failed, however they tried again.*
   ✅ *The plan failed. However, they tried again.*
   *Why:* "However" is a conjunctive adverb, not a conjunction — a comma alone cannot join two independent clauses with it.

2. **Comma splice with "furthermore"**
   ❌ *The report was late, furthermore it was incomplete.*
   ✅ *The report was late; furthermore, it was incomplete.*
   *Why:* Same rule — conjunctive adverbs need a semicolon or a new sentence, not just a comma.

3. **Double connector ("moreover, but")**
   ❌ *The test was hard, moreover but she passed.*
   ✅ *The test was hard, but she passed.* / *The test was hard. Moreover, she passed.*
   *Why:* Addition and contrast connectors cannot be stacked on the same join.

4. **Missing comma after the connector**
   ❌ *However the results were surprising.*
   ✅ *However, the results were surprising.*
   *Why:* Conjunctive adverbs at the start of a sentence always take a comma after them.

5. **Using "besides" to mean "except"**
   ❌ *Besides English, I don't speak any other language.* (intended: "Except for English…")
   ✅ *Apart from English, I don't speak any other language.*
   *Why:* As a connector, "besides" means "in addition to / moreover," not "except."

6. **Using "on the contrary" instead of "on the other hand"**
   ❌ *Cats are independent. On the contrary, dogs are loyal.* (intended: comparing, not correcting)
   ✅ *Cats are independent. On the other hand, dogs are loyal.*
   *Why:* "On the contrary" corrects a false statement; "on the other hand" introduces an alternative perspective.

7. **Starting a sentence with "and" or "but" in formal writing**
   ❌ (in an academic essay) *And the results confirmed the hypothesis.*
   ✅ *Furthermore, the results confirmed the hypothesis.*
   *Why:* In formal academic writing, coordinating conjunctions should not begin sentences; use conjunctive adverbs instead.

8. **"what is more, but" — mixing addition and contrast**
   ❌ *What is more, but the costs increased.*
   ✅ *Moreover, the costs increased.* OR *However, the costs increased.*
   *Why:* Choose either addition or contrast — not both at once.

## TOEFL Practice

In the TOEFL Writing section, addition and contrast connectors are essential for paragraph cohesion. Practice using them in your integrated and independent essays. Remember:

- **Thesis support**: use *furthermore* and *moreover* to build your argument point by point.
- **Acknowledging counterarguments**: use *however*, *nevertheless*, or *on the other hand* before your rebuttal.
- **Punctuation**: TOEFL graders notice comma splices — they lower your Syntactic Variety and Grammatical Range scores.

## Quick Summary

- **Addition connectors**: *furthermore*, *moreover*, *in addition*, *besides*, *what is more* — all need a semicolon before them or a new sentence; comma after.
- **Contrast connectors**: *however*, *nevertheless*, *on the other hand*, *in contrast* — same punctuation rule: semicolon or new sentence; comma after.
- **Coordinating conjunctions** (*but*, *yet*, *and*) join clauses with only a comma — they are the exception.
- **"However" + comma** (comma splice) is the single most common punctuation error at B1–B2 level.
- **Register matters**: prefer *furthermore/moreover* over *also* in formal writing; prefer *however/nevertheless* over *but*.
- **Never double-stack connectors**: *moreover, but* or *however, yet* are always wrong.
- **"on the contrary"** ≠ **"on the other hand"** — use the former only to correct a false statement.
- Use *in contrast / by contrast* when two things are being explicitly compared; use *on the other hand* to present an alternative viewpoint.
