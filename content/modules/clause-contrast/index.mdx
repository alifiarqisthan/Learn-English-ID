## Explanation

**Contrast clauses** express that two ideas are surprising or contradictory in relation to each other. English has several different words and structures for contrast — choosing the right one depends on whether you are using a **clause** (with a subject and verb) or a **noun/phrase**.

### Subordinating conjunctions — use with a clause

These words introduce a **dependent clause** (subject + verb):

| Conjunction | Meaning / Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **although** | simple contrast | **Although** it was cold, she went for a run. |
| **even though** | stronger contrast (emphasizes surprise) | **Even though** she was exhausted, she finished. |
| **though** | informal version of although | **Though** it rained, the game continued. |
| **while** | contrast between two parallel facts | **While** she loves coffee, her husband prefers tea. |
| **whereas** | formal contrast between two opposing facts | **Whereas** some prefer solitude, others need company. |

All of these take a **full clause** (subject + verb) after them.

### Prepositions — use with a noun or noun phrase

| Preposition | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **despite** | despite + noun/-ing | **Despite** the rain, they went out. / **Despite** being tired, she worked. |
| **in spite of** | in spite of + noun/-ing | **In spite of** the noise, he slept. |
| **regardless of** | regardless of + noun/-ing | **Regardless of** the cost, they proceeded. |

⚠️ These CANNOT be followed by a full clause with "that":
- ❌ Despite **that** she was tired, she worked.
- ✅ Despite **being** tired, she worked. *(gerund)*
- ✅ Despite **her tiredness**, she worked. *(noun)*
- ✅ **Although** she was tired, she worked. *(conjunction + clause)*

### Conjunctive adverbs — use between two independent clauses

| Adverb | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **however** | ; however, | She studied hard; **however,** she failed. |
| **nevertheless** | ; nevertheless, | It was risky; **nevertheless,** they proceeded. |
| **nonetheless** | ; nonetheless, | The data was limited; **nonetheless,** conclusions were drawn. |
| **on the other hand** | ; on the other hand, | Coffee wakes you up; **on the other hand,** too much causes anxiety. |
| **in contrast** | ; in contrast, | City life is busy; **in contrast,** village life is peaceful. |

⚠️ Conjunctive adverbs need a **semicolon** before them (or a new sentence):
- ❌ She studied hard, however she failed.
- ✅ She studied hard; **however,** she failed.
- ✅ She studied hard. **However,** she failed.

### Summary: contrast word map

| Structure | Word | Takes a... |
|---|---|---|
| Subordinating conjunction | although, even though, though, while, whereas | Full clause (S + V) |
| Preposition | despite, in spite of | Noun / -ing phrase |
| Conjunctive adverb | however, nevertheless, nonetheless | Semicolon + clause |
| Coordinating conjunction | but, yet | Independent clause (comma before) |

### "Although" vs. "despite"

| Pattern | Correct |
|---|---|
| although + clause | Although she was tired, she continued. |
| despite + noun/-ing | Despite her tiredness, she continued. |
| despite + being + adj | Despite being tired, she continued. |
| ❌ despite + clause | ~~Despite that she was tired, she continued.~~ |

### "Whereas" vs. "while" for contrast

Both express contrast between two parallel facts, but:
- **Whereas** is more formal and clearer for contrast.
- **While** can also mean "at the same time" — potentially ambiguous.

> **Whereas** the north is cold, the south is tropical. *(clear contrast)*
> **While** the north is cold, the south is tropical. *(also acceptable — context clarifies contrast)*

In formal/academic writing, prefer **whereas** for contrast to avoid ambiguity with the time meaning of "while."

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian contrast words

| Bahasa Indonesia | English equivalent | Type |
|---|---|---|
| walaupun / meskipun / meski | although / even though | conjunction |
| namun / tetapi / tapi | however / but | coordinating / conjunctive |
| sedangkan / sementara | whereas / while (contrast) | conjunction |
| meskipun begitu | nevertheless | conjunctive adverb |
| meskipun demikian | nonetheless | conjunctive adverb |

### The double-conjunction trap (most common error)

Indonesian allows: *Walaupun dia lelah, **tetapi** dia terus bekerja.* *(although...but)*

English **forbids** using both a subordinating conjunction and a coordinating conjunction for the same contrast:
- ❌ **Although** she was tired, **but** she continued.
- ❌ **Even though** it rained, **yet** we went out.
- ✅ **Although** she was tired, she continued.
- ✅ She was tired, **but** she continued.

### "Despite" — no Indonesian particle equivalent

*Meskipun* in Indonesian works like "although" (before a clause). English "despite" takes a noun, but Indonesian learners often use it with a clause:
- ❌ Despite **that** she was tired...
- ✅ Despite **being** tired... / Despite **her fatigue**...

## Common Mistakes

**1. Double conjunction (although...but)**
- ❌ Although she trained hard, but she lost the match.
- ✅ Although she trained hard, she lost the match.

**2. "Despite" + clause (should be noun/-ing)**
- ❌ Despite **that** the weather was bad, they continued.
- ✅ Despite **the bad weather**, they continued.
- ✅ Despite **being** in bad weather, they continued.
- ✅ **Although** the weather was bad, they continued.

**3. "However" with only a comma — not a semicolon**
- ❌ She studied hard, however she failed.
- ✅ She studied hard**; however,** she failed.

**4. "While" used for contrast when it's ambiguous**
- Potentially ambiguous: While I was reading, she was cooking. *(time or contrast?)*
- Clearer contrast: **Whereas** I prefer reading, she prefers cooking.

**5. Missing comma after "although" when it leads**
- ❌ Although it rained we went out.
- ✅ Although it rained**,** we went out.

**6. "In spite of" + clause**
- ❌ In spite of she was tired, she worked.
- ✅ In spite of **being** tired, she worked.

## Quick Summary

- **although / even though / though** + full clause. Comma after if clause is first.
- **despite / in spite of** + noun or -ing phrase (NOT a full clause).
- **however / nevertheless** + semicolon + comma: *; however,*
- **whereas / while** = formal contrast between two parallel facts — prefer **whereas** in academic writing.
- **NEVER** use: *although...but*, *even though...yet* — pick one, not both.
- "Despite **that**..." is always wrong — use "despite + noun/-ing" or switch to "although + clause".
