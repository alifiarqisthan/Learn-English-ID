## Explanation

When we report a question, we do **not** use question word order. The reported (indirect) question follows **statement word order** (subject + verb).

### Yes/No questions → if / whether + statement word order

| Direct question | Reported question |
|---|---|
| "Are you ready?" | She asked **if I was ready**. |
| "Did he call?" | She asked **whether he had called**. |
| "Can you help me?" | He asked **if I could help him**. |

> **Formula:** asked + **if / whether** + subject + verb (no auxiliary inversion)

*if* and *whether* are interchangeable here; *whether* is slightly more formal.

### Wh-questions → wh-word + statement word order

| Direct question | Reported question |
|---|---|
| "Where **do** you live?" | He asked where **I lived**. |
| "What **is** she doing?" | She asked what **he was doing**. |
| "Why **did** they leave?" | I asked why **they had left**. |
| "How long **have** you been here?" | She asked how long **I had been there**. |

> **Key rule:** The auxiliary verb is removed; the main verb takes the correct tense form.

### Tense backshift still applies

All the same backshift rules from the reported statements module apply here:

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"Where **do** you work?"* | She asked where I **worked**. |
| *"What **will** you do?"* | He asked what I **would** do. |
| *"Have you **seen** it?"* | She asked if I **had seen** it. |

### Reported commands and requests

Use **tell / ask + object + to-infinitive**:

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| "Sit down!" (command) | She **told** us **to sit** down. |
| "Please open the window." (request) | He **asked** me **to open** the window. |
| "Don't be late!" (negative command) | She **told** him **not to be** late. |

> **Formula (command):** told + object + (not) to + base verb
> **Formula (request):** asked + object + (not) to + base verb

### Reported requests with *if / whether*

When someone asks *Can/Could/Would you…?* as a polite request, report it as a request with *to-infinitive*, not as a question:

- "Could you help me?" → She asked me **to help** her.
- "Would you mind closing the door?" → He asked me **to close** the door.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Apakah" dan "di mana" tidak mengubah susunan kata

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, pertanyaan tidak langsung tidak mengubah susunan kata kerja.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Dia bertanya apakah saya siap. | She asked **if I was ready**. |
| Dia bertanya di mana saya tinggal. | He asked where **I lived**. |
| Dia bertanya kapan saya akan datang. | She asked when **I would come**. |

Pelajar Indonesia sering membuat kesalahan karena menggunakan susunan kata pertanyaan langsung dalam pertanyaan tidak langsung.

### Kesalahan khas pelajar Indonesia

| ❌ Salah (gaya Indonesia) | ✅ Benar (English) |
|---|---|
| She asked where **did I live**. | She asked where **I lived**. |
| He asked if **was I ready**. | He asked if **I was ready**. |
| She asked me **help** her. | She asked me **to help** her. |
| He **asked that** she was tired. | He **said** (that) she was tired. |

### "Suruh" dan "minta"

- **menyuruh** = *told (someone) to* (perintah)
- **meminta** = *asked (someone) to* (permintaan sopan)

*"Dia menyuruh **kami** untuk duduk."* → *She told **us** to sit down.*

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *She asked where **did I live**.* → ✅ *She asked where **I lived**.*
   *Why:* Indirect questions use statement word order — no auxiliary inversion.

2. ❌ *He asked if **was she** ready.* → ✅ *He asked if **she was** ready.*
   *Why:* No subject-auxiliary inversion after *if* in reported questions.

3. ❌ *She **asked that** he come.* → ✅ *She **asked him to** come.*
   *Why:* Reported commands and requests use *ask + object + to-infinitive*, not *asked that*.

4. ❌ *He asked **what did** she want.* → ✅ *He asked **what she wanted**.*
   *Why:* *Did* is dropped; the main verb takes the past form in statement word order.

5. ❌ *She asked me **help** her.* → ✅ *She asked me **to help** her.*
   *Why:* After *ask + object*, use the full *to-infinitive*.

6. ❌ *He told me **not leave**.* → ✅ *He told me **not to leave**.*
   *Why:* Negative reported commands: *told + object + not + to + base verb*.

7. ❌ *She asked where **I live**.* (no backshift) → ✅ *She asked where **I lived**.*
   *Why:* Tense backshift still applies in reported questions when the reporting verb is past.

8. ❌ *He **asked if or not** she had finished.* → ✅ *He asked **whether** she had finished.*
   *Why:* Use *whether* (not *if or not*) for more formal indirect yes/no questions.

## TOEFL Practice

Indirect questions are common in TOEFL listening and reading. The test will ask you to:
- Choose correct statement word order in indirect questions
- Identify *if/whether* vs. *that* errors
- Spot missing *to* in reported commands

> **Strategy:** Cover the reporting clause (*She asked*) and check that the remaining clause is a statement, not a question. If it looks like a question (inverted verb), it is wrong.

## Translation Drill

Convert these direct questions and commands into reported speech.

1. "Are you coming tomorrow?" (He asked me…) → *He asked me **if I was coming the next day**.*
2. "Where did she go?" (I asked…) → *I asked **where she had gone**.*
3. "What time does the bus leave?" (She asked…) → *She asked **what time the bus left**.*
4. "Please wait outside." (He asked us…) → *He asked us **to wait** outside.*
5. "Don't touch that!" (She told him…) → *She told him **not to touch** that.*

## Quick Summary

- Yes/no indirect questions use **if** or **whether** + statement word order, never question word order.
- Wh- indirect questions use the wh-word + statement word order (no auxiliary inversion).
- The auxiliary *do/does/did* is removed in indirect questions; the verb conjugates in the correct tense.
- Tense backshift still applies: *did* → *had done*, *will* → *would*, *can* → *could*.
- Reported commands: **told + object + (not) to + base verb**.
- Reported requests: **asked + object + (not) to + base verb**.
- Never use *asked that* for commands — use *asked + object + to-infinitive*.
- Indonesian word order doesn't change for indirect questions — be careful not to transfer this pattern into English.
