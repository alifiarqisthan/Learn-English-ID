## Explanation

A **complex sentence** contains one **independent clause** (a complete thought) and at least one **dependent clause** (an incomplete thought that cannot stand alone).

The dependent clause is introduced by a **subordinating conjunction**.

```
Independent clause + subordinating conjunction + dependent clause
Subordinating conjunction + dependent clause , + independent clause
```

> She studies hard **because she wants to pass**. *(independent + dependent)*
> **Because she wants to pass,** she studies hard. *(dependent first — note the comma)*

### Independent vs. dependent clauses

| Type | Can stand alone? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Independent | Yes | She studies hard. |
| Dependent | No | Because she studies hard. / Although it rained. |

A dependent clause alone is a **fragment**. It must attach to an independent clause.

### Categories of subordinating conjunctions

| Category | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, while, before, after, until, as, once, as soon as | **When the alarm rang,** she woke up. |
| **Cause** | because, since, as | He passed **because he prepared**. |
| **Contrast** | although, even though, though, whereas, while | **Although it rained,** they played. |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided that, as long as | **If you study,** you will pass. |
| **Purpose** | so that, in order that | She left early **so that she could rest**. |
| **Result** | so…that, such…that | It was **so cold that** she wore a coat. |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though | He spoke **as though he knew everything**. |

### Comma rule — the most tested rule

| Order | Comma? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Independent → Dependent | **No comma** | She studies **because she wants to pass**. |
| Dependent → Independent | **Comma after dependent clause** | **Because she wants to pass,** she studies. |

> The comma goes after the dependent clause when it comes **first** — think of it as a pause before the main idea lands.

### Subordinating conjunctions vs. coordinating conjunctions

| Type | Examples | Join what? | Comma rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordinating (FANBOYS) | and, but, so, or... | Two independent clauses | Comma *before* |
| Subordinating | because, although, when... | Independent + dependent | Comma after dependent *if first* |

### "Although" vs. "however" — a critical distinction

These words express contrast, but they work very differently:

| Word | Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| although | subordinating conjunction | + clause | **Although** it rained, we went out. |
| however | conjunctive adverb | ; ___ , | It rained**; however,** we went out. |

- ❌ Although it rained, **however** we went out. *(cannot use both)*
- ✅ **Although** it rained, we went out.
- ✅ It rained**; however,** we went out.

### Compound-complex sentences

A **compound-complex sentence** has two or more independent clauses **and** at least one dependent clause.

> **Because the data was incomplete,** the researchers revised their methods, **and** the final report was delayed.

This is the most advanced sentence type. Use it to show sophisticated academic writing.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian subordinating conjunctions

Most Indonesian subordinating conjunctions have direct equivalents:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| karena / karena itu | because / therefore |
| walaupun / meskipun / meski | although / even though |
| jika / kalau / apabila | if / when |
| ketika / saat | when |
| sebelum | before |
| setelah | after |
| agar / supaya | so that |

The key difference: **Indonesian does not have the English comma rule** for clause order.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English (dependent first) |
|---|---|
| Karena hujan, mereka tinggal di rumah. | **Because it rained,** they stayed home. *(comma after dependent)* |
| Meskipun lelah, dia terus belajar. | **Although she was tired,** she kept studying. *(comma after dependent)* |

### "Walaupun/meskipun" vs. "tetapi" — the double-conjunction trap

This is the **most common complex sentence error** by Indonesian speakers. In Indonesian, you can say:

> *Walaupun dia lelah, **tetapi** dia terus bekerja.*

In English, you **cannot** use both the subordinating conjunction and a coordinating conjunction:
- ❌ **Although** he was tired, **but** he kept working.
- ✅ **Although** he was tired, he kept working.
- ✅ He was tired, **but** he kept working.

Choose one structure. Using both is a grammatical error.

### Embedded clause position

In Indonesian, the dependent clause can often appear mid-sentence more flexibly. In English, the main structural positions are:

1. Dependent clause first: **Because she studied,** she passed. *(comma required)*
2. Dependent clause second: She passed **because she studied**. *(no comma)*

## Common Mistakes

**1. Fragment — dependent clause left alone**
- ❌ Although the results were promising.
- ✅ Although the results were promising, **more research is needed**.

**2. Missing comma when dependent clause is first**
- ❌ Because she was tired she left early.
- ✅ Because she was tired**,** she left early.

**3. Adding comma when independent clause is first**
- ❌ She left early, because she was tired.
- ✅ She left early because she was tired.

  *Exception:* a comma before "because" is acceptable when it is needed to avoid ambiguity. In academic writing, omit it unless required for clarity.

**4. Double conjunction ("although...but")**
- ❌ Although he studied hard, but he failed.
- ✅ Although he studied hard, he failed.
- ✅ He studied hard, but he failed.

**5. Confusing "although" and "however"**
- ❌ Although it rained, however we went out.
- ✅ Although it rained, we went out.
- ✅ It rained; however, we went out.

**6. Using "despite" as a subordinating conjunction**
- ❌ Despite she was tired, she kept working.
- ✅ **Despite** being tired, she kept working. *(despite + noun/gerund)*
- ✅ **Although** she was tired, she kept working. *(although + clause)*

**7. "While" meaning contrast vs. time — ambiguity**
- ❌ While I like tea, my sister prefers coffee. *(ambiguous — is this time or contrast?)*
- ✅ **Whereas** I like tea, my sister prefers coffee. *(clear contrast)*

## Quick Summary

- A **complex sentence** = one independent clause + one (or more) dependent clause(s).
- Dependent clause = introduced by a subordinating conjunction; **cannot stand alone**.
- **Comma rule:** dependent clause **first** → comma after it. Dependent clause **second** → no comma.
- Key subordinating conjunctions: **because, although, when, if, while, unless, since, so that**.
- **Never** combine "although" with "but" or "even though" with "yet" in the same clause.
- "Although" = subordinating conjunction (use with a clause). "However" = conjunctive adverb (use after a semicolon).
- "Despite" takes a noun or gerund, **not** a clause.
