## Explanation

**Would** has several distinct uses: conditional results, past habitual actions, polite requests and offers, and formal preferences. It is one of the most versatile modal verbs in English.

### 1. Would for conditional results

In conditional sentences (especially Type 2 and Type 3), "would" expresses the hypothetical result:

| Conditional | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Type 2 (unreal present) | If + V2, would + V1 | If I had more time, I **would travel**. |
| Type 3 (unreal past) | If + had + V3, would + have + V3 | If I had studied, I **would have passed**. |

> "Would" in conditionals = the consequence of an unreal or hypothetical situation.
> I **would go** if I were invited. *(not invited, so not going)*
> She **would have come** if she had known. *(she didn't know, so she didn't come)*

### 2. Would for past habits (used to + V1)

"Would" can describe past habitual or repeated actions (not states):

> Every summer, we **would go** to the beach.
> He **would always** bring coffee for everyone.
> She **would spend** hours in the library as a student.

> **Important limitation**: "Would" for past habits only works with **action verbs**, not stative verbs. For stative verbs (be, have, know, love), use "used to":
> - ❌ We would live in Jakarta. *(stative)*
> - ✅ We **used to** live in Jakarta.
> - ✅ We **would visit** the market every weekend. *(action)*

### 3. Would for polite requests and offers

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Polite request | **Would** you mind closing the window? |
| Offering help | **Would** you like some assistance? |
| Preference/choice | I **would like** a coffee, please. |
| Formal invitation | **Would** you care to join us? |

> "Would you mind + V-ing?" is a very polite form of asking someone to do something.
> "Would you like…?" is more polite than "Do you want…?"

### 4. Would for reported speech

In reported speech, "will" in the original becomes "would":

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| "I **will** call you." | She said she **would** call me. |
| "We **will** finish by Friday." | They said they **would** finish by Friday. |

### 5. Would + have + V3 — conditional perfect

For hypothetical past results:
> She **would have won** if she had practised more.
> I **wouldn't have come** if I had known it was so far.

### Summary table

| Use | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional (Type 2) | would + V1 | I **would apply** if eligible. |
| Conditional (Type 3) | would + have + V3 | I **would have applied** if I'd known. |
| Past habit (action only) | would + V1 | We **would walk** to school every day. |
| Polite request | Would you + V1 | **Would** you help me? |
| Preference | would like + V1/noun | I **would like** to apply. |
| Reported speech | would + V1 | She said she **would** come. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Akan" / "Biasanya" / "Ingin"

Indonesian uses **akan** (future/conditional), **biasanya** or **dulu selalu** (used to/would for habits), and **ingin** (would like):

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Kalau ada waktu, saya akan pergi. | If I had time, I **would go**. |
| Dulu kami selalu bermain di taman. | We **would** play / **used to** play in the park. |
| Saya mau/ingin segelas kopi. | I **would like** a glass of coffee. |
| Bisakah kamu menutup pintunya? | **Would** you mind closing the door? |

### Common interference errors

**1. "Would" for past habits with stative verbs**

*Dulu saya suka tinggal di Jakarta* → Indonesian doesn't distinguish action vs stative, so students use "would" for both:
- ❌ I would live in Jakarta when I was young.
- ✅ I **used to** live in Jakarta when I was young.

**2. "Would" + present (forgetting conditional structure)**

*Kalau saya punya mobil, saya akan pergi* → students sometimes drop "if":
- ❌ I would go, I have a car.
- ✅ If I **had** a car, I **would** go.

**3. "Would like" vs "want"**

*Saya mau* = I want (informal). In formal/polite English:
- ❌ I want a glass of water. *(correct but informal)*
- ✅ I **would like** a glass of water. *(polite)*

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "would" for stative verbs in past habits**
- ❌ He would be very shy as a child.
- ✅ He **used to be** very shy as a child.
- *Why:* "Would" for past habits is limited to action verbs. Stative verbs (be, have, know, love, own) require "used to".

**2. Confusing "would" (conditional) with "will" (certain future)**
- ❌ If I study hard, I would pass.
- ✅ If I study hard, I **will** pass. *(Type 1 — real/likely)*
- ✅ If I studied hard, I **would** pass. *(Type 2 — unreal/hypothetical)*
- *Why:* "Would" in conditionals signals an unreal/hypothetical situation (Type 2 or 3).

**3. "Would have" without "had + V3" in the if-clause**
- ❌ If I know, I would have helped.
- ✅ If I **had known**, I **would have helped**.
- *Why:* Type 3 conditional: if + had + V3 → would + have + V3.

**4. Adding "-s" to "would"**
- ❌ She woulds prefer to meet on Monday.
- ✅ She **would** prefer to meet on Monday.
- *Why:* Modals never inflect — no "woulds".

**5. "Would you mind" + V1 instead of V-ing**
- ❌ Would you mind open the window?
- ✅ Would you mind **opening** the window?
- *Why:* "Mind" is followed by the gerund (V-ing), not the infinitive.

**6. Using "would" in the if-clause**
- ❌ If you would come earlier, we could talk.
- ✅ If you **came** earlier, we could talk.
- *Why:* "Would" is generally not used in the "if" part of a conditional. It goes in the result clause.

## Quick Summary

- **Conditional**: If + V2, **would** + V1 (Type 2); if + had V3, **would have** + V3 (Type 3)
- **Past habit**: **would** + V1 for repeated past actions (action verbs only — not stative)
- **Stative verbs** (be, have, know, love): use **used to**, not "would"
- **Polite request**: "**Would** you mind V-ing?" / "**Would** you like…?"
- **Preference**: "I **would like** to…" (more polite than "I want to")
- **Reported speech**: "She said she **would** call." (direct "will" → reported "would")
- "Would" never goes in the **if-clause** of a conditional
- "Would" never takes "-s": never "woulds"
