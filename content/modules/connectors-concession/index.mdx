## Explanation

Concession connectors acknowledge a point that seems to argue against your position, before reasserting your main claim. They are the backbone of balanced academic argumentation.

### What Is Concession?

Concession says: *"I admit X is true — but my main point still stands."*

```
Although the study had limitations, its findings are significant.
  └── concession ──┘   └────────── main claim ──────────────┘

Despite the rain, the event was a success.
  └── concession ┘  └── main claim ──────┘
```

### Subordinating Conjunctions (+ full clause)

| Connector | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| although | even though — neutral/formal | interchangeable with *even though* |
| even though | emphasised concession | stronger than *although* |
| though | informal version of *although* | also used mid-sentence: *He tried, though.* |
| while | contrast/concession (not time here) | formal; used to compare two facts |
| whereas | direct contrast between two things | formal; often compares two subjects |

**Structure: subordinate clause + main clause (or vice versa)**

```
Although she studied hard, she did not pass.
She did not pass, although she studied hard.

While urban areas have many amenities, rural life offers peace.
Whereas the North is industrialised, the South relies on agriculture.
```

**"While" vs "whereas":**
- *While*: concedes one fact before stating the opposite. Can imply the two things exist simultaneously.
- *Whereas*: directly contrasts two different things or groups. More explicitly contrastive.

### Prepositions (+ noun / gerund phrase)

| Connector | Follows… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| despite | noun / gerund (-ing) | Despite the rain / Despite raining |
| in spite of | noun / gerund (-ing) | In spite of her efforts / In spite of trying |

```
Despite his hard work, he was not promoted.
In spite of the bad weather, the event continued.

Despite working overtime, the team missed the deadline.
In spite of studying all night, she failed the test.

❌ Despite he was tired, he continued.    ← WRONG (clause after despite)
✅ Despite being tired, he continued.    ← correct (gerund)
✅ Although he was tired, he continued.  ← correct (clause after although)
```

### Conjunctive Adverbs (between sentences)

| Connector | Register | Punctuation |
|---|---|---|
| nevertheless | formal | Semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| nonetheless | formal | Semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| even so | neutral | Semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| yet | semi-formal | Comma before (joins clauses like a conjunction) |
| still | neutral | Mid-sentence; no special punctuation rule |

```
The data was incomplete. Nevertheless, the conclusions are valid.
The data was incomplete; nonetheless, the conclusions are valid.
The treatment had side effects. Even so, patients preferred it.
The film received poor reviews, yet it became a box-office hit.
```

### Despite vs Although — Core Rule

| | despite / in spite of | although / even though |
|---|---|---|
| Follows | noun / noun phrase / gerund | subject + verb (full clause) |
| Example | despite the difficulty | although it was difficult |
| Wrong version | ❌ despite it was difficult | ❌ although the difficulty |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Padanan dalam Bahasa Indonesia

| Bahasa Inggris | Bahasa Indonesia | Catatan |
|---|---|---|
| although / even though | meskipun, walaupun, kendatipun | "meskipun" paling umum |
| though | meskipun (informal) | |
| while (contrast) | sementara (itu) | berbeda dari "while" waktu |
| whereas | sedangkan, sementara | membandingkan dua hal |
| despite / in spite of | meskipun, terlepas dari, walaupun | Bahasa Indonesia tidak membedakan frasa vs klausa |
| nevertheless | meski demikian, namun demikian | formal |
| nonetheless | bagaimanapun juga | formal |
| even so | meski begitu | semi-formal |

### Interferensi Bahasa Indonesia

**Masalah utama: "meskipun" + klausa diterjemahkan langsung menjadi "despite" + klausa**

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "meskipun" selalu diikuti klausa (S + V). Pelajar salah mengasumsikan "despite" juga diikuti klausa:

```
Bahasa Indonesia: Meskipun dia lelah, dia terus bekerja.

❌ Despite he was tired, he continued working.
✅ Despite being tired, he continued working.
✅ Although he was tired, he continued working.
```

**Masalah kedua: "although, but" — double connector**

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "meskipun … tetapi …" adalah pola yang lazim:
*Meskipun hujan, tetapi mereka tetap bermain.*

Namun dalam Bahasa Inggris, dua connector tidak boleh digunakan sekaligus:

```
❌ Although it rained, but they continued playing.
✅ Although it rained, they continued playing.
✅ It rained. Nevertheless, they continued playing.
```

**Masalah ketiga: "in spite of that it rained"**

"In spite of that" tidak bisa diikuti klausa. "That" tidak mengubahnya menjadi conjunction:

```
❌ In spite of that it rained, the match continued.
✅ In spite of the rain, the match continued.
✅ In spite of the fact that it rained, the match continued. (verbose but grammatical)
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **"Despite" + clause**
   ❌ *Despite the team worked hard, they lost.*
   ✅ *Despite the team's hard work, they lost.*
   ✅ *Although the team worked hard, they lost.*
   *Why:* "Despite" is a preposition — it must be followed by a noun or gerund, never a subject-verb clause.

2. **"Although, but" (double connector)**
   ❌ *Although it was expensive, but we bought it.*
   ✅ *Although it was expensive, we bought it.*
   ✅ *It was expensive, but we bought it.*
   *Why:* "Although" and "but" both signal contrast — using both creates a grammatical redundancy.

3. **"In spite of that it" (preposition + that + clause)**
   ❌ *In spite of that it was raining, they held the event.*
   ✅ *In spite of the rain, they held the event.*
   ✅ *In spite of the fact that it was raining, they held the event.*
   *Why:* "In spite of" needs a noun — "in spite of that" is not a conjunction.

4. **Comma splice with "nevertheless"**
   ❌ *The evidence was weak, nevertheless the verdict stood.*
   ✅ *The evidence was weak. Nevertheless, the verdict stood.*
   ✅ *The evidence was weak; nevertheless, the verdict stood.*
   *Why:* "Nevertheless" is a conjunctive adverb — it cannot join two clauses with only a comma.

5. **Confusing "while" (time) and "while" (concession/contrast)**
   ❌ *While I was studying in London for three years, the city is expensive.* (mixed time + contrast)
   ✅ *While London is expensive, it offers world-class education.*
   *Why:* When "while" is used for contrast, both clauses should describe states or facts, not actions in time.

6. **"Whereas" with more than two items**
   ❌ *Whereas cats, dogs, and birds are common pets in the city, horses are farm animals.*
   ✅ *While cats, dogs, and birds are common city pets, horses are typically farm animals.*
   *Why:* "Whereas" most naturally contrasts exactly two entities or groups.

7. **"Even so" at the wrong position**
   ❌ *Even so the project failed, we learned a great deal.*
   ✅ *The project failed. Even so, we learned a great deal.*
   *Why:* "Even so" is a sentence connector — it goes between sentences, not at the start of a subordinate clause.

8. **"Though" at the start of a formal sentence**
   ❌ (In a formal essay) *Though many argued against the policy, it was implemented.*
   ✅ *Although many argued against the policy, it was implemented.*
   *Why:* "Though" is acceptable in semi-formal writing but "although" is preferred in academic contexts.

## TOEFL Practice

Concession connectors are essential for TOEFL Writing Task 2 (Independent) and IELTS Task 2. When acknowledging a counterargument:

1. State the concession with *although* or *even though*.
2. Immediately follow with your rebuttal using *however*, *nevertheless*, or *nonetheless*.
3. Avoid *despite* + clause — examiners notice this error.

Example paragraph structure:
*Although renewable energy sources have high initial costs, they generate significant long-term savings. Furthermore, as technology improves, these costs are decreasing. Therefore, governments should invest in renewable infrastructure.*

## Quick Summary

- **Subordinating conjunctions** (*although*, *even though*, *though*, *while*, *whereas*) introduce a full clause (subject + verb).
- **Prepositions** (*despite*, *in spite of*) introduce a noun or gerund — never a full clause.
- **Conjunctive adverbs** (*nevertheless*, *nonetheless*, *even so*) connect sentences — need a semicolon or a new sentence; comma after.
- **"Although, but"** is always wrong — use one connector only.
- **"Despite he was tired"** is always wrong — use *despite being tired* or *although he was tired*.
- **"While" and "whereas"** signal direct contrast between two states or groups — not to be confused with *while* as a time connector.
- **"Nevertheless/nonetheless"** + comma (comma splice) is a frequent B2 error.
- In academic writing, prefer *although* over *though*, and *nevertheless/nonetheless* over *even so*.
