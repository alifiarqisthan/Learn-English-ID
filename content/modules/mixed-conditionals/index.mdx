## Explanation

**Mixed conditionals** combine elements from the second and third conditionals to express situations where the **time of the condition and the time of the result are different**. There are two main types.

---

### Type A: Past Condition → Present Result

Use this when a **past event** has an effect on the **present situation**.

**Formula:** `If + had + V3 (past perfect), would + V1 (base verb)`

| Clause | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| If-clause | If + past perfect | Something that didn't happen in the past |
| Result clause | would + V1 | How things would be different now |

**Examples:**

> If I **had taken** that job offer, I **would be** in Singapore now.
> *(I didn't take the job — that's a past fact. Now, as a result, I'm not in Singapore.)*

> If she **had studied** engineering, she **would have** a better-paying career right now.

> If they **had invested** in that property ten years ago, they **would be** millionaires today.

---

### Type B: Present Condition → Past Result

Use this when a **present state** explains why something **in the past** happened or didn't happen.

**Formula:** `If + past simple/were (present condition), would have + V3`

| Clause | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| If-clause | If + past simple / were | A present or permanent characteristic |
| Result clause | would have + V3 | A different outcome in the past |

**Examples:**

> If she **weren't** so shy, she **would have applied** for the leadership position.
> *(She is shy — that's her current characteristic. Because of it, she didn't apply.)*

> If he **were** more careful with money, he **wouldn't have wasted** his savings.

> If I **spoke** better English, I **would have gotten** that job at the international company.

---

### When to Use Each Type

| Type | Condition | Result | Signal words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type A (Past → Present) | Past event that didn't happen | Present situation | now, today, at the moment, still |
| Type B (Present → Past) | Current state/character | Past event that didn't happen | last year, yesterday, at that time |

**Tip:** Look for time markers. If "now" or "today" appears in the result, use Type A. If "yesterday" or "last year" appears, use Type B.

---

### Contrast with Pure Conditionals

| Type | If-clause | Result clause | Both refer to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second (pure) | Past simple | would + V1 | Present / future |
| Third (pure) | Past perfect | would have + V3 | Past |
| Mixed A | Past perfect | would + V1 | Past condition → present |
| Mixed B | Past simple / were | would have + V3 | Present condition → past |

**Compare these three sentences:**

> **Second (pure):** If I lived in London, I **would visit** the museums regularly. *(hypothetical now)*
> **Third (pure):** If I had lived in London, I **would have visited** the museums. *(hypothetical past)*
> **Mixed A:** If I had moved to London, I **would know** the city well by now. *(past choice → present knowledge)*

---

### Common Structures to Recognise

**Type A markers:** "…now / by now / today / at this point / still"
> If he **had graduated** on time, he **would be** working already.
> If she **had recovered** faster, she **would still be** playing football.

**Type B markers:** "…last year / yesterday / at the time / when…"
> If I **were** a native speaker, I **would have understood** that joke immediately.
> If they **had** more experience, they **would have handled** the crisis better.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Tidak Ada Padanan Langsung

Bahasa Indonesia tidak membedakan "mixed conditional" dari conditional biasa secara gramatikal. Penutur Indonesia mengandalkan **konteks waktu** dan kata keterangan seperti "sekarang", "tadi", "dulu" untuk membedakan waktu.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English (Mixed Conditional) | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Seandainya aku sudah mengambil pekerjaan itu, sekarang aku sudah ada di Jakarta. | If I had taken that job, I would be in Jakarta now. | A (past → present) |
| Kalau dia tidak pemalu, dia pasti sudah melamar posisi itu dulu. | If she weren't shy, she would have applied for that position. | B (present → past) |
| Andaikan saya bisa berbahasa Inggris lebih baik, tadi saya pasti sudah mengerti presentasi itu. | If I spoke better English, I would have understood that presentation. | B (present → past) |

### Kesalahan Umum Pelajar Indonesia

> ❌ If I took that job, I would be in Singapore now. *(second conditional — doesn't signal past event)*
> ✅ If I **had taken** that job, I would be in Singapore now. *(Mixed A)*

> ❌ If she weren't shy, she wouldn't have applied. *(result clause wrong — implies she did apply)*
> ✅ If she **weren't** shy, she **would have applied**. *(she is shy now → she didn't apply then)*

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *If I **took** that job, I would be in London now.* → ✅ *If I **had taken** that job, I would be in London now.* *Why:* Type A mixed conditional needs past perfect in the if-clause to signal the unreal past event.

2. ❌ *If I had studied harder, I **would have been** fluent by now.* → ✅ *If I had studied harder, I **would be** fluent by now.* *Why:* When the result is in the present ("by now"), use "would + V1," not "would have + V3."

3. ❌ *If she **wasn't** so lazy, she would have finished it yesterday.* → ✅ *If she **weren't** so lazy, she would have finished it yesterday.* *Why:* The subjunctive "weren't" is correct for present conditions using "be."

4. ❌ *If he **had be** more experienced, he would have handled it better.* → ✅ *If he **were** more experienced, he would have handled it better.* *Why:* For a present characteristic (Type B), use "were" (past simple of be), not "had be."

5. ❌ *If they **had invested**, they **would have been** rich today.* → ✅ *If they had invested, they **would be** rich today.* *Why:* "Today" signals a present result — use "would be," not "would have been."

6. ❌ *If I **spoke** better French, I **would understand** that film at the time.* → ✅ *If I spoke better French, I **would have understood** that film at the time.* *Why:* "At the time" signals a past result — use "would have + V3."

7. ❌ *If you **would have been** more careful, you wouldn't be in this situation now.* → ✅ *If you **had been** more careful, you wouldn't be in this situation now.* *Why:* Never use "would have" in the if-clause.

8. ❌ *If she had studied medicine, she **would have been** a doctor now.* → ✅ *If she had studied medicine, she **would be** a doctor now.* *Why:* Present result ("now") needs "would + V1," not "would have + V3."

## TOEFL Practice

Mixed conditionals rarely appear in isolation on TOEFL but may appear in complex sentence completion tasks. Key skills:
- Recognise time markers to decide which type of mixed conditional is needed
- Distinguish "would + V1" (present result) from "would have + V3" (past result)
- Spot "would have" errors in if-clauses

**Strategy:** Identify the time of the condition and the time of the result separately before choosing the form.

## Quick Summary

- Mixed conditionals combine **different time frames** in the two clauses
- **Type A (Past → Present):** If + **had + V3** → **would + V1** (past event, present result)
- **Type B (Present → Past):** If + **past simple/were** → **would have + V3** (present state, past result)
- Look for time markers: "now/today" → present result (Type A); "yesterday/last year" → past result (Type B)
- Never use "would have" in the if-clause
- Never confuse "would + V1" (present result) with "would have + V3" (past result)
- Indonesian has no direct equivalent — rely on context and time markers
- These structures appear most naturally when expressing **regret** or **explanation** for a current situation
