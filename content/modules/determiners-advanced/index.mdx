## Explanation

Advanced determiners make fine-grained distinctions that are often tested in TOEFL and IELTS. This module covers **either/neither, no/none, any, another/other/others**, and the **double determiner** patterns.

### Either / Neither — two options

**Either** and **neither** refer to exactly **two** items.

| Word | Meaning | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **either** | one or the other (both are acceptable) | singular | **Either** option **works** for me. |
| **neither** | not one and not the other | singular | **Neither** candidate **was** qualified. |
| **either of** | + the + plural | singular | **Either of** the options **is** fine. |
| **neither of** | + the + plural | singular (formal) / plural (informal) | **Neither of** the candidates **was/were** selected. |

> You can take **either** route — both lead to the station.
> **Neither** explanation made sense to me.
> **Neither of** the proposals **was** approved. *(formal — singular)*
> **Neither of** the proposals **were** approved. *(informal — plural accepted)*

⚠️ "Neither" is for **two** items only. For three or more, use "none":
- ❌ **Neither** of the three candidates was selected.
- ✅ **None** of the three candidates was selected.

### No / None

**No** is a determiner used before a noun. **None** is a pronoun used without a noun (or with "of").

| Word | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **no** | no + noun | There is **no** evidence to support that claim. |
| **none** | none (pronoun) / none of + the + noun | **None** of the evidence supports that claim. I asked for help but found **none**. |

> There is **no** time left. *(determiner before noun)*
> **None** of the candidates qualified. *(pronoun — replaces "no candidates")*

"None of" verb agreement — singular or plural?

| Register | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Formal/academic | singular | None of the evidence **was** compelling. |
| Informal | plural accepted | None of the students **were** ready. |

### Any — three different uses

**Any** has three distinct meanings depending on context:

| Context | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Negatives/questions | existence in doubt | Is there **any** milk? I don't have **any** time. |
| Conditional | "if there is" | If you have **any** questions, ask now. |
| Free choice (positive) | "it doesn't matter which" | **Any** student can apply. / You can sit **anywhere**. |

> **Any** doctor will tell you that sleep is important. *(free choice — it doesn't matter which doctor)*
> **Any** mistake could be costly. *(general — even the smallest one)*

### Another / Other / Others / The other(s)

These are frequently confused. The key is **singular vs. plural** and **specific vs. non-specific**.

| Word | Noun type | Specific? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **another** | singular countable | non-specific | Can I have **another** cup of coffee? |
| **other** | plural countable / uncountable | non-specific | **Other** students may disagree. |
| **the other** | singular countable | specific (the remaining one) | I lost one glove. **The other** is in my bag. |
| **the others** | plural countable | specific (the remaining ones) | Some students left early; **the others** stayed. |
| **others** | plural countable (pronoun) | non-specific | Some people agree; **others** do not. |

#### Detailed examples

**Another** (= one more, a different one):
> I'd like **another** chance. *(one more chance)*
> That answer is wrong. Try **another**. *(a different answer)*

**Other** (= different, additional — before a noun):
> **Other** students were more prepared. *(different students)*
> Do you have **other** questions?

**The other(s)** (= the remaining one/ones — specific):
> I have two sisters. One lives in Bali; **the other** lives in Surabaya. *(the second/remaining one)*
> Some applicants passed; **the others** were rejected. *(the remaining group)*

**Others** (= some other people/things — pronoun, no noun after it):
> Some believe in fate; **others** believe in free will. *(pronoun — no noun follows)*

### "No other" and "any other"

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| no other | There is **no other** option. |
| any other | Is there **any other** way to solve this? |
| the other | **The other** solution is more expensive. |

### Double determiners — common errors

Some determiner combinations are not allowed:

| Error | Correct |
|---|---|
| ❌ the another | ✅ another (non-specific) / the other (specific) |
| ❌ an other | ✅ another (one word) |
| ❌ the every | ✅ every (no article) |
| ❌ the each | ✅ each / each of the |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian equivalents

| English | Bahasa Indonesia |
|---|---|
| either | salah satu (dari dua) |
| neither | tidak satu pun (dari dua) |
| no (determiner) | tidak ada / tidak punya |
| none | tidak satupun / tidak ada |
| any (free choice) | mana saja / siapa saja |
| another | satu lagi / yang lain |
| other | lain / lainnya |
| the other | yang satunya lagi / yang lainnya |
| others | yang lain (orang lain) |

### "Either/Neither" — Indonesian has no direct equivalent

Indonesian doesn't have a direct structural equivalent for "either/neither" as a determiner. *Salah satu dari keduanya* (one of the two) and *tidak satu pun dari keduanya* (not one of the two) are approximations. The singular verb requirement in English is the key learning point.

### "Another" vs. "other" — "lain" in Indonesian

Both map to *lain* in Indonesian, but the grammar differs:

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Saya ingin satu lagi. | I'd like **another** one. *(singular)* |
| Siswa lain mungkin tidak setuju. | **Other** students may disagree. *(plural, before noun)* |
| Yang lainnya sudah pergi. | **The others** have left. *(pronoun, remaining group)* |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "neither" for more than two items**
- ❌ Neither of the three proposals was accepted.
- ✅ **None** of the three proposals was accepted.

**2. "Neither" with plural verb (formal writing)**
- ❌ Neither candidate were selected. *(formal context)*
- ✅ Neither candidate **was** selected.

**3. Writing "an other" instead of "another"**
- ❌ Could I have an other piece?
- ✅ Could I have **another** piece?

**4. "The another" — double determiner error**
- ❌ I have two pens. **The another** is red.
- ✅ **The other** is red. *(specific — the remaining one)*

**5. "Others" followed by a noun**
- ❌ Others students disagreed with the conclusion.
- ✅ **Other** students disagreed. *(before noun → "other")*
- ✅ **Others** disagreed. *(pronoun — no noun)*

**6. Using "either" for three or more options**
- ❌ You can use **either** of the three methods.
- ✅ You can use **any** of the three methods.

**7. "No" vs. "none"**
- ❌ **No** of the students passed.
- ✅ **None** of the students passed.
- ❌ There is **none** evidence.
- ✅ There is **no** evidence. *(before noun → "no")*

**8. "Any" in free-choice positive context — not negative meaning**
- "**Any** student can apply." *(correct — free choice, it doesn't matter which)*
- Not the same as "I don't have **any** students." *(negative)*

## Quick Summary

- **Either** = one or the other (of two) — singular verb. **Neither** = neither of two — singular verb (formal).
- **Either/Neither** = exactly two only. For 3+, use **any/none**.
- **No** = determiner before noun. **None** = pronoun (standalone or "none of the").
- **Any** = negatives/questions OR free choice ("any student can apply").
- **Another** = one more / a different one (singular). Never "an other" or "the another".
- **Other** = different/additional (before plural noun or uncountable).
- **The other** = the remaining one (specific, singular). **The others** = the remaining ones (specific, plural).
- **Others** = pronoun, no noun after it. ("Some agree; **others** don't.")
