## Explanation

English has **two main ways** to talk about the simple future: **will** and **going to**. They are not interchangeable — each one has specific uses. Choosing the wrong one is one of the most common mistakes Indonesian learners make, because Bahasa Indonesia uses just one word (*akan*) for both.

> "I **will help** you." — spontaneous decision made right now.
> "I **am going to study** medicine." — plan already decided before this moment.
> "It **will rain** tomorrow." — prediction based on general knowledge.
> "Look at those clouds — it **is going to rain**." — prediction with visible evidence.

### "will" — the spontaneous future

Use **will** for:

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Spontaneous decision (made at the moment of speaking) | "The phone is ringing." — "I**'ll** get it!" |
| Promise | I **will** always be here for you. |
| Offer | **I'll** carry that for you. |
| Prediction (general belief, no evidence in front of you) | I think it **will** be a great year. |
| Threat or warning | If you do that again, you **will** regret it. |
| Requests with "will you" | **Will** you help me with this? |

### "going to" — the planned future

Use **going to** for:

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Plan already decided before speaking | I**'m going to** apply for that job next week. |
| Intention | She**'s going to** learn to drive this year. |
| Prediction with visible evidence | Look at him — he**'s going to** fall off that ladder! |
| Things that are already set in motion | The economy**'s going to** recover — the signals are already there. |

### Form

#### will

```
subject + will + V1
```

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | subject + will + V1 | I **will call** you later. |
| Negative | subject + will not / won't + V1 | She **won't come** to the party. |
| Question | Will + subject + V1? | **Will** you **be** there? |
| Wh- question | Wh- + will + subject + V1? | **When will** she **arrive**? |

**Contractions** (very common in spoken English and TOEFL listening):
- I will → **I'll** /aɪl/
- You will → **You'll**
- He/She will → **He'll / She'll**
- We will → **We'll**
- They will → **They'll**
- will not → **won't** /woʊnt/

⚠️ After *will*, the verb is **always V1** (base form). Never add -s or conjugate:
- ❌ She **will goes**.
- ✅ She **will go**.

#### going to

```
subject + am/is/are + going to + V1
```

| Subject | "to be" | + going to + V1 |
|---|---|---|
| I | **am** | going to study |
| He / She / It | **is** | going to study |
| You / We / They | **are** | going to study |

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Positive | I **am going to** start a new job. |
| Negative | He **isn't going to** attend. / He **is not going to** attend. |
| Question | **Are** you **going to** join us? |
| Wh- question | **What are** you **going to** do? |

⚠️ *going to* still needs the right form of **to be** — don't drop it:
- ❌ She **going to** call you.
- ✅ She **is going to** call you.

### "will" vs "going to" — the decision chart

| Situation | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| You decided right now | **will** | "The food's here." — "Great, I**'ll** pay." |
| You decided before, it's a plan | **going to** | "I**'m going to** cook dinner tonight" (planned earlier) |
| General prediction, no evidence | **will** | "I think robots **will** replace some jobs." |
| Prediction + visible evidence | **going to** | "Watch out! You**'re going to** spill that!" |
| Promise | **will** | "I **won't** forget your birthday." |
| Offer/request | **will** | "**Will** you open the window?" |

### Future time markers

These signal future tense:
- **tomorrow**, the day after tomorrow
- **next** week / month / year / Monday
- **in** + time: in three hours, in two weeks, in 2030
- **soon**, **shortly**
- **later** (today)
- **this** weekend / evening / afternoon
- **by** + deadline: by Friday, by the end of the year

### Present Continuous for arranged future

When a future event is **already arranged** (especially with another person), present continuous is also correct — and often more natural:

> "We **are meeting** the client on Thursday." *(arrangement already made)*
> "I **am flying** to Bali next week." *(ticket booked, time set)*

This is the same as *going to* for arranged plans — both are acceptable.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### *akan* and *mau* — one word covering two English structures

Indonesian uses *akan* (will/going to) for formal future and *mau* (want to / going to) for informal intent. Neither captures the will/going to distinction:

| Indonesian | Likely English | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Saya **akan** membantu kamu. | I **will** help you. | Promise/offer → will |
| Saya **akan** belajar kedokteran. | I**'m going to** study medicine. | Plan already decided → going to |
| **Akan** hujan besok. | It **will** rain tomorrow. | General prediction → will |
| Lihat awan itu — **akan** hujan. | It**'s going to** rain. | Visible evidence → going to |

Because *akan* translates both, Indonesian learners often pick one English form and use it everywhere. The fix is to read the **context** — not just translate the word.

### *mau* — intent and plan

*Mau* is informal and maps most naturally to *going to*:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Saya **mau** pergi ke Jakarta minggu depan. | I**'m going to** go to Jakarta next week. |
| Dia **mau** berhenti kerja. | She**'s going to** quit her job. |

### *nanti* — later/eventually

*Nanti* is a time marker meaning "later" — it doesn't determine will vs going to. Use context:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Nanti saya **telepon** kamu. (spontaneous) | I**'ll** call you later. |
| Nanti saya **sudah** siapkan semuanya. (already planned) | I**'m going to** have everything ready later. |

### Side-by-side: future markers

| Feature | Bahasa Indonesia | English will | English going to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Future marker | akan / mau / nanti | will (+ V1) | am/is/are going to (+ V1) |
| Verb changes? | ❌ Never | ❌ V1 stays base | ❌ V1 stays base |
| "to be" needed? | ❌ No | ❌ No | ✅ Yes (am/is/are) |
| Contraction? | — | I'll, won't | I'm gonna (informal) |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using *will* for all future contexts (ignoring *going to*)**
- ❌ I **will** go to the dentist next Tuesday. *(appointment already booked)*
- ✅ I**'m going to** go to the dentist next Tuesday.

  *Why it happens:* Indonesian *akan* covers both. Learners default to *will* as the "future word."

**2. Using *going to* for spontaneous decisions**
- ❌ "There's no milk." — "Oh, I**'m going to** buy some." *(decision made just now)*
- ✅ "There's no milk." — "Oh, I**'ll** buy some."

  *Why it happens:* *going to* feels "more complete" as a phrase, so learners overuse it.

**3. Adding -s after *will***
- ❌ She **will goes** home.
- ✅ She **will go** home.

  *Why it happens:* present simple habit (she goes) bleeds into the future form. After *will*, always use V1.

**4. Dropping "to be" before *going to***
- ❌ She **going to** study tonight.
- ❌ They **going to** travel next month.
- ✅ She **is going to** study tonight.
- ✅ They **are going to** travel next month.

  *Why it happens:* in Indonesian, you don't need a verb before *mau/akan*. In English, *going to* is always preceded by *am/is/are*.

**5. Writing *gonna* in formal writing**
- ❌ I **gonna** call you. *(informal spoken only)*
- ✅ I **am going to** call you. *(written/formal)*
- ✅ I**'m gonna** call you. *(informal spoken only — never in TOEFL writing)*

**6. Using present simple instead of future for scheduled events**
- ❌ The concert **is** tomorrow night at 8pm.
- ✅ The concert **is** tomorrow night at 8pm. *(actually OK for schedules!)*
- ✅ The concert **will be** / **is going to be** tomorrow. *(also correct)*

  Note: present simple is valid for fixed schedules (timetables, programs). This is an exception, not a mistake.

**7. *won't* vs *isn't going to***
Both mean "will not," but:
- **won't** = spontaneous refusal or promise not to do something
- **isn't/aren't going to** = a plan not to do something

> "I **won't** tell anyone." *(promise)*
> "I**'m not going to** apply for that job." *(decision already made)*

## Quick Summary

- **will + V1**: spontaneous decisions, promises, offers, general predictions, requests.
- **am/is/are going to + V1**: plans already decided, intentions, predictions with visible evidence.
- *will* negative = **won't**; *going to* negative = **isn't/aren't going to**.
- After *will*, the verb is **always V1** — never add -s.
- *going to* always needs **am/is/are** before it — never drop "to be."
- Indonesian *akan* = could be either; read the context to choose.
- Indonesian *mau* = usually *going to* (intent/plan).
- For arranged future plans, **present continuous** is also acceptable (*I'm meeting her tomorrow*).
