## Explanation

The **present continuous** (also called present progressive) describes an
action that is **happening now** or **around now**. The action is in progress
— it has started but has not finished.

> "I **am writing** an email." — happening at this moment.
> "She **is studying** for the IELTS exam this month." — around now (not necessarily this exact second).
> "We **are meeting** Sarah at 7pm tomorrow." — arranged future plan.

This tense is the natural partner to the **present simple**. Where present
simple is for the *big picture* of your life, present continuous is for the
*action right now* or *temporary situations*.

> Compare with [Present Simple](/modules/present-simple) — they have very different uses.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Action happening at this exact moment | The kids **are sleeping**. Look — it **is raining**. |
| Action around the present (not this second) | I **am reading** a great book this week. |
| Temporary situation (not permanent) | She **is staying** with her aunt while her house is repaired. |
| Changing/developing situation | The climate **is getting** warmer. Prices **are rising**. |
| Arranged future plan (with a time marker) | We **are flying** to Bali on Saturday. |
| Annoying habits with "always" | He **is always interrupting** me! *(complaint)* |

### Form — am / is / are + Ving

The present continuous always has **two parts**: a form of **"to be"** + the **-ing form** of the main verb.

#### Positive

```
subject + am/is/are + Ving
```

| Subject | "to be" | + Ving |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | working |
| You / We / They | are | working |
| He / She / It | is | working |

> I **am writing** a letter. You **are watching** TV. She **is cooking**.

#### Negative

```
subject + am/is/are + not + Ving
```

> I **am not working** today. (or *I'm not working today*)
> She **isn't listening**.
> They **aren't coming** to the party.

⚠️ **Never use do/does** with present continuous — *to be* makes its own negatives.
- ❌ I **don't am** working.
- ✅ I **am not** working.

#### Question

```
Am/Is/Are + subject + Ving ?
Wh-word + am/is/are + subject + Ving ?
```

> **Am** I **doing** this right?
> **Is** she **studying** in her room?
> **What are** you **eating**?

⚠️ Same rule — no *do/does*:
- ❌ **Do** you **are** working?
- ✅ **Are** you working?

### Spelling rules for the -ing form

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: just add **-ing** | work → work**ing**, play → play**ing** |
| Verbs ending in silent **-e**: drop the e, add **-ing** | live → liv**ing**, write → writ**ing**, make → mak**ing** |
| One syllable, CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant): **double** the final consonant | run → ru**nning**, swim → swi**mming**, sit → si**tting** |
| Two syllables, stress on last + CVC: double the consonant | begin → begi**nning**, prefer → prefe**rring** |
| Two syllables, stress on first + CVC: do **NOT** double | open → ope**ning**, listen → liste**ning** |
| Verbs ending in **-ie**: change ie to **y**, add -ing | lie → l**ying**, die → d**ying**, tie → t**ying** |
| Verbs ending in **-c**: add **-king** | picnic → picnic**king** |

### Stative verbs — verbs that DON'T usually take -ing

Some verbs describe **states**, not actions. They almost never appear in continuous form, even when the meaning is "right now."

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Mental state | know, understand, believe, remember, forget, mean, agree |
| Emotion | love, like, hate, prefer, want, need |
| Possession | have *(when meaning "own")*, own, belong, possess |
| Senses | see, hear, smell, taste *(involuntary perception)* |
| Existence | be *(usually)*, seem, appear, exist |

> ❌ I **am knowing** the answer.
> ✅ I **know** the answer.

> ❌ She **is loving** chocolate.
> ✅ She **loves** chocolate.

⚠️ Some verbs are stative *or* active depending on meaning:
- I **have** a car. *(possession — stative)*
- I **am having** lunch. *(action of eating — active, OK)*
- I **see** the bus coming. *(perceiving — stative)*
- I **am seeing** my doctor tomorrow. *(meeting — active, OK)*

### Common time markers

These signal that present continuous is needed:

- **now**, **right now**, **at this moment**, **at the moment**
- **today**, **tonight**, **this week / month / year**
- **currently**, **these days**, **nowadays**
- **still** (an action in progress)
- **Look!**, **Listen!** — present continuous is what follows
- For arranged future: a future time marker (*tomorrow, on Friday, next week*)

> *Look* — it **is snowing**! *Right now* I **am studying** for my IELTS.

### Present Simple vs Present Continuous — the big comparison

| Use | Present Simple | Present Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Habits & routines | I **drink** coffee every morning. | — |
| Permanent state | She **lives** in Bandung. | — |
| Universal facts | Water **boils** at 100°C. | — |
| Action at this moment | — | She **is drinking** coffee right now. |
| Temporary situation | — | He **is staying** with us this week. |
| Changing situation | — | Prices **are rising**. |
| Mental state | I **know** her. | ❌ I am knowing her. |
| Arranged future | — | We **are leaving** at 8pm. |
| Annoying habit | — | He **is always complaining**. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### How Indonesian expresses "in progress"

In Bahasa Indonesia, you mark an action as in-progress by adding a small word **before the verb**, but the **verb itself doesn't change**:

| Indonesian word | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **sedang** | currently doing | Saya **sedang** makan. *(I am eating.)* |
| **lagi** *(informal)* | currently doing | Dia **lagi** belajar. *(He is studying.)* |
| **tengah** *(formal)* | in the middle of | Mereka **tengah** rapat. *(They are meeting.)* |

In English, the same idea requires **two grammatical changes** at once:

1. Add a form of **"to be"** (am / is / are)
2. Add **-ing** to the main verb

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Saya **sedang** **makan**. | I **am** **eating**. |
| Dia **lagi** **belajar**. | He / She **is** **studying**. |
| Mereka **sedang tidak** bekerja. | They **are not** working. |
| Apakah kamu **sedang** belajar? | **Are** you study**ing**? |

### Common traps

#### Trap 1: forgetting "to be"

Indonesian's *sedang* is enough on its own. English needs both *am/is/are* AND *-ing*.

- ❌ I **eating** rice. *(missing "am")*
- ❌ I **am eat** rice. *(missing "-ing")*
- ✅ I **am eating** rice.

#### Trap 2: using continuous for permanent states

In Indonesian, you can naturally say *"Saya sedang tinggal di Jakarta"* even
for your permanent home. In English, **permanent = present simple**.

| Situation | Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|---|
| You live there permanently | Saya **tinggal** di Jakarta. | I **live** in Jakarta. |
| You're staying temporarily | Saya **sedang tinggal** di Jakarta sampai bulan depan. | I **am staying** in Jakarta until next month. |

The English **continuous** signals "this is temporary." The English **simple** signals "this is my normal life."

#### Trap 3: applying -ing to stative verbs

This trap is **specifically** Indonesian: in Bahasa Indonesia, you can say *"Saya sedang tahu"* awkwardly but understandably — there's no rule forbidding it. English **forbids** continuous form for *know, want, need, like, love, understand*.

- ❌ I **am knowing** the answer.
- ✅ I **know** the answer.

### Side-by-side: what changes

| Feature | Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|---|
| Marker word for "in progress" | sedang / lagi / tengah | — *(no marker word)* |
| Verb changes form? | ❌ Never | ✅ Yes — adds -ing |
| Auxiliary "to be" needed? | ❌ Never | ✅ Always (am / is / are) |
| Stative verbs allowed? | ✅ Sometimes | ❌ No |
| Same form for habit & "now"? | ✅ Yes (just drop *sedang*) | ❌ No — different tenses |

### Translation strategy

When translating an Indonesian sentence with *sedang / lagi / tengah*:

1. **Drop the marker word** (sedang / lagi / tengah). English doesn't have one.
2. **Add the right form of "to be"** based on the subject (am / is / are).
3. **Add -ing** to the main verb. Watch the spelling rules.
4. **Check if the verb is stative.** If yes, use present simple instead.

## Common Mistakes

These trace back to two roots: **the verb doesn't change in Indonesian** and **Indonesian uses a marker word, not an auxiliary**.

**1. Forgetting "to be"**
- ❌ I **studying** for the test.
- ❌ She **working** at the hospital.
- ✅ I **am studying** for the test.
- ✅ She **is working** at the hospital.

  *Why it happens:* Indonesian's *sedang* is one word, so learners only translate one English word. English needs two: *am/is/are* + Ving.

**2. Forgetting -ing**
- ❌ I **am eat** rice now.
- ❌ They **are watch** the movie.
- ✅ I **am eating** rice now.
- ✅ They **are watching** the movie.

  *Why it happens:* the main verb in Indonesian doesn't change, so learners forget the -ing form. The verb must change in English.

**3. Using do/does with continuous**
- ❌ **Do** you **are** working?
- ❌ I **don't am** studying.
- ✅ **Are** you working?
- ✅ I **am not** studying.

  *Why it happens:* over-applying the do/does rule from present simple. "to be" makes its own questions and negatives.

**4. Wrong "to be" form**
- ❌ I **is** working.
- ❌ They **am** coming.
- ❌ She **are** sleeping.
- ✅ I **am** working. / They **are** coming. / She **is** sleeping.

  *Why it happens:* "to be" forms aren't intuitive when there's no equivalent in Indonesian.

**5. -ing on stative verbs**
- ❌ I **am knowing** her.
- ❌ He **is wanting** to go home.
- ❌ She **is loving** the music.
- ✅ I **know** her. / He **wants** to go home. / She **loves** the music.

  *Why it happens:* "right now" feels like it should be continuous. But stative verbs use present simple even for "right now."

**6. Continuous for permanent situations**
- ❌ I **am living** in Jakarta. *(when describing your permanent home)*
- ✅ I **live** in Jakarta. *(permanent)*
- ✅ I **am living** in Jakarta. *(only if it's temporary — e.g., for an internship)*

  *Why it happens:* "sedang tinggal" works in Indonesian for any situation. In English, continuous = temporary.

**7. Wrong -ing spelling**
- ❌ runing, swiming, writeing, lieing
- ✅ running, swimming, writing, lying

  *Why it happens:* the spelling rules (CVC doubling, drop -e, ie → y) aren't obvious. Memorize the rules above.

**8. Adverb position with continuous**
- ❌ I am **always** **complaining** is ok, but: She **always is** working.
- ✅ She **is always** working. *(adverb after "to be")*

  *Why it happens:* in continuous, the adverb still goes **after** "to be," not before it.
