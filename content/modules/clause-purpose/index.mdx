## Explanation

**Purpose clauses** answer the question "**Why?**" in terms of the intended outcome of an action — not the cause, but the **goal** or **aim** of doing something.

> She left early **so that she could catch the train**. *(the purpose of leaving early)*
> He studies every day **in order to pass the TOEFL**. *(the goal of studying)*

### Structure 1 — "so that" + clause

```
Main clause + so that + subject + modal + base verb
```

The modal in the "so that" clause expresses ability (can/could) or necessity (will/would):

| Context | Modal | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present/future | can / will | She runs daily **so that** she **can** stay healthy. |
| Past | could / would | He saved money **so that** he **could** travel. |
| Negative purpose | won't / wouldn't | She spoke quietly **so that** she **wouldn't** wake the baby. |
| Formal | may / might | He submitted early **so that** the committee **might** review it. |

> She spoke quietly **so that** she **wouldn't** disturb anyone.
> They simplified the form **so that** applicants **could** complete it easily.

### Structure 2 — "in order to" + infinitive

```
Subject + verb + in order to + base verb
```

Use **in order to** when the subject of the purpose clause is the **same** as the main clause subject. It is more formal than "to" alone.

> She left early **in order to** catch the train. *(she left + she catches = same subject)*
> He saves money **in order to** travel abroad.

In everyday English, the plain infinitive ("to") is used:
> She left early **to** catch the train.

Both are correct. "In order to" is preferred in formal/academic writing.

**Negative form — "in order not to":**
> She spoke quietly **in order not to** wake the baby. *(formal)*
> She spoke quietly **so as not to** wake the baby. *(formal alternative)*

### Structure 3 — "in order that" + clause (formal)

```
Main clause + in order that + subject + modal + base verb
```

"In order that" is the formal version of "so that." It is common in academic and legal writing.

> The data was encrypted **in order that** users' privacy **might** be protected.
> Guidelines were issued **in order that** all staff **could** follow the same procedure.

### Structure 4 — "for fear that" / "lest" (negative purpose, formal)

These express a **negative purpose** — doing something to avoid an unwanted outcome.

> She whispered **for fear that** she **might** wake the guards. *(afraid of waking them)*
> He double-checked the calculations, **lest** there **be** any errors. *(subjunctive — formal/archaic)*

### Summary of purpose structures

| Structure | Formality | Same subject? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| to / in order to | neutral / formal | Yes | She left early **to** catch the train. |
| so that + clause | neutral | Can differ | She left early **so that** he **could** drive the car. |
| in order that + clause | formal | Can differ | … **in order that** they **could** proceed. |
| in order not to | formal | Yes | … **in order not to** be late. |
| so as not to | formal | Yes | … **so as not to** disturb. |
| for fear that | formal | Can differ | … **for fear that** he **might** return. |

### "So that" vs. "so" — a critical distinction

**"So that"** = purpose.
**"So"** = result (coordinating conjunction).

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| so that | purpose / aim | She left early **so that** she could rest. |
| so | result / consequence | She was tired, **so** she left early. |

- ❌ She left early **so** she could catch the train. *(technically "result" reading — borderline; prefer "so that" for purpose)*
- ✅ She left early **so that** she could catch the train. *(clear purpose)*

### "To" vs. "for" — common confusion

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **to** + verb (purpose) | She went to the library **to** study. |
| **for** + noun (purpose/benefit) | She went to the library **for** some books. |

- ❌ She went to study **for** improving her grades.
- ✅ She went to study **to** improve her grades. *(or "in order to improve")*
- ✅ She studied **for** a better grade. *(noun after "for")*

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Indonesian purpose expressions

| Bahasa Indonesia | English equivalent |
|---|---|
| agar / supaya | so that |
| untuk | to / in order to |
| demi | for the sake of (purpose) |
| agar tidak | so as not to / in order not to |

### Direct mapping

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Dia belajar keras agar bisa lulus. | She studies hard **so that** she **can** pass. |
| Dia belajar keras untuk lulus. | She studies hard **to** pass. / She studies hard **in order to** pass. |
| Dia berbicara pelan agar tidak membangunkan bayinya. | She spoke quietly **so as not to** wake her baby. |

### Tense in "so that" clause

In Indonesian, there is no tense change for the modal in the purpose clause. In English, the modal must match the time frame:

| Time | Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|---|
| Present/future | agar dia **bisa** lulus | so that she **can** pass |
| Past | agar dia **bisa** lulus | so that she **could** pass |

Indonesian uses *bisa* in both cases. English requires *can* for present/future and *could* for past.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "so" instead of "so that" for purpose**
- ❌ She studies hard **so** she can pass the exam. *(reads as result)*
- ✅ She studies hard **so that** she can pass the exam. *(clear purpose)*

**2. Wrong modal tense in "so that" clause**
- ❌ She left early **so that** she **can** catch the train. *(past → use could)*
- ✅ She left early **so that** she **could** catch the train.

**3. Different subjects but using "in order to" (requires same subject)**
- ❌ She explained it simply **in order to** students understand.
- ✅ She explained it simply **so that** students **could** understand.
- ✅ She explained it simply **in order for** students **to** understand.

**4. "For" + verb instead of "to" + verb for purpose**
- ❌ She went to the gym **for** improve her fitness.
- ✅ She went to the gym **to** improve her fitness.

**5. "In order not to" confusion**
- ❌ He spoke quietly **in order to not** disturb anyone.
- ✅ He spoke quietly **in order not to** disturb anyone. *("not" goes before "to")*

**6. Leaving out the modal in "so that" clause**
- ❌ She studied hard **so that** she pass.
- ✅ She studied hard **so that** she **could** pass.

## Quick Summary

- **to / in order to** + infinitive: same subject, formal alternative to plain "to".
- **so that** + subject + modal: can have a different subject. Present/future → can/will; past → could/would.
- **in order that** + clause: formal version of "so that" — academic writing.
- **in order not to / so as not to**: negative purpose with same subject.
- **for fear that**: negative purpose — to avoid an unwanted outcome.
- **"so that"** = purpose | **"so"** = result — do not confuse.
- **"to"** before a verb (purpose) | **"for"** before a noun (purpose/benefit).
- If subjects differ in purpose clause, use **"so that"**, not "in order to".
