## Explanation

**Should** expresses advice, recommendation, expectation, and mild obligation. **Ought to** has the same meaning but is slightly more formal and less common in spoken English. Both are followed by V1.

### Advice and recommendation

```
subject + should / ought to + V1
```

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Giving advice | You **should** see a doctor about that cough. |
| Making a recommendation | They **should** submit the application early. |
| Suggesting what is right | Students **ought to** attend all seminars. |

### Expectation — "should" for what is expected

"Should" also expresses what you expect to happen or what is normal/expected:

> The package **should** arrive tomorrow — I paid for express delivery.
> She **should** know the answer — she's the expert.
> The meeting **should** start at 9 am.

This is not obligation — it's a prediction based on what is normal or arranged.

### Negative — advice against / not expected

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| shouldn't + V1 | You **shouldn't** work so late — it affects your health. |
| ought not to + V1 | They **ought not to** ignore the warning signs. |

### Questions with "should"

"Should" is used in questions to ask for advice or to confirm what is expected:

> **Should** I bring anything to the meeting?
> **Should** we wait for him, or start without him?
> **Should** I send the invoice now?

### Should vs must vs have to

| Modal | Strength | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| should | Mild | Advice / expectation — doing otherwise is possible but inadvisable |
| must | Strong | Obligation — not doing it is wrong or forbidden |
| have to | Strong | External rule — not doing it has consequences |

> You **should** submit early. *(advisable)*
> You **must** submit before midnight. *(deadline — rule)*
> You **have to** submit through the portal. *(institutional requirement)*

### Ought to vs should

"Ought to" has the same meaning as "should" but is slightly more formal:

| Should | Ought to |
|---|---|
| You should apologise. | You **ought to** apologise. |
| He should take a break. | He **ought to** take a break. |

"Ought to" is less common in American English and in questions/negatives.

### Summary table

| Function | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Advice (positive) | should + V1 | You **should** rest. |
| Advice (negative) | shouldn't + V1 | You **shouldn't** skip meals. |
| Expectation | should + V1 | It **should** be ready by noon. |
| Formal advice | ought to + V1 | You **ought to** reconsider. |
| Question for advice | Should + subject + V1? | **Should** I apply? |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Sebaiknya" / "Seharusnya"

Indonesian uses **sebaiknya** (it would be better) and **seharusnya** (it should be / ideally):

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Sebaiknya kamu istirahat. | You **should** rest. |
| Seharusnya dia tahu. | She **should** know. |
| Kamu sebaiknya jangan datang terlambat. | You **shouldn't** arrive late. |
| Apakah sebaiknya saya melamar? | **Should** I apply? |

### Interference errors

**1. Confusing "should" (advice) with "must" (obligation)**

Indonesians sometimes use *harus* (must) where *sebaiknya* (should) is more appropriate:
- Too strong: You **must** see a doctor.
- More natural for advice: You **should** see a doctor.

**2. Omitting "to" in "ought to"**

*Seharusnya* → "ought to" (with "to"):
- ❌ You ought apologise.
- ✅ You **ought to** apologise.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "should" without V1 (adding "to")**
- ❌ You should to rest more.
- ✅ You **should** rest more.
- *Why:* "Should" is a modal — followed by V1 directly, never "to".

**2. Using "should" for strong obligation (should ≠ must)**
- ❌ You should not park here. *(mild)*
- ✅ You **must not** park here. *(prohibition)*
- *Why:* "Shouldn't" = advisable not to; "must not" = forbidden. Don't understate prohibitions.

**3. "Ought to" without "to"**
- ❌ You ought apologise to her.
- ✅ You **ought to** apologise to her.
- *Why:* Unlike other modals, "ought" always takes "to" — "ought to + V1".

**4. Using "should" for logical deduction (use "must")**
- ❌ She should be home — her car is there.
- ✅ She **must** be home — her car is there. *(strong deduction)*
- *Why:* Evidence-based deduction = "must". "Should" implies expectation (what you think is normal), not reasoning from evidence.

**5. Adding "-s" after "should"**
- ❌ She shoulds take care of herself.
- ✅ She **should** take care of herself.
- *Why:* Modals never inflect. No "-s" for third person singular.

**6. "Should" for past expectation (use "should have")**
- ❌ The parcel should arrive yesterday.
- ✅ The parcel **should have** arrived yesterday. *(expected but didn't happen)*
- *Why:* Past expectation that wasn't fulfilled uses "should have + V3".

**7. Double modal**
- ❌ You should must see a doctor.
- ✅ You **should** see a doctor. / You **must** see a doctor.
- *Why:* One modal per clause. Choose the one that fits the meaning.

## Quick Summary

- **Should + V1** = advice, recommendation, expectation
- **Shouldn't + V1** = advise against, not expected
- **Ought to + V1** = same as should, slightly more formal — always needs "to"
- **Should** ≠ **must**: should = advisable; must = obligatory
- **Should** for expectation: "The report **should** be ready soon" = I expect it to be
- "Should + V1" never has "to": never "should to go"
- Past unfulfilled expectation: **should have + V3** — "It should have arrived"
- Questions: "**Should** I…?" = asking for advice or confirmation
