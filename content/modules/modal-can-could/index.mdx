## Explanation

**Can** and **could** are the most common modal verbs in English. They express **ability**, **permission**, and **possibility**. Could is the past form of can, but it also carries a sense of politeness and tentativeness.

### Ability

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present ability | can + V1 | She **can** speak three languages. |
| Past ability | could + V1 | I **could** swim when I was five. |
| Negative present | cannot / can't + V1 | He **can't** drive yet. |
| Negative past | couldn't + V1 | They **couldn't** afford it then. |

> "Can" describes a present or general ability. "Could" describes an ability that existed at a specific time in the past.

### Permission

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Asking permission (neutral) | **Can** I use your phone? |
| Asking permission (polite) | **Could** I borrow your notes? |
| Giving permission | You **can** leave early today. |
| Refusing permission | You **can't** park here. |

> "Could" for permission is more polite/formal than "can". In formal writing, "may" is even more formal.

### Possibility

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Present/future possibility | It **can** get very cold here in winter. |
| Uncertain possibility | She **could** be in the library — I'm not sure. |
| Negative impossibility | That **can't** be right — I checked twice. |

> "Could" for possibility is more tentative than "can". "Can't" for impossibility expresses logical certainty that something is false.

### Requests — polite use of "could"

| Neutral | Polite |
|---|---|
| Can you help me? | **Could** you help me, please? |
| Can you send the file? | **Could** you send the file? |

> "Could you…?" is a standard polite request form in both spoken and written English.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Bisa" — the Indonesian equivalent

Indonesian uses **bisa** (informal) or **dapat** (formal) for both ability and possibility. English distinguishes them more precisely with can/could.

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Saya bisa berenang. | I **can** swim. (ability) |
| Dia bisa sedang di perpustakaan. | She **could** be in the library. (possibility) |
| Bolehkah saya pergi? | **Can** / **May** I go? (permission) |
| Bisakah kamu membantuku? | **Could** you help me? (polite request) |

### Common interference errors

**1. Using "can" for polite requests (not wrong, but less natural)**

Indonesians say *bisa kamu bantu?* without a formality distinction, so they may use "can" everywhere:
- Acceptable: Can you send the report?
- More natural: **Could** you send the report?

**2. Using "bisa" → "could" for general ability**

*Saya bisa main gitar* = present ability → use **can**, not **could**:
- ❌ I could play the guitar. *(sounds like past ability or tentative)*
- ✅ I **can** play the guitar.

**3. Double modal**

Indonesian sometimes stacks *bisa + boleh*:
- ❌ I can could go tomorrow.
- ✅ I **can** go tomorrow. / I **could** go tomorrow.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using "could" for a confirmed past action (not just ability)**
- ❌ Yesterday I could finish the report.
- ✅ Yesterday I **was able to** finish the report.
- *Why:* "Could" for past ability implies you had the skill, not that you succeeded. For a single completed past event, use "was able to" or "managed to."

**2. "Can't" vs "couldn't" confusion**
- ❌ I can't go to the party last night.
- ✅ I **couldn't** go to the party last night.
- *Why:* Past tense context requires "couldn't", not "can't".

**3. Using "can" for polite/formal permission requests**
- ❌ Can I trouble you for a moment? *(abrupt in formal context)*
- ✅ **Could** I trouble you for a moment?
- *Why:* "Could" signals politeness; use it in professional/formal situations.

**4. Using "could" for general present ability**
- ❌ She could drive — she passed her test last year.
- ✅ She **can** drive — she passed her test last year.
- *Why:* Current ability = "can". "Could" implies past or uncertainty.

**5. Forgetting "not" position**
- ❌ She can not come. *(can not = two words is rare and emphatic)*
- ✅ She **cannot** / **can't** come.
- *Why:* "Cannot" (one word) is the standard negative form. "Can not" (two words) only for strong emphasis.

**6. Using "could" + have for a simple past ability**
- ❌ I could have swim when I was five.
- ✅ I **could** swim when I was five.
- *Why:* "Could have + V3" is a modal perfect (unrealised past action). Simple past ability just uses "could + V1."

**7. Omitting the base verb**
- ❌ She can speaks French.
- ✅ She **can speak** French.
- *Why:* Modals are always followed by the base form (V1), never an inflected form.

## Quick Summary

- **Can** = present/general ability, neutral permission, general possibility
- **Could** = past ability, polite permission request, uncertain/tentative possibility, polite requests
- Polite request: *Could you…?* is more formal than *Can you…?*
- Impossibility (logical): *can't* — "That **can't** be right."
- Modal + V1 (base form) always — never "can speaks", "could went"
- Past completed success: "was able to" / "managed to" (not "could" for single events)
- Double modals are not allowed: never "can could", "might can"
