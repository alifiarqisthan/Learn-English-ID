## Explanation

Some English verbs can be followed by **either** a gerund (V-ing) or a to-infinitive (to + V1). In some cases the meaning is the same; in others, the choice of form **changes the meaning entirely**.

---

### Group A — Same meaning with either form

These verbs can take a gerund or a to-infinitive with **no difference in meaning**.

| Verb | Gerund form | Infinitive form |
|---|---|---|
| begin | She **began crying**. | She **began to cry**. |
| start | He **started running**. | He **started to run**. |
| continue | They **continued working**. | They **continued to work**. |
| hate | I **hate waiting** in queues. | I **hate to wait** in queues. |
| like | She **likes reading**. | She **likes to read**. |
| love | He **loves cooking**. | He **loves to cook**. |
| prefer | I **prefer staying** home. | I **prefer to stay** home. |

> **Practical note:** With "begin", "start", and "continue", the infinitive is slightly more common in formal writing; the gerund is common in speech. The meaning is identical.

---

### Group B — Different meaning depending on the form

These verbs change meaning when followed by a gerund vs. a to-infinitive. This is the most important section of this module.

---

#### stop

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| stop + gerund | cease doing the action (quit) | He **stopped smoking**. *(He no longer smokes.)* |
| stop + to-infinitive | pause in order to do something | He **stopped to smoke**. *(He paused what he was doing in order to have a cigarette.)* |

> **Critical error:** "I stopped to smoke" does NOT mean you quit smoking — it means you paused to have a cigarette. To express quitting: *I stopped smoking.*

---

#### remember

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| remember + gerund | recall a past event you did | I **remember locking** the door. *(I have the memory of locking it.)* |
| remember + to-infinitive | remember to do a task (future duty) | Remember **to lock** the door. *(Don't forget — lock it.)* |

> **Mnemonic:** Gerund = looking **back** (recall). Infinitive = looking **forward** (to-do list).

---

#### forget

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| forget + gerund | forget that you once did something | I'll never **forget meeting** her for the first time. *(The memory stays.)* |
| forget + to-infinitive | fail to do something you should have done | I **forgot to send** the email. *(I should have sent it but didn't.)* |

---

#### try

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| try + gerund | experiment with something / test a method | **Try adding** more salt — it might taste better. *(Experiment.)* |
| try + to-infinitive | make an effort to do something difficult | She **tried to lift** the heavy box. *(She made an effort but it was hard.)* |

---

#### regret

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| regret + gerund | feel sorry about a past action | I **regret saying** that to her. *(I wish I hadn't said it.)* |
| regret + to-infinitive | be sorry to convey news right now (formal) | We **regret to inform** you that your application was unsuccessful. *(Formal announcement.)* |

> "Regret + to-infinitive" is almost always in formal or official communication. "Regret + gerund" is personal and about the past.

---

#### mean

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| mean + gerund | involve / entail (a consequence) | Getting this job **means moving** to a new city. *(It involves moving.)* |
| mean + to-infinitive | intend / plan to do | I **meant to call** you yesterday. *(I intended to call.)* |

---

### Summary contrast table

| Verb | + Gerund | + Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| stop | quit doing | pause in order to do |
| remember | recall doing (past) | remember to do (future task) |
| forget | forget that you did | forget to do (omission) |
| try | experiment with | make an effort to |
| regret | sorry about past action | sorry to say now (formal) |
| mean | involve / entail | intend to do |

## Bahasa Indonesia

Indonesian has no equivalent grammatical distinction. The same Indonesian verb is used regardless of the time frame or nuance, and context carries the meaning.

| English | Indonesian equivalent | Note |
|---|---|---|
| He stopped **smoking**. (quit) | Dia **berhenti merokok**. | Identical form |
| He stopped **to smoke**. (paused) | Dia **berhenti untuk merokok**. | *untuk* shows purpose |
| I remember **locking** the door. | Saya ingat **mengunci** pintunya. | No gerund/infinitive distinction |
| Remember **to lock** the door. | Jangan lupa **mengunci** pintunya. | Context clarifies meaning |

**Why this is hard for Indonesian learners:** Because Indonesian relies on context and adverbs to distinguish these meanings, learners often pick the form randomly — or always choose the infinitive because "to + verb" feels like *untuk + verb*.

**Most dangerous errors:**

| Error | What they meant | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ "I stopped **to smoke**." | They quit smoking | ✅ "I stopped **smoking**." |
| ❌ "I remembered **to call** her yesterday." | They recalled making a past call | ✅ "I remembered **calling** her yesterday." |
| ❌ "I forgot **taking** my medicine." | They failed to take it | ✅ "I forgot **to take** my medicine." |

---

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *She stopped **to eat** meat two years ago.* → ✅ *She stopped **eating** meat two years ago.* — *Why:* "Stop + gerund" means to quit. "Stop + to-infinitive" means she paused to eat, which is the opposite meaning.

2. ❌ *Don't forget **telling** your parents about the results.* → ✅ *Don't forget **to tell** your parents about the results.* — *Why:* "Forget + to-infinitive" refers to a future task not yet done.

3. ❌ *I'll never forget **to meet** her for the first time.* → ✅ *I'll never forget **meeting** her for the first time.* — *Why:* "Forget + gerund" refers to a memory of a past event.

4. ❌ *I **regret to say** that rude comment yesterday.* → ✅ *I **regret saying** that rude comment yesterday.* — *Why:* Regret about a past personal action uses a gerund. "Regret + to-infinitive" is only for formal present announcements.

5. ❌ *We **regret informing** you that your visa has been denied.* → ✅ *We **regret to inform** you that your visa has been denied.* — *Why:* Formal announcements use "regret + to-infinitive".

6. ❌ *Getting a PhD **means to sacrifice** a lot.* → ✅ *Getting a PhD **means sacrificing** a lot.* — *Why:* "Mean + gerund" expresses entailment or consequence.

7. ❌ *She **tried to add** more spice to see if it tasted better.* → ✅ *She **tried adding** more spice to see if it tasted better.* — *Why:* "Try + gerund" means experimenting or testing a method.

8. ❌ *He **tried solving** the lock for an hour but couldn't open it.* → ✅ *He **tried to solve** the problem for an hour but couldn't.* — *Why:* "Try + to-infinitive" expresses effort toward a difficult goal.

## TOEFL Practice

TOEFL questions frequently test the meaning-changing pairs. A sentence will use one form when context clearly requires the other. Focus on:
- The **timeline**: Does the action look back (past) or forward (future)? → remember/forget
- The **intent**: Is the action a cessation or a purpose? → stop
- The **nature**: Is it experimentation or effort? → try

## Quick Summary

- **Same meaning:** begin, start, continue, hate, like, love, prefer — either form is correct.
- **Different meaning pairs** — memorise each:
  - **stop + gerund** = quit; **stop + infinitive** = pause in order to
  - **remember + gerund** = recall the past; **remember + infinitive** = future task
  - **forget + gerund** = forgot you once did; **forget + infinitive** = failed to do
  - **try + gerund** = experiment; **try + infinitive** = make effort
  - **regret + gerund** = sorry about past action; **regret + infinitive** = formal announcement
  - **mean + gerund** = entail; **mean + infinitive** = intend
- Indonesian has no equivalent distinction — meaning comes from context, so learners must memorise each pair.
- The most dangerous error: "I stopped to smoke" ≠ quitting smoking.
- "Remember to lock" = future duty; "I remember locking" = past memory.
