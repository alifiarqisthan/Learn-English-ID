## Explanation

The **present perfect continuous** is the present perfect in motion. Where
the present perfect says *"something happened and is relevant now,"* the
present perfect continuous says *"something has been happening continuously
and is still going — or just stopped."*

> "I **have been studying** for three hours." — I started 3 hours ago, I'm still at it (or just finished).
> "She **has been working** on that project all week." — ongoing action, still in progress.
> "You look tired. **Have you been running**?" — you can see the evidence now.

The key idea is **duration + continuity up to now**. The action was ongoing
over a stretch of time, not just done once.

> Compare with [Present Perfect →](/modules/present-perfect) — which focuses on the result, not the duration.

### When to use it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Duration of an ongoing action (up to now) | I **have been reading** this novel for a week. |
| Repeated action over a period | He **has been calling** me all morning. |
| Activity whose evidence is visible now | Why is the floor wet? Someone **has been washing** it. |
| Emphasize effort or process (not just result) | I**'ve been trying** to fix this bug for hours. |
| After "How long...?" when the action is still ongoing | How long **have** you **been waiting**? |

### Form — have/has + been + Ving

Two parts: **have/has been** (the present perfect of "be") + **V-ing**.

#### Positive

```
subject + have/has + been + Ving
```

| Subject | Auxiliary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **have been** | I **have been studying**. |
| He / She / It | **has been** | She **has been studying**. |

> Watch the -ing spelling rules. See [Present Continuous →](/modules/present-continuous) for the full table (CVC doubling, drop -e, -ie → y).

#### Negative

```
subject + have/has + not + been + Ving
→ haven't been / hasn't been + Ving
```

> I **haven't been sleeping** well lately.
> He **hasn't been attending** class regularly.

#### Question

```
Have/Has + subject + been + Ving ?
Wh-word + have/has + subject + been + Ving ?
```

> **Have** you **been waiting** long?
> **How long has** she **been learning** English?
> **Why have** they **been arguing**?

### Time markers

Present perfect continuous is almost always used with **for** or **since** —
it's fundamentally about duration.

| Marker | Example |
|---|---|
| **for** | I've been living here **for** 3 years. |
| **since** | She's been working there **since** 2020. |
| **all day / all morning / all week** | He's been playing games **all day**. |
| **lately / recently** | I've **been feeling** tired **lately**. |
| **How long...?** | **How long have** you **been waiting**? |

### Stative verbs — the same rule applies

Just as with the present continuous, **stative verbs** (know, want, love,
believe, understand, have = own) cannot use the continuous form. Use present
perfect simple instead.

- ❌ I **have been knowing** him for years.
- ✅ I **have known** him for years.

- ❌ She **has been wanting** a new phone.
- ✅ She **has wanted** a new phone.

### Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous

This is the key distinction at B2 level:

| Aspect | Present Perfect | Present Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Result / completion | Duration / ongoing process |
| Example | I **have written** the report. | I **have been writing** the report. |
| Meaning | The report is done. | I've been busy writing it (may or may not be done). |
| Irregular verbs | need V3 | need Ving (base + -ing) |
| Stative verbs | ✅ I have known him for years. | ❌ I have been knowing him. |

**Short test:** Can you ask "how long?" → use continuous. Is the result the
point? → use simple.

| Sentence | Which? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| She **has read** three chapters. | Perfect simple | Focus: 3 chapters completed |
| She **has been reading** for two hours. | Perfect continuous | Focus: duration |
| I **have lived** here for 5 years. | **Either** | Both are accepted with 'for' for states |
| I **have eaten** today. | Perfect simple | Experience/result |
| I **have been eating** too much lately. | Perfect continuous | Ongoing habit, duration |

⚠️ With "for/since" + state verbs like *live, work, wait*, both forms are
often accepted. But with action verbs, the distinction matters.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### No exact equivalent in Indonesian

Bahasa Indonesia has no grammatical structure that maps cleanly to present
perfect continuous. The closest patterns are:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Saya **sudah belajar selama** 3 jam dan masih belum selesai. | I **have been studying** for 3 hours and I'm not done yet. |
| Dia **terus menelepon** saya dari tadi pagi. | She **has been calling** me all morning. |
| **Dari tadi** saya menunggu. | I **have been waiting** since a while ago. |

Notice that Indonesian uses a combination of time words (*sudah*, *selama*,
*dari tadi*, *terus*) to express what English encodes in a single verb form.

### Why Indonesian learners simplify

Because there's no direct Indonesian structure, learners tend to collapse
present perfect continuous into:

1. **Present continuous** — "I am studying for 3 hours." (wrong — ignores the past start)
2. **Present perfect simple** — "I have studied for 3 hours." (acceptable but loses the emphasis on ongoing activity)
3. **Simple past** — "I studied for 3 hours." (only correct if you've already finished)

### The "sudah + masih" clue

When Indonesian uses both *sudah* (already/have) and *masih* (still), that's
a strong signal English needs present perfect continuous:

| Indonesian | English |
|---|---|
| Saya **sudah** belajar 3 jam tapi **masih** belum mengerti. | I **have been studying** for 3 hours but I still don't understand. |
| Dia **sudah** menunggu lama, **masih** di sini. | She **has been waiting** for a long time, she's still here. |

### Side-by-side: the 4 present tenses

| Indonesian | English tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Saya belajar (kebiasaan) | Present Simple | I **study** every day. |
| Saya **sedang** belajar sekarang | Present Continuous | I **am studying** now. |
| Saya **sudah** belajar (selesai) | Present Perfect | I **have studied** chapter 5. |
| Saya **sudah belajar selama** 2 jam (masih berlangsung) | Present Perfect Continuous | I **have been studying** for 2 hours. |

## Common Mistakes

**1. Using present continuous instead of present perfect continuous**
- ❌ I **am waiting** here for two hours already.
- ✅ I **have been waiting** here for two hours.

  *Why it happens:* Indonesian *sedang* maps to present continuous, but when duration is emphasized, English needs the present perfect continuous.

**2. Using present perfect simple when duration + ongoing is implied**
- ❌ I **have studied** English for 10 years and I'm still learning.
- ✅ I **have been studying** English for 10 years and I'm still learning.

  *Not wrong, but less natural when the ongoing effort is the point.*

**3. Forgetting "been"**
- ❌ I **have working** here since March.
- ❌ She **has studying** all morning.
- ✅ I **have been working** here since March.
- ✅ She **has been studying** all morning.

  *Why it happens:* three-word structure (have + been + Ving) is unusual in Indonesian. Learners drop "been."

**4. Using stative verbs in continuous form**
- ❌ I **have been knowing** her for 5 years.
- ❌ He **has been believing** in hard work all his life.
- ✅ I **have known** her for 5 years.
- ✅ He **has believed** in hard work all his life.

**5. Wrong subject agreement (have vs has)**
- ❌ She **have been** working all day.
- ❌ My sister **have been** trying to reach you.
- ✅ She **has been** working all day.
- ✅ My sister **has been** trying to reach you.

**6. Wrong -ing form**
- ❌ She has been **runing** all morning.
- ❌ He has been **writeing** the report.
- ✅ She has been **running** all morning. (CVC double)
- ✅ He has been **writing** the report. (drop silent -e)

## Quick Summary

- Form: **have/has + been + Ving**. Three parts — don't drop "been."
- Use for: **duration of an ongoing action up to now**. Almost always with *for* or *since*.
- **Not** for stative verbs (know, want, love, understand) → use present perfect simple.
- Present perfect simple = focus on **result/completion**; Present perfect continuous = focus on **duration/ongoing process**.
- Indonesian *sudah…selama*, *dari tadi*, *terus* are clues this tense is needed.
- "How long have you been...?" is the classic present perfect continuous question.
- **have been** for I/you/we/they; **has been** for he/she/it.
