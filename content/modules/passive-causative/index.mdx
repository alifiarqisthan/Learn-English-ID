## Explanation

The **causative** describes situations where the subject **arranges for someone else to do something** — rather than doing it themselves. English has two main causative structures: **have something done** and **get something done**.

### Structure 1 — "Have something done"

```
Subject + have + object + V3
```

The subject arranges for someone else to perform the action on the object.

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| Regular service | She **has** her hair **cut** every month. *(a hairdresser cuts it)* |
| Arranged repair | He **had** his car **serviced** last week. |
| Professional service | They **have** their accounts **audited** annually. |
| Unpleasant experience | She **had** her wallet **stolen** at the market. |

> I need to **have** my laptop **repaired**. *(I'll arrange for a technician to do it)*
> We **had** the house **painted** last summer.

**Tense changes** — only "have" changes, V3 stays constant:

| Tense | Example |
|---|---|
| Present simple | She **has** her nails **done** every week. |
| Past simple | He **had** his bike **fixed** yesterday. |
| Present perfect | I **have had** my eyes **tested** recently. |
| Future | She **will have** her car **serviced** next month. |
| Continuous | He **is having** his house **renovated** right now. |
| Modal | You should **have** this mole **checked** by a doctor. |

### Structure 2 — "Get something done"

```
Subject + get + object + V3
```

"Get something done" is more informal than "have something done" but means the same. It often implies **more effort** was involved.

> I need to **get** my visa **sorted** before the trip.
> She **got** her car **repaired** at the garage.
> He finally **got** the report **finished**. *(sometimes implies the subject did it — context needed)*

### "Have/Get someone do something" — active causative

When you name the person doing the task, the verb after them is the **base form** (not V3):

```
Subject + have/get + person + base verb
```

> I'll **have** the technician **look** at it. *(not "looked")*
> She **got** her assistant **prepare** the report. *(not "prepared")*
> Can you **have** someone **call** me back?

Compare:
- "I **had** my car **repaired**." *(object + V3 — we don't say who did it)*
- "I **had** a mechanic **repair** my car." *(person + base verb — we name who did it)*

### "Have" + person + V3 — unpleasant experience (no choice)

"Have" with a person as object and V3 can also describe something unpleasant that happened to the subject without their arrangement:

> She **had** her phone **stolen**.
> He **had** his leg **broken** in the accident.
> They **had** all their files **deleted** by the virus.

Context distinguishes this from the arranged-service meaning.

### "Get" as passive — informal passive

"Get + V3" (without an object between "get" and V3) functions as an informal passive:

```
Subject + get + V3   (= is/was + V3)
```

> He **got** arrested by the police. *(= He was arrested)*
> She **got** promoted last year. *(= She was promoted)*
> The car **got** damaged in the storm. *(= The car was damaged)*

Common in spoken English. Avoid in formal academic writing.

### Summary

| Structure | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Causative (arranged) | have/get + object + V3 | I **had** my hair **cut**. |
| Causative (named agent) | have/get + person + base verb | I **had** the barber **cut** my hair. |
| Unpleasant experience | have + object + V3 | She **had** her bag **stolen**. |
| Informal passive | get + V3 | He **got** fired. |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### "Minta (seseorang) untuk" / "Disuruh"

Indonesian expresses the causative with *minta... untuk...* (ask someone to), *menyuruh* (have/make someone do), or *diperbaiki* (repaired — passive form):

| Bahasa Indonesia | English |
|---|---|
| Saya minta montir memperbaiki mobil saya. | I **had** a mechanic **repair** my car. |
| Saya memperbaiki mobil saya (di bengkel). | I **had** my car **repaired** (at the garage). |
| Dia memotong rambutnya setiap bulan. | She **has** her hair **cut** every month. |
| Tas saya dicuri. | I **had** my bag **stolen**. / My bag **was stolen**. |

### The V3 vs base verb trap

Indonesian learners often use V3 after the named person, because the whole action feels "passive":

- ❌ I had the technician **repaired** my laptop.
- ✅ I had the technician **repair** my laptop. *(named person → base verb)*
- ✅ I had my laptop **repaired**. *(object → V3)*

### "Get" passive in formal writing

Indonesians sometimes use "get + V3" in academic writing, where "be + V3" is expected:

- ❌ The sample **got** contaminated during transport. *(informal)*
- ✅ The sample **was** contaminated during transport. *(formal passive)*

## Common Mistakes

**1. V3 after a named person (should be base verb)**
- ❌ I had the technician **looked** at it.
- ✅ I had the technician **look** at it.

**2. Base verb after object (should be V3)**
- ❌ I had my car **repair** at the garage.
- ✅ I had my car **repaired** at the garage.

**3. Missing the object between "have" and V3**
- ❌ I had repaired my car.
- ✅ I had **my car** repaired.

**4. Confusing "have something done" with simple past**
- ❌ I cut my hair yesterday. *(implies you cut it yourself)*
- ✅ I **had** my hair **cut** yesterday. *(a hairdresser did it)*

**5. Using "get passive" in formal writing**
- ❌ The samples **got** analysed at the lab. *(informal)*
- ✅ The samples **were** analysed at the lab.

**6. Wrong tense of "have"**
- ❌ She is **have** her report reviewed.
- ✅ She **is having** her report reviewed.

## Quick Summary

- **Causative**: *have/get + object + V3* — *I had my car repaired.* (someone else did it)
- **Named agent**: *have/get + person + base verb* — *I had a mechanic repair it.*
- **Unpleasant experience**: *have + object + V3* — *She had her phone stolen.*
- **Informal passive**: *get + V3* — *He got promoted.* (avoid in formal writing)
- Key distinction: **object → V3** | **named person → base verb**
- All tenses work; only "have/get" changes: *had repaired / will have repaired / is having repaired*
