## Explanation

Several English verbs can be followed by **either a gerund (-ing)** or **an infinitive (to + verb)**, but the meaning changes significantly depending on which form you use. This is one of the most important distinctions in advanced English grammar.

### The Key Verbs

The seven most important verbs where the form changes the meaning:

**stop / remember / forget / try / regret / mean / go on**

---

### STOP

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| stop + **gerund** | cease an activity permanently or temporarily | She **stopped smoking** last year. (She no longer smokes.) |
| stop + **infinitive** | pause an activity in order to do something else | She stopped **to smoke**. (She paused what she was doing in order to smoke.) |

- He **stopped working** at midnight. (He finished working.)
- He **stopped to work** on the report. (He paused his journey/activity to do the report.)

---

### REMEMBER

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| remember + **gerund** | recall a past action (the action happened before the remembering) | I **remember meeting** her at the conference. (I met her; I recall it now.) |
| remember + **infinitive** | not forget to do a future/planned action | **Remember to lock** the door. (Don't forget — do it.) |

- She **remembers signing** the document. (She knows she signed it.)
- She **remembered to sign** the document. (She did not forget to sign it.)

---

### FORGET

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| forget + **gerund** | fail to recall a past action | I'll never **forget seeing** the Northern Lights. (The memory stays.) |
| forget + **infinitive** | fail to do a planned action | He **forgot to send** the email. (He didn't send it.) |

- She **forgot meeting** him. (She has no memory of meeting him.)
- She **forgot to meet** him. (She failed to attend the meeting.)

---

### TRY

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| try + **gerund** | experiment with an action to see if it helps (one option among several) | **Try adding** more salt. (Experiment — see if it makes it better.) |
| try + **infinitive** | attempt something difficult (may or may not succeed) | He **tried to open** the jar but couldn't. (He made an effort.) |

- **Try calling** her again. (Experiment — see if she answers this time.)
- He **tried to call** her but the line was busy. (He made an attempt, which failed.)

---

### REGRET

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| regret + **gerund** | feel sorry about a past action | She **regrets quitting** her job. (She wishes she hadn't quit.) |
| regret + **infinitive** | be sorry to deliver bad news (formal/written) | We **regret to inform** you that your application was unsuccessful. |

- He **regrets saying** those words. (He feels bad about what he said.)
- We **regret to announce** the cancellation of the event. (Formal notice.)

---

### MEAN

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| mean + **gerund** | involve / entail (as a consequence) | Getting the promotion **means working** longer hours. |
| mean + **infinitive** | intend to do something | I **meant to call** you yesterday but I forgot. |

- Saving money **means spending** less. (It involves or requires spending less.)
- She **meant to apologise** but never did. (She intended to but didn't.)

---

### GO ON

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| go on + **gerund** | continue doing the same thing | He **went on talking** for an hour. (He kept talking — same activity.) |
| go on + **infinitive** | move on to do something new after finishing | After the introduction, she **went on to explain** the main points. |

- The speaker **went on complaining** about the system. (Continued complaining.)
- After graduating, he **went on to become** a doctor. (He then did something new.)

---

### Summary Table

| Verb | + Gerund (-ing) | + Infinitive (to + verb) |
|---|---|---|
| stop | cease an activity | pause in order to do sth |
| remember | recall a past action | not forget a future action |
| forget | fail to recall a past action | fail to do a planned action |
| try | experiment to see if it works | attempt (may fail) |
| regret | feel sorry about a past action | be sorry to say (formal) |
| mean | involve / entail | intend to do |
| go on | continue the same activity | move on to a new activity |

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Perbedaan dengan Bahasa Indonesia

Bahasa Indonesia tidak memiliki perbedaan bentuk seperti gerund dan infinitive. Kata kerja tambahan langsung mengikuti kata kerja utama tanpa perubahan bentuk:
- *Dia berhenti merokok.* → She **stopped smoking**. (correct gerund)
- *Dia berhenti untuk merokok.* → She **stopped to smoke**. (infinitive for purpose)

Dalam bahasa Indonesia, perbedaan makna ditunjukkan melalui konteks atau kata "untuk," bukan bentuk kata kerja. Ini membuat struktur ini sangat sulit bagi pelajar Indonesia.

### Tabel Kontras

| Bahasa Indonesia | Makna | Bentuk Inggris yang benar |
|---|---|---|
| Dia berhenti merokok (= tidak merokok lagi) | ceased the habit | stopped **smoking** |
| Dia berhenti untuk merokok (= istirahat sebentar) | paused to do it | stopped **to smoke** |
| Ingat mengunci pintu (= jangan lupa) | future task | remember **to lock** |
| Ingat pernah mengunci pintu (= memiliki kenangan) | past action recalled | remember **locking** |
| Dia bermaksud menelepon | intended to | meant **to call** |
| Itu artinya kerja keras | it entails | means **working hard** |

### Interferensi Khas

Karena bahasa Indonesia tidak membedakan bentuk ini, pelajar cenderung memilih satu bentuk (biasanya gerund) dan menggunakannya untuk semua konteks. Akibatnya, kalimat seperti "He forgot meeting her at the party" (artinya: dia tidak ingat pernah bertemu) dan "He forgot to meet her at the party" (artinya: dia lupa janji bertemu) dianggap sama saja.

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *I remember to meet her at the airport last year.* → ✅ *I remember **meeting** her at the airport last year.* *Why:* You recall a past action → gerund. "Remember to meet" means "don't forget a future task."

2. ❌ *She stopped to work on the project and now it's finished.* → ✅ *She **stopped working** on the project and now it's finished.* *Why:* She ceased the activity → gerund. "Stopped to work" would mean she paused in order to work.

3. ❌ *He forgot meeting to submit his assignment.* → ✅ *He **forgot to submit** his assignment.* *Why:* He failed to do a planned task → infinitive.

4. ❌ *Try to add more garlic — it might taste better.* → ✅ *Try **adding** more garlic — it might taste better.* *Why:* Experimenting with an option → gerund. "Try to add" implies difficulty in the act of adding.

5. ❌ *We regret informing you that your application was unsuccessful.* → ✅ *We **regret to inform** you that your application was unsuccessful.* *Why:* Formal announcement of bad news → infinitive.

6. ❌ *Getting the job means to work harder.* → ✅ *Getting the job **means working** harder.* *Why:* "Mean" in the sense of "involve/entail" takes a gerund.

7. ❌ *After solving the first problem, she went on solving the second one.* → ✅ *After solving the first problem, she **went on to solve** the second one.* *Why:* Moving on to a new, different task → infinitive.

8. ❌ *He tried opening the window but it was stuck.* → ✅ *He **tried to open** the window but it was stuck.* *Why:* An attempt that failed → infinitive. "Tried opening" suggests experimenting to see if opening it would help.

## TOEFL Practice

These verbs appear frequently in TOEFL reading and structure sections. The test often presents two sentences with the same verb but different forms and asks you to identify the correct meaning or spot the error. Focus on context clues:
- Past action being recalled → gerund with remember/forget
- Future task not to be forgotten → infinitive with remember
- Cessation of habit → gerund with stop
- Purpose of pausing → infinitive with stop

## Translation Drill

Translate these sentences into English using the correct form (gerund or infinitive).

1. *Dia lupa mengirim emailnya.* (He failed to send it.)
2. *Saya ingat pernah bertemu dengannya di konferensi itu.* (I recall the past meeting.)
3. *Coba tambahkan sedikit garam — mungkin rasanya lebih baik.* (Experiment.)
4. *Saya bermaksud menghubungimu kemarin.* (Intended, but didn't.)
5. *Setelah menyelesaikan S1-nya, dia melanjutkan untuk meraih gelar master.* (New activity.)

## Quick Summary

- **stop + -ing** = cease an activity; **stop + to-infinitive** = pause in order to do something else
- **remember + -ing** = recall a past action; **remember + to-infinitive** = not forget a future task
- **forget + -ing** = fail to recall a past action; **forget + to-infinitive** = fail to do a planned task
- **try + -ing** = experiment to see if it works; **try + to-infinitive** = attempt something (may fail)
- **regret + -ing** = feel sorry about a past action; **regret + to-infinitive** = sorry to announce (formal)
- **mean + -ing** = involve/entail; **mean + to-infinitive** = intend to do
- **go on + -ing** = continue the same activity; **go on + to-infinitive** = move on to something new
- Indonesian has no equivalent distinction — this must be learned through context and practice
