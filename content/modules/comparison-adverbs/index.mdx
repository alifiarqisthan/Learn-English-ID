## Explanation

Comparative and superlative adverbs describe **how** actions are performed and allow us to compare the manner, degree, or frequency of those actions.

### Forming Comparative Adverbs

| Adverb type | Comparative form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Short adverbs (same form as adjective: fast, hard, late) | add **-er** | fast → **faster**, hard → **harder** |
| Most adverbs ending in -ly | **more** + adverb | quickly → **more quickly** |
| Two-syllable adverbs (often) | **more** + adverb | often → **more often** |

> Always use **than** after the comparative adverb when naming what you are comparing to.

- She runs **faster than** her brother.
- He explained it **more clearly than** the teacher.
- The machine works **more efficiently than** before.

### Forming Superlative Adverbs

| Adverb type | Superlative form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Short adverbs | **the** + adverb + **-est** | fast → **the fastest**, hard → **the hardest** |
| Adverbs ending in -ly | **the most** + adverb | carefully → **the most carefully** |

- Of all the runners, she finished **the fastest**.
- He works **the hardest** in the office.
- She spoke **the most confidently** of all the candidates.

### Irregular Adverbs

| Base adverb | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| well | **better** | **the best** |
| badly | **worse** | **the worst** |
| far | **farther / further** | **the farthest / furthest** |
| little | **less** | **the least** |
| much | **more** | **the most** |

### Adjective vs Adverb — A Critical Distinction

| ❌ Adjective used incorrectly | ✅ Adverb used correctly |
|---|---|
| She runs more **quick** than he does. | She runs more **quickly** than he does. |
| He works more **careful** now. | He works more **carefully** now. |
| She sings more **beautiful** than anyone. | She sings more **beautifully** than anyone. |
| He did the test more **easy** than expected. | He did the test more **easily** than expected. |

> Remember: adverbs modify verbs. If you are describing **how** something is done, you need the adverb (-ly form), not the adjective.

### Structure Summary

> **Subject + verb + comparative adverb + than + second item**
> **Subject + verb + the + superlative adverb (+ of all / in the group)**

- She arrived **later than** the others.
- He speaks **the most fluently** of all the students.

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Perbedaan dengan Bahasa Indonesia

Dalam bahasa Indonesia, kata keterangan (adverbia) tidak berubah bentuk untuk membandingkan — kita tetap menggunakan "lebih...dari" atau "paling":
- *Dia berlari lebih cepat dariku.* → She runs **faster than** me.
- *Dia bekerja paling keras.* → She works **the hardest**.

Tidak ada perbedaan antara kata sifat dan kata keterangan dalam bentuk perbandingan bahasa Indonesia, sehingga pelajar sering menggunakan kata sifat alih-alih kata keterangan.

### Tabel Kontras: Kata Sifat vs Kata Keterangan

| Bahasa Indonesia | ❌ Kesalahan (kata sifat) | ✅ Benar (kata keterangan) |
|---|---|---|
| lebih cepat (cara berlari) | more fast | **faster / more quickly** |
| lebih hati-hati | more careful | **more carefully** |
| paling efisien (cara kerja) | the most efficient | **the most efficiently** |
| lebih keras (cara bekerja) | more hard | **harder** |

### Interferensi Khas

Karena bahasa Indonesia tidak membedakan secara jelas kata sifat dan kata keterangan dalam perbandingan, pelajar Indonesia sering menulis "more fast" (kata sifat) alih-alih "faster" atau "more quickly" (kata keterangan). Selalu tanyakan: apakah kata ini menerangkan kata benda (kata sifat) atau kata kerja (kata keterangan)?

## Common Mistakes

1. ❌ *She finished the exam more quick than anyone.* → ✅ *She finished the exam more **quickly** than anyone.* *Why:* "Quick" is an adjective; the adverb form is "quickly."

2. ❌ *He drives more careful than his father.* → ✅ *He drives more **carefully** than his father.* *Why:* "Careful" is an adjective; "carefully" is the adverb needed here.

3. ❌ *Of all the speakers, she spoke most confidently.* → ✅ *Of all the speakers, she spoke **the** most confidently.* *Why:* Superlative adverbs need "the."

4. ❌ *He works more hardly than his colleagues.* → ✅ *He works **harder** than his colleagues.* *Why:* "Hard" is one of the short adverbs that uses -er, not "more."

5. ❌ *She sings more good than her sister.* → ✅ *She sings **better** than her sister.* *Why:* "Well" is the adverb form of "good"; its comparative is "better."

6. ❌ *They performed more bad than expected.* → ✅ *They performed **worse** than expected.* *Why:* "Badly" → "worse" (irregular).

7. ❌ *He runs the most fast in the team.* → ✅ *He runs the **fastest** in the team.* *Why:* "Fast" is a short adverb — use the -est form.

8. ❌ *She explained more clear than the professor.* → ✅ *She explained more **clearly** than the professor.* *Why:* You need the -ly adverb, not the adjective.

## TOEFL Practice

TOEFL tests comparative and superlative adverbs in two main ways:
1. Choosing between the adjective and adverb form in a comparative sentence
2. Identifying double-form errors (more harder, the most fastest)

Focus on the question: does the word modify a verb or a noun?

## Quick Summary

- Short adverbs (fast, hard, late) → comparative: **-er**, superlative: **the -est**
- Most -ly adverbs → comparative: **more** + adverb, superlative: **the most** + adverb
- Irregular: well → better → the best; badly → worse → the worst
- Always use **than** after a comparative adverb when comparing two items
- Superlative adverbs always need **the**: "she works the most efficiently"
- Key error: using adjectives instead of adverbs ("more quick" → "more quickly")
- "Hard" and "fast" as adverbs use -er/-est, not "more/most"
- Indonesian "lebih + [adjective/adverb]" → English must distinguish adjective vs adverb form
