## Explanation

Cause and result connectors express why something happens (cause) and what follows from it (result/effect). The key challenge is knowing whether a connector needs a clause or a noun phrase after it, and how formal it is.

### Cause Connectors — Overview

| Connector | Type | Follows… | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| because | subordinating conjunction | subject + verb (clause) | neutral |
| since | subordinating conjunction | subject + verb (clause) | neutral/formal |
| as | subordinating conjunction | subject + verb (clause) | formal |
| because of | preposition | noun / noun phrase | neutral |
| due to | preposition | noun / noun phrase | formal |
| owing to | preposition | noun / noun phrase | formal |

**Clause vs. noun phrase — critical distinction:**

```
because + clause:   She was late because it rained.
because of + noun:  She was late because of the rain.

due to + noun:      The delay was due to heavy traffic.
❌ due to + clause: The delay was due to it rained.   ← WRONG

since + clause:     Since the budget was cut, we cancelled the event.
owing to + noun:    Owing to the budget cuts, we cancelled the event.
```

**"Since" vs "because":**
- Both can mean cause, but *since* implies the listener already knows the reason, so it is often more formal or used when the cause is established fact.
- *Since* also means time — avoid ambiguity. If the meaning is unclear, use *because*.

**"As" (cause):**
- Formal and academic. Usually begins the clause: *As the deadline had passed, the submission was rejected.*

### Result Connectors — Overview

| Connector | Register | Position | Punctuation |
|---|---|---|---|
| so | informal | between clauses | comma before (short clauses) |
| therefore | formal | start of sentence or mid-sentence | semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| consequently | formal | start of sentence | semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| as a result | formal | start of sentence | semicolon or new sentence; comma after |
| thus | formal/academic | start or mid-sentence | semicolon or new sentence; no comma needed mid-sentence |
| hence | very formal/academic | start or mid-sentence | semicolon or new sentence; comma after at start |

**Punctuation rules:**

```
Option A — New sentence:
  The experiment failed. As a result, the team started over.

Option B — Semicolon:
  The experiment failed; consequently, the team started over.

Option C — "so" (informal, comma before):
  The experiment failed, so the team started over.

❌ WRONG (comma splice with "therefore"):
  The experiment failed, therefore the team started over.
```

### Formality Scale

| Informal → Formal | Cause | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Informal | because | so |
| Neutral | because of | — |
| Semi-formal | since, as | as a result |
| Formal | due to, owing to | therefore, consequently |
| Very formal/academic | — | thus, hence |

### Key Distinctions

- **so** vs **therefore**: both show result, but *so* is too informal for academic essays. Use *therefore* or *consequently* in IELTS/TOEFL writing.
- **because** vs **due to**: *because* introduces a clause (S + V); *due to* introduces a noun phrase. Mixing them is a very common error.
- **thus** mid-sentence: *The sample size was small, thus limiting the study's validity.* (participial phrase follows — note no second subject).
- **hence**: almost exclusively academic. *The data is unreliable, hence the need for further research.*

## Bahasa Indonesia

### Padanan dalam Bahasa Indonesia

| Bahasa Inggris | Bahasa Indonesia | Catatan |
|---|---|---|
| because | karena | paling umum, netral |
| because of / due to | karena, dikarenakan | "dikarenakan" lebih formal |
| since (cause) | karena, mengingat | "mengingat" formal |
| so (result) | jadi, sehingga | "sehingga" formal/tulisan |
| therefore | oleh karena itu, oleh sebab itu | sangat formal |
| consequently | akibatnya, dengan demikian | |
| as a result | sebagai hasilnya, akibatnya | |
| thus | dengan demikian, sehingga | |
| hence | oleh karenanya | sangat formal, jarang digunakan |

### Interferensi Bahasa Indonesia

**Masalah 1 — "karena" diterjemahkan langsung menjadi "due to" + klausa:**

Dalam Bahasa Indonesia, "karena" diikuti klausa ATAU frasa secara bebas. Pelajar sering menerapkan pola yang sama untuk "due to":

```
❌ The accident happened due to the driver was not careful.
   (Bahasa Indonesia: Kecelakaan terjadi karena pengemudinya tidak hati-hati.)

✅ The accident happened because the driver was not careful.
✅ The accident happened due to the driver's carelessness.
```

**Masalah 2 — "sehingga" diterjemahkan sebagai "so" di awal kalimat:**

"Sehingga" bisa memulai klausa independen dalam Bahasa Indonesia, tetapi "so" membutuhkan struktur klausa yang terhubung dengan koma:

```
❌ The report was incomplete. So it was rejected.  (terlalu informal/terputus)
✅ The report was incomplete; therefore, it was rejected.
✅ The report was incomplete, so it was rejected.
```

**Masalah 3 — "oleh karena itu" → comma splice dengan "therefore":**

Karena "oleh karena itu" bebas di Bahasa Indonesia, pelajar lupa aturan titik koma:

```
❌ The data was wrong, therefore the results are invalid.
✅ The data was wrong. Therefore, the results are invalid.
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **"Due to" + clause**
   ❌ *The match was cancelled due to it rained heavily.*
   ✅ *The match was cancelled because it rained heavily.*
   ✅ *The match was cancelled due to heavy rain.*
   *Why:* "Due to" is a preposition — it must be followed by a noun or noun phrase, not a subject-verb clause.

2. **"Because of" + clause**
   ❌ *She was fired because of she arrived late every day.*
   ✅ *She was fired because she arrived late every day.*
   ✅ *She was fired because of her persistent lateness.*
   *Why:* "Because of" is also a preposition — followed by noun only, not a clause.

3. **Comma splice with "therefore"**
   ❌ *The bridge was unsafe, therefore it was closed.*
   ✅ *The bridge was unsafe. Therefore, it was closed.*
   ✅ *The bridge was unsafe; therefore, it was closed.*
   *Why:* "Therefore" is a conjunctive adverb, not a conjunction — it cannot join two clauses with only a comma.

4. **Using "so" in formal writing**
   ❌ (In an academic essay) *The sample was biased, so the findings are questionable.*
   ✅ *The sample was biased; consequently, the findings are questionable.*
   *Why:* "So" is informal and weakens academic register.

5. **Missing comma after result connector**
   ❌ *As a result the experiment was repeated.*
   ✅ *As a result, the experiment was repeated.*
   *Why:* Multi-word connectors at the start of a sentence always take a comma after them.

6. **Confusing "since" (time) with "since" (cause)**
   ❌ *Since he moved to Jakarta, it has become more expensive.* (ambiguous — time or cause?)
   ✅ *Because he moved to Jakarta, costs increased.* (cause made clear)
   *Why:* When ambiguity exists, replace causal "since" with "because."

7. **"Owing to the fact that" over-formality bloat**
   ❌ *Owing to the fact that the system crashed, all data was lost.*
   ✅ *Because the system crashed, all data was lost.*
   *Why:* "Owing to the fact that" is verbose — use "because" instead.

8. **"Hence" in informal speech**
   ❌ *I was tired, hence I left the party early.*
   ✅ *I was tired, so I left the party early.*
   *Why:* "Hence" is academic register — inappropriate in casual or spoken contexts.

## TOEFL Practice

In TOEFL Integrated and Independent Writing tasks, cause-result connectors are frequently tested in two ways:

1. **Reading/lecture summaries**: identify the cause and result the author presents, then use precise connectors in your response.
2. **Argument development**: use *therefore* and *consequently* to build a logical chain of reasoning from evidence to conclusion. Avoid *so* in formal responses.

Key TOEFL writing tip: a chain of cause-effect reasoning (*because → therefore → consequently*) shows coherence and logical development — both scored criteria.

## Quick Summary

- **Cause connectors that need a clause**: *because*, *since*, *as* — followed by subject + verb.
- **Cause connectors that need a noun phrase**: *because of*, *due to*, *owing to* — followed by noun/gerund only.
- **Result connectors (formal)**: *therefore*, *consequently*, *as a result*, *thus*, *hence* — all need a semicolon or new sentence before them, and a comma after.
- **"So"** is a result conjunction (informal) — joins clauses with just a comma; avoid in academic writing.
- **Comma splice** with *therefore/consequently* is the most common error at this level.
- **"Due to" + clause** ("due to it rained") is always wrong — use *because* for clauses.
- **Formality**: choose *due to/therefore/consequently* for essays; choose *because/so* for everyday speech.
- **"Since"** can mean cause or time — replace with *because* if ambiguous.
